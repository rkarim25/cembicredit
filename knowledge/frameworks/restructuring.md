# Sovereign and corporate restructuring framework

What determines recovery, timing and process when a credit cannot pay. Precedents are the evidence; every restructuring is argued from the last three.

## 1. Sovereign: the process

1. Trigger: missed payment, announced standstill, or IMF programme request with a DSA that requires relief. Sometimes a pre-emptive exchange (Ukraine 2015, Ecuador 2020).
2. Perimeter: which debt is in (external bonds, loans, domestic, guaranteed, SOE, central bank) and which is excluded (multilaterals, short-term trade). The perimeter decides burden-sharing.
3. Envelope: the IMF DSA sets the relief needed to reach targets (debt to GDP, GFN to GDP, debt service to revenue, PV of external debt to exports for LICs). The envelope is the ceiling on what bondholders can hope for.
4. Official creditors first: Paris Club or the G20 Common Framework (Chad, Zambia, Ghana, Ethiopia) agree terms; China's participation is the usual delay.
5. Comparability of treatment: bondholders are asked for terms at least as favourable to the debtor in PV terms, using the official creditors' discount rate assumptions. Dispute over the discount rate (official 5 percent versus market exit yields) is where value is argued.
6. Bondholder committee, restricted negotiations, term sheet, consent solicitation using CACs, exchange.
7. Exit: new bonds priced at an exit yield; recovery is the PV of new instruments at that yield, not the face haircut.

## 2. Legal terms that move recovery

- CACs: single-limb aggregated (ICMA 2014 model, most post-2015 Eurobonds) allow one vote across series at 75 percent; two-limb needs 66⅔ aggregate plus 50 percent per series; series-by-series at 75 percent lets holdouts block one bond. Check each bond's prospectus.
- Governing law (English or New York), trustee versus fiscal agent (trustee structures limit individual enforcement), pari passu and sharing clauses, cross-default and cross-acceleration thresholds, negative pledge.
- Sovereign immunity waivers and attachable assets. Holdout litigation (Argentina NML) is slow; it is leverage, not a strategy, for most funds.
- State-contingent instruments: GDP warrants (Argentina, Ukraine, Greece), value-recovery instruments tied to oil or fiscal revenue (Zambia, Suriname, Ukraine 2024). Price them on the contingency, not the headline.

## 3. Recovery arithmetic

Recovery value = Σ PV(new cash flows) at exit yield + any upfront cash + warrant value. Build the new-instrument cash flows from the term sheet: face haircut, coupon step-ups, maturity extension, grace period, capitalised interest (PDI treatment). Sensitivity table on exit yield (±200bp) and on comparability discount rate. Compare with current price to size expected return and the asymmetry.

## 4. Sovereign precedents to argue from

| Case | Year | Key features to cite |
|---|---|---|
| Ukraine | 2015, 2024 | GDP warrants (2015); 2024 wartime deal with contingent step-ups, done under the IMF programme |
| Ghana | 2023–24 | Domestic exchange first, then Eurobonds; Common Framework; PV relief with disco-par menu |
| Zambia | 2020–24 | Common Framework pioneer; China delays; value-recovery instrument tied to IMF DSA upgrade |
| Sri Lanka | 2022–24 | Macro-linked bonds; domestic treatment limited to central bank and pension funds |
| Ethiopia | 2023– | Single Eurobond; Common Framework; dispute over whether a liquidity or solvency case |
| Suriname | 2023 | Oil-linked value recovery instrument |
| Ecuador | 2020 | Fast pre-emptive exchange under single-limb CACs |
| Argentina | 2001–05, 2020 | Holdouts, pari passu litigation, the 2020 use of CAC re-designation (the Pac-Man issue) |
| Lebanon | 2020– | No restructuring for years; what happens without an IMF anchor |
| Mozambique | 2016–19 | Hidden debt, tuna bonds, legality disputes |

## 5. Corporate and quasi-sovereign EM restructuring

- Creditor hierarchy by entity and jurisdiction: operating company versus holdco, secured bank lenders versus Eurobond holders, local pre-export facilities and trade banks that keep the business alive (the "creditor violence" analysis used on Zorlu and Aragvi).
- Local insolvency regimes: Brazil recuperação judicial, Turkey konkordato, UK schemes and restructuring plans used by Russian, Ukrainian and Gulf issuers, Chapter 11 and Chapter 15 for US-law bonds. The forum decides who controls the process.
- Sovereign support for quasi-sovereigns is a willingness question: ownership, strategic importance, precedent (Pemex, Eskom, Petrobras, Turkish state banks). Support is never a guarantee unless it is written.
- Valuation: sum-of-the-parts enterprise value, liquidation floor, waterfall by seniority, then recovery per instrument under base, upside and hostile scenarios. The repo's calculators (`braskem_calculator.html`, `zoren_calculator.html`, `aragvi_calculator.html`) are the house implementation.

## 6. Questions to answer before taking a distressed position

1. Who controls the process (IMF, official creditors, a court, the sovereign alone)?
2. What is the envelope and is the perimeter settled?
3. What do the documents allow (CAC type, thresholds, trustee)?
4. What is the price relative to a sober recovery estimate at a realistic exit yield?
5. What is the timeline and the carry while waiting (PDI accrual, cash coupons)?
6. Who else holds it and what do they want (hedge funds, real money, local banks)?
