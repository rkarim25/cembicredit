# EM macro regimes framework

The global drivers that move EM debt as an asset class, and how to classify the current regime before making any single-country call.

## 1. The five global drivers

1. US rates and the Fed: level and direction of real rates, the shape of the curve, and the pace of change. EM suffers most from fast tightening in real terms (2013, 2018, 2022), less from high but stable rates.
2. The dollar: broad USD strength is the single most reliable headwind for EM hard-currency and local returns. Watch the DXY trend, the US twin deficits, and relative growth.
3. Commodities: oil for the Gulf, Nigeria, Angola, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Colombia, Ecuador; copper for Zambia, Chile, Peru; agriculture for Brazil, Argentina, Ukraine, Côte d'Ivoire; gas for Egypt, Mozambique. Classify each sovereign by its terms-of-trade exposure.
4. China: growth, credit impulse, commodity demand, and its lending behaviour as a creditor (Common Framework delays, swap lines, rollover of policy-bank loans).
5. Global risk appetite: credit spreads in US HY and Xover, equity volatility, EM fund flows (EPFR). The repo's CDX desk tracks the first two.

## 2. Regime classification

Combine the UST curve regime (bear or bull, steepening or flattening, from the UST desk) with the dollar trend and the commodity trend:

| Regime | Rates | USD | Commodities | EM implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goldilocks | Bull steepening, cuts priced | Weak | Firm | Add duration and FX; HY spreads compress |
| Taper / tightening shock | Bear flattening | Strong | Mixed | Cut local FX first, then HY; stay in IG hard currency |
| Growth scare | Bull flattening | Strong (haven) | Weak | Hard-currency IG outperforms; commodity exporters lag |
| Reflation | Bear steepening | Weak | Strong | Commodity exporters and local rates outperform |
| Stagflation | Bear steepening with high inflation | Strong | Strong | Worst for importers with weak reserves (Egypt, Pakistan, Kenya type) |

State the regime at the top of every macro note, with the evidence, and the single most likely transition.

## 3. Transmission to individual credits

For each sovereign in the universe, the page should say: oil sensitivity (sign and size), USD funding dependence, share of non-resident holdings in local debt, and the official-sector anchor. The regime plus these four fields gives the first-order direction before any country-specific work.

## 4. EM-specific cycles

- Issuance windows: January and September are the heavy months; a closed primary market for HY sovereigns is itself a stress signal.
- Elections cluster: list the year's elections in `themes/calendar.md`.
- IMF calendar: Spring and Annual Meetings (April, October) are when programmes and restructurings get announced.
- Index rebalances: month-end, with inclusion and exclusion events announced in advance by JP Morgan.

## 5. Macro trade expression

Trade expression conventions (recorded on the topic's report, not in a tracker): entry, target, stop, DV01 or notional, and the regime assumption that invalidates the trade. Macro views without an invalidation are opinions, not trades.
