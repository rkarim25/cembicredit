# EM crisis history: what each episode teaches

A reference of the episodes a seasoned EM investor argues from, with the mechanism and the lesson. Dates are approximate to the year; cite primary accounts when a date matters.

| Episode | Years | Mechanism | Lesson still used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Latin American debt crisis | 1982–89 | Floating-rate syndicated loans into a Volcker rate shock; commodity collapse | Brady bonds created the asset class; official-sector-led workouts take years |
| Mexico Tequila | 1994–95 | Peg plus short-dated dollar-linked debt (Tesobonos) and a political shock | Short-term dollar debt rollover risk; US and IMF backstop size matters |
| Asian crisis | 1997–98 | Pegged currencies, private-sector FX borrowing, banking fragility | Private external debt is sovereign risk in a crisis; reserves must be judged against short-term debt |
| Russia | 1998 | Fiscal weakness, GKO pyramid, oil at 10 dollars, devaluation and default together | Local debt can default; contagion through leveraged holders (LTCM) |
| Brazil, Turkey, Argentina | 1999–2002 | Pegs break (Brazil 1999, Turkey 2001), Argentina's convertibility ends in default | Hard pegs fail fiscally before they fail externally; domestic deposit freezes destroy political capital |
| Global financial crisis | 2008–09 | Dollar funding shock; EM with reserves recovered fastest | Reserve accumulation worked; Fed swap lines for a few |
| Taper tantrum | 2013 | Fed signal; "Fragile Five" current-account deficits | Real-rate shocks hit deficit countries with foreign-held local debt |
| Commodity bust | 2014–16 | Oil halves; Nigeria, Angola, Azerbaijan, Russia, Venezuela | Pegs plus oil dependence equal parallel markets and arrears |
| Turkey and Argentina | 2018 | Policy credibility collapse, dollar strength, external financing needs | Orthodoxy restored late is expensive; Argentina's record IMF programme failed |
| Covid shock | 2020 | Global sudden stop; DSSI and G20 Common Framework born | Official sector improvised; China became the pivotal creditor |
| Russia sanctions | 2022 | Index exclusion, frozen reserves, local-market closure to non-residents | Sanctions risk is a credit factor; reserves held abroad can be immobilised |
| Frontier default wave | 2020–24 | Zambia, Sri Lanka, Ghana, Ethiopia; Egypt and Pakistan near-misses | Interest to revenue above 30 percent and lost market access are the common tells; Common Framework slow but delivered |
| Gulf support episodes | 2022–24 | Egypt, Pakistan, Turkey (swap lines, deposits, Ras El Hekma) | Anchors change outcomes; price the anchor's conditionality |

## Recurring patterns

1. Sudden stops hit countries with external financing needs above reserves, whatever the debt ratio.
2. Pegs and managed rates create parallel markets, then arrears, then devaluation and inflation. The sequence is reliable.
3. Local debt held by foreigners turns a rates market into a credit market in a shock.
4. The official sector decides the timeline; the market decides the price.
5. Political transitions matter less than policy continuity; count finance ministers, not presidents.
