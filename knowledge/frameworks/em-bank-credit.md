# EM bank credit framework

For the 19 banks in the corporate universe and any new one. Banks are analysed differently from corporates: the sovereign link is tighter, the balance sheet is the business, and the instrument's rank in the capital stack decides most of the outcome.

## 1. Sovereign and system first

- Sovereign ceiling and the doom loop: government securities as a share of assets, central bank funding, deposit dollarisation, and whether the sovereign has ever imposed deposit freezes or conversions (Argentina 2001, Lebanon 2019, Egypt and Nigeria FX rationing). A bank cannot be stronger than its sovereign's ability to supply dollars.
- System structure: concentration, state ownership, regulator quality and independence, resolution regime (bail-in powers, deposit insurance, precedent for senior creditor losses).
- Macro drivers of asset quality: policy rate path, FX, real growth, commodity exposure of the loan book.

## 2. Franchise and funding

- Deposit franchise: share of retail and SME deposits, cost of funds versus system, concentration of the largest depositors, FX deposit share and FX liquidity coverage.
- Wholesale reliance: Eurobonds, syndications, central bank facilities, repo. Maturity ladder of wholesale funding against liquid assets.
- Liquidity: LCR and NSFR where reported, liquid assets to short-term funding, unencumbered FX assets.

## 3. Asset quality

- NPL ratio, stage 2 and stage 3 under IFRS 9, coverage ratio, restructured loans, write-off policy. Regulatory forbearance distorts all of these; read the notes.
- Concentration: top-20 exposures to equity, related-party lending, sector and FX-denominated lending to unhedged borrowers.
- Cost of risk versus pre-provision profit: the cushion that absorbs a cycle.

## 4. Capital and earnings

- CET1, Tier 1, total capital against regulatory minimum plus buffers; leverage ratio. Capital in nominal and after an FX shock (RWA inflation on FX assets).
- Pre-provision operating profit to average assets; NIM; cost to income; return on equity relative to inflation and the cost of equity.
- Dividends and capital distribution policy under stress.

## 5. The instrument

- Rank: senior preferred, senior non-preferred or holdco senior, Tier 2 (dated subordinated), AT1 (perpetual, discretionary coupons, write-down or conversion triggers, call risk). For EM banks the Tier 2 and AT1 documents vary; read the trigger, the point of non-viability language and the governing law.
- Call economics for AT1 and Tier 2: reset spread, the issuer's refinancing cost, regulatory capital treatment after call date.
- Transfer and convertibility: even a sound bank may be unable to pay offshore bonds if the sovereign restricts FX (the Nigerian and Egyptian precedents).
- Resolution precedent in the jurisdiction: how senior and subordinated holders fared in the last bank failure.

## 6. Relative value

- Spread to the sovereign and to the bank's own senior curve; AT1 to Tier 2 to senior differentials versus DM and EM peers; yield to call versus yield to perpetuity.
- Peers by system and rank; the screener and peer comparison page support bank metrics (assets, loans, deposits, net interest income, total income).

## 7. Questions for management

Deposit trends and FX deposit behaviour; the largest exposures and their status; stage 2 migration; capital plan and buffer management after an FX move; AT1 and Tier 2 call intentions; access to central bank FX liquidity; related-party exposure.

## 8. Output standard

A bank note states: sovereign and system context with the transfer-risk judgement, franchise and funding, asset quality with coverage, capital with an FX-shock sensitivity, the instrument's rank and documentation features, relative value, management questions, and a view with confidence. Sources are the bank's audited accounts and Pillar 3 disclosures, central bank statistics, and Cognitive Credit where covered.
