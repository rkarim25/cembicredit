## View
- The Common Framework (CF) cohort is largely finished. Ethiopia's $1bn Eurobond exchange is the last item of size and is still awaiting settlement (IMF GSDR, 15-Apr-26; Capital Ethiopia, 22-Aug-26, High/Medium-High).
- Ethiopia sets the bondholder template: a 12% nominal reduction ($880m 6.15% 2029), missed coupons paid in cash, a 0.5% consent fee and a warrant. Official creditors forced a rework before validating comparability of treatment (CoT) (Medium-High).
- Timing is the main driver. Precedent runs 16 to 24 months from standstill to bond closure (GSDR 6th report, High). The 7th report (14-Oct-26) has not been read.
- The next wave sits outside the CF. Senegal has a staff-level agreement but still needs IMF Board approval, corrective measures and financing assurances. The government says "reprofiling", and Eurobond treatment is unconfirmed (Medium).
- Mozambique: Fitch CC treats any bond operation under an IMF programme as a distressed debt exchange (DDE). The Finance Ministry now says a staff-level agreement and Board approval are possible by end-2026, earlier than Fitch's "early 2027" (Medium).
- Biggest risk: a "reprofiling" of Senegal's Eurobonds that is economically a DDE. That would set a template for others.
- Confidence: High on the closed CF cohort and timelines. Medium on Senegal, Mozambique and Ethiopia, because no IMF or issuer primary documents were read. Low on Gabon, Cameroon and Tunisia, which were not researched.

## What changed

- Senegal: confirmed that the staff-level agreement (about $2.2bn, three years) is not final. It still needs corrective measures, financing assurances and Board approval. Reported Eurobond maturities are about $1.1bn over 2026-28, one-third in 2026.

- Open items were merged. The Gabon, Cameroon and Tunisia gap remains.

## Analysis
### Closed CF cohort and timelines
- The GSDR 6th report (15-Apr-26) describes Ethiopia, Ghana, Zambia, Sri Lanka and Suriname as largely completed (IMF GSDR, 15-Apr-26, High).
- Timeline ranges:
  - Standstill to programme approval: 2.1 to 10.5 months.
  - Approval to first review: 6.0 to 17.7 months.
  - Bond exchange closure: 16.0 to 24.0 months (IMF GSDR, 15-Apr-26, High).
- The sequence is IMF programme and DSA, then the official creditor committee (OCC), then the bondholder deal under CoT, then bilateral implementation. The DSA targets size the bondholder ask. IMF lending into arrears lets a programme proceed during default (repo knowledge base, tier 2, High).
- The CF covers DSSI-eligible countries only. Middle-income cases used ad hoc committees. On that basis Senegal is unlikely to enter the CF (Medium).
- A Reuters piece of 24-Jun-26 (via chinaglobalsouth.com) reports the Paris Club wanting the CF made faster and more efficient, with strict comparability (Medium; paywalled original not read).

### Ethiopia
- Terms: the $1bn Eurobond is swapped for an $880m 6.15% bond due 15-Jul-29. $99.37m of three missed coupons is paid in full. A consent fee of 0.5% of $1bn is paid on settlement. A tradable New Money Warrant covers future issuance of up to $1bn (Capital Ethiopia, Jun-Aug-26; EastAfrican, Medium-High).
- The first bond deal was rejected by official creditors. The agreement in principle came on 28-Jun-26. Ethiopia's statement of 21-Aug-26 says the OCC accepted that the proposal complies with comparability. The OCC reported net relief as "relatively low" (Capital Ethiopia, 22-Aug-26, Medium-High).
- The IMF welcomed the deal (The EastAfrican, Zawya, Medium). Settlement is not confirmed.

### Senegal
- Staff-level agreement on a 36-month ECF of about $2.2bn, announced 01-Sep-26. It still needs corrective measures, financing assurances and Board approval (Engineering News, 25-Sep-26; dabafinance.com; capmad.com, Medium).
- The prime minister said Senegal would reprofile (extend maturities and renegotiate rates) rather than restructure. Reported Eurobond repayments are about $1.1bn over 2026-28 (Engineering News, 25-Sep-26; africa.com; serrarigroup.com, Medium).
- Moody's: Caa2, negative outlook, 28-Aug-26, per a position citing a secondary source. Interest was reported at about 24% of revenue in 2026, and hidden debt of $11-13bn (furtherafrica.com, 01-Sep-26, Medium-Low; the sources conflict on programme status).
- The 13-Sep-26 Eurobond coupon was reportedly paid (capmad.com, 04-Sep-26, Medium).
- If Eurobond maturities are extended or coupons cut, this is a distressed exchange regardless of label. The perimeter is not established.

### Mozambique
- Fitch: CC. It expects any IMF programme to need a liability management operation on the sole $900m 9% 2031 Eurobond, likely a DDE. It expects a programme agreement by early 2027 (clubofmozambique.com; businessday.ng, 2026, Medium).
- Finance Ministry: a new IMF programme, with staff-level agreement and Board approval, by end-2026 (allAfrica, 11-Sep-26, Medium).
- President Chapo: creditor talks follow the IMF deal (360mozambique.com). EcoFin Agency carries a Fitch view that restructuring may precede an IMF deal (Medium). The two tier-3 views conflict, so no winner is picked.

### Malawi and preferred creditors
- The restructuring launched in mid-2022 is stalled. The ECF lapsed in May-25. About $1bn is in scope: $887m commercial and $99m bilateral. Regional lenders such as Afreximbank and TDB dominate the commercial part (mwnation.com, Credendo, Medium; dates not captured).
- Preferred-creditor claims by regional lenders shrink the bondholder pool (repo knowledge base on Ghana and Zambia, Medium).

### Not researched
- Gabon, Cameroon, Tunisia, Bolivia, Kenya, Egypt and Pakistan were not searched, and the Ghana and Zambia haircuts were not sourced. Absence from this note is not absence of events.

## Where the models disagreed
No material dispute; the one position given and the previous document agree on the facts. One tension is left open: Mozambique's President says creditor talks follow the IMF deal, while a Fitch analyst says restructuring may come first. Both are tier 3, so no winner is picked (open items 4 and 6). The position's "10-20% typical nominal reduction" generalised from one case was not adopted, since the Ghana, Zambia and Sri Lanka haircuts were not sourced.
