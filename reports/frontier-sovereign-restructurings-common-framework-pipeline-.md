## View
- The first-wave Common Framework (CF) cohort (Zambia, Ghana, Ethiopia) is essentially closed. The live pipeline has moved to Senegal, the first large, market-accessed, non-low-income case (Medium).
- Senegal launched a debt treatment plan (PTDS) on 04-Sep-26 under a staff-level IMF agreement (36 months, about $2.2bn). It reportedly signalled intent to use the CF in "enhanced" form (Medium).
- Eurobond treatment is unconfirmed, and the labels conflict. The September "reprofiling, not restructuring" line sits against reports of restructuring nearly $5bn of external debt. A maturity extension or coupon cut is a distressed exchange whatever it is called.
- Senegal's Board date is unpredictable. An IMF team reportedly arrived in Dakar on 22-Oct-26 with DSA work unfinished (Medium-Low).
- Ethiopia is closing out. The bondholder leg is agreed (an $880m 6.15% 2029 bond plus a warrant), and the OCC accepted CoT on 21-Aug-26. Settlement and the other agreements are unconfirmed.
- Mozambique is pre-restructuring. A second IMF mission is due in Nov-26, and Fitch CC expects any bond operation to be a distressed debt exchange (DDE). A programme before year-end looks tight.
- Precedent points to 16 to 24 months from standstill to bond closure, and to extension and coupon cuts over face haircuts (GSDR, High; the latter Medium).
- Biggest risk: Senegal's DSA requires a nominal haircut on the Eurobonds, which would reprice the Africa frontier curve.
- Confidence: High on the closed cohort and timelines, Medium on Senegal, Ethiopia and Mozambique, Low on Gabon, Cameroon and Tunisia.

## What changed

- Added: the IMF team in Dakar from 22-Oct-26, the coupon paid on 13-Sep-26, CFA-market debt kept outside the perimeter, Moody's Caa2 negative (Aug-26), and a reported S&P CC (Low).

- Added Gabon (Moody's outlook negative on 26-Jun-26, a $920m Eurobond in Aug-26) and the Maldives as tail names.

## Analysis
### CF cohort and timelines
- The GSDR 6th report (15-Apr-26) describes Ethiopia, Ghana, Zambia, Sri Lanka and Suriname as largely completed (IMF GSDR, 15-Apr-26, High).
- Standstill to programme approval ranged 2.1 to 10.5 months, approval to first review 6.0 to 17.7 months, and bond exchange closure 16.0 to 24.0 months (IMF GSDR, 15-Apr-26, High).
- The GSDR 7th report and a technical compendium were issued in Bangkok on 14-Oct-26. The press release names no countries and no rule changes (IMF PR 26/337, 14-Oct-26, Medium; report body unread).
- Sequence: IMF programme and DSA, then OCC, then bondholder deal under CoT, then bilateral implementation. IMF lending into arrears lets a programme proceed during default (repo knowledge base, High).
- The CF covers DSSI-eligible countries only, and middle-income cases used ad hoc committees (repo knowledge base, High). Senegal's stated intent to use an enhanced CF is therefore unusual and unverified.

### Senegal
- Staff-level agreement on a 36-month ECF of about $2.2bn, announced 01-Sep-26 (Africanews, 01-Sep-26, Medium). Board approval, corrective measures and financing assurances were still outstanding as of 25-Sep-26 (Engineering News, 25-Sep-26, Medium).
- PTDS launched 04-Sep-26 per the Finance Ministry, seen only via search summaries (finances.gouv.sn, Sep-26, Medium). Senegal reportedly informed official partners of its intent to use the CF in enhanced form and to restructure nearly $5bn of external debt (capmad.com, Sep-26; Ecofin, 01-Sep-26, Medium-Low; the capmad page could not be opened).
- The prime minister earlier said Senegal would reprofile rather than restructure, and Eurobond repayments are about $1.1bn over 2026-28 (Engineering News, 25-Sep-26, Medium). The two framings are both tier 3 and cannot be reconciled from the evidence. Open items 3 and 4.
- The 13-Sep-26 Eurobond coupon was paid, and CFA-market debt is outside the perimeter (position, Medium-Low).
- Ratings: Moody's Caa2, negative outlook, Aug-26 (dabafinance.com, Aug-26, Medium). S&P CC is reported only via a headline, and an older S&P page showed a different level (le360.ma, Sep-26, Low).
- A reported IMF team in Dakar from 22-Oct-26, with DSA work unfinished, is a search-summary claim (Low-Medium).

### Ethiopia
- Terms: the $1bn Eurobond is swapped for an $880m 6.15% bond due 15-Jul-29, with $99.37m of missed coupons paid in full, a 0.5% consent fee and a tradable New Money Warrant for up to $1bn (Capital Ethiopia and The EastAfrican, Jun-Aug-26, Medium-High).
- The OCC accepted CoT per Ethiopia's 21-Aug-26 statement and described net relief as "relatively low" (Capital Ethiopia, 22-Aug-26, Medium-High).
- Five OCC bilateral agreements were concluded by May-26, with an Oct-26 deadline for the rest (Capital Ethiopia, 06-May-26, Medium). France signed in Feb-26, the first bilateral under the CF (Medium).
- Settlement is not confirmed.

### Mozambique
- Fitch CC. The sole Eurobond is $900m 9% due Sep-2031. Fitch expects an IMF programme by early 2027 and any bond operation to be a DDE (clubofmozambique.com and businessday.ng, 2026, Medium).
- A technical mission ended 21-Sep-26 and a second is scheduled for Nov-26 (allAfrica, 21-Sep-26, Medium). The Finance Ministry targets staff-level agreement and Board approval by end-2026 (allAfrica, 11-Sep-26, Medium).
- The President says creditor talks follow the IMF deal, while an EcoFin-carried Fitch view says restructuring may come first. Both are tier 3 and conflict, so no winner is picked.
- A $726.5m figure in search results is treated as stale (2016-19 precedent).

### Tail names
- Gabon: Fitch CCC- (22-May-26) and Moody's Caa2 with outlook cut to negative (26-Jun-26) (financialafrik.com, 25-Jun-26, Medium). It issued a $920m 7-year Eurobond in Aug-26 (dabafinance.com, Medium). It is not in a formal process.
- Maldives: Fitch upgraded from CC to CCC-, with $535m due in H2-26 (adhadhu.com, 2026, Low-Medium).
- Malawi: a restructuring launched in 2022 is stalled, with about $1bn in scope, mostly regional commercial lenders (mwnation.com and Credendo, dates not captured, Medium-Low). Nothing new was found this run.
- Regional lenders' preferred-creditor claims shrink the pool for bondholders (repo knowledge base, Medium).
- Not researched: Cameroon, Tunisia, Bolivia, Kenya, Egypt, Pakistan. The Ghana, Zambia and Sri Lanka haircuts were not sourced.

Both are tier 3, and neither establishes whether the Eurobonds are inside the perimeter. Resolution: open item, no winner, per the rule against resolving on tier 3 conflicts.
