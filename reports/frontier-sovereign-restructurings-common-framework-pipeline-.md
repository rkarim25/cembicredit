## View
- The CF is a slow but repeatable template: IMF programme and debt sustainability analysis (DSA), then an official creditor committee (OCC) memorandum of understanding (MoU), then a bondholder deal under comparability of treatment (CoT), then bilateral implementation. Timelines have run one to three years (repo knowledge base, tier 2, Medium).
- The DSA targets set the restructuring envelope. Lending-into-arrears lets an IMF programme anchor talks while bonds are in default (repo, tier 2, High).
- The main drivers are China's behaviour, the size of the multilateral and Afreximbank preferred-creditor share, and CAC aggregation and governing law. China prefers maturity extension and rate cuts over face haircuts and is slow in committee. A larger preferred share leaves a smaller pool for bondholders.
- Middle-income cases (Sri Lanka, Suriname) ran through ad hoc committees outside the CF. Future Eurobond-heavy middle-income restructurings are more likely to follow that route than the CF. This is an inference (Low).
- Whether Gabon, Cameroon, Mozambique and Tunisia enter the CF is not established and is not a supportable claim. Treat them as stress-watch until sourced.
- The biggest risk is a new case, such as an undisclosed-debt revelation or a Eurobond missed payment, that is handled outside the template, or a CF case that stalls on China or multilateral burden-sharing.
- Confidence: Medium on the structural framework. Low on the current pipeline and case status, because no independent research was done this run.

## Analysis
### Process the CF follows
- Sequence: IMF programme and DSA, OCC co-chaired by the Paris Club and a non-Paris Club creditor (usually China), MoU, bondholder agreement under CoT, then bilateral implementation (knowledge/themes/official-sector-and-common-framework.md, undated, tier 2, High on sequence, Medium on timing).
- Cases listed in the repo: Chad (2022), Zambia (2023 MoU), Ghana (2024 MoU), Ethiopia (2024–25). The Global Sovereign Debt Roundtable has tried to shorten timelines (same source, Medium).
- Eligibility is limited to DSSI-eligible (low-income) countries. Middle-income precedents (Sri Lanka, Suriname) used ad hoc committees with similar mechanics (same source, tier 2, Medium).

### What sizes the bondholder ask
- The DSA's targets are the restructuring envelope. Bondholders are asked to close the gap after official, multilateral and domestic creditors are treated (repo, tier 2, High).
- The IMF can lend into arrears to private creditors (LIA) if negotiations are in good faith, and to official creditors under LIOA conditions. This is why a programme can proceed while bonds are in default (repo, tier 2, High).
- Exceptional access above 435 percent of quota requires debt sustainable with high probability (repo, tier 2, High).

### Delay drivers
- China: Exim, CDB and bank lending, plus PBoC swap lines that inflate gross reserves but are not freely usable. It prefers extension and interest reduction to face haircuts, insists multilaterals share the burden and participates slowly. Collateralised and resource-backed loans complicate the perimeter (repo, tier 2, Medium because it is structural, not case-dated).
- Multilateral development banks: preferred-creditor status means their debt is excluded from restructurings. The Afreximbank preferred-creditor claim was contested in the Ghana and Zambia cases (repo, tier 2, Medium).
- Gulf deposits are rollover-dependent liabilities, not reserves, unless the maturity is long (repo, tier 2, Medium).

### Pipeline
- The repo has run folders dated 08-Oct-26 for Gabon, Cameroon, Ethiopia, Mozambique, Zambia and Tunisia, and 09-Oct-26 for this topic. The existence of a run folder says nothing about CF eligibility, default status or IMF programme status.
- Zambia and Ethiopia are CF cases. Their current stage is unverified (Low). The other four have no sourced evidence of a CF request or a missed payment (Low).
- Hidden or misreported debt is a plausible route for a name to enter the pipeline unexpectedly. The 2025 Senegal debt revision is cited from memory and is not re-sourced (Low).

### Unverified in this run
- No 90-day search was run: IMF country reports, Paris Club, G20 and OCC statements, issuer RNS filings, rating actions and press were not checked. Absence from this note is not absence of events.
- No market prices, spreads or recovery values were available. Nothing here is a pricing view.
- No sovereign data fills (reserves, debt stock, DSA risk rating) were pulled for the four stress-watch names.
