## View
- Neutral to modestly constructive, unchanged. Dollar ability to pay is strong in the near term, but fiscal capacity is weak, so this stays a single-B credit (Medium confidence).
- External: gross reserves are $55.07B (09-Oct-26, after a CBN downward revision from about $58B), up about 30% on a year earlier. The current account surplus was $15.61B over the last four quarters. The next Eurobond bullet is the $1.50B due 28-Nov-27.
- Ratings: S&P B/Stable (15-May-26), Fitch B/Stable (10-Apr-26), Moody's B3/Positive (29-Aug-26). Press reports of the actions agree with the repo dates. The next move is more likely up than down, and Moody's is the likeliest to move.
- Fiscal is the binding constraint. Debt is about 32% of GDP, but interest takes over 55% of retained revenue, against the framework's 30% threshold where defaults cluster.
- A 23% policy rate against 15.39% CPI supports the naira and carry, but keeps domestic financing costs high.
- Biggest risk: a Brent or output shock that hits FX inflows and revenue together, or an early CBN pivot.
- Not covered: market levels, net reserves, IMF status and 2027 financing plan. Confidence: Medium on fundamentals; Low on value.

## Analysis

### Ratings and reform trajectory
- Moody's affirmed B3 and moved the outlook to positive on 29-Aug-26, citing stronger reserves, better-than-expected growth and a current account surplus that holds under lower crude. It still flags limited revenue-generating capacity and weak debt affordability (Nairametrics, 29-Aug-26, https://nairametrics.com/2026/08/29/moodys-turns-positive-on-nigeria-as-reserves-strengthen-affirms-b3-rating/; Capmad summary, Medium; agency release not read).
- S&P upgraded to B from B- (stable) on 15-May-26, with a Brent assumption of USD 100 for the rest of 2026 (AllAfrica, 19-May-26, Medium). Fitch affirmed B/Stable on 10-Apr-26 (repo knowledge base, Medium; not independently verified).
- The S&P assumption of USD 100 Brent is high relative to the IMF reference scenario of USD 82 (IMF WEO, Apr-26, High). The upgrade is therefore sensitive to oil.

### External position
- Gross reserves were $55.07B at 09-Oct-26 (CBN, via Nairametrics corrigendum, 14-Oct-26, https://nairametrics.com/2026/10/14/corrigendum-nigerias-external-reserves-figure-corrected-following-cbn-data-revision/, Medium-High) and $54.98B at 05-Oct-26 (CBN bulletin, High). The IMF series including gold was $54.85B in Aug-26, up 30.8% in 12 months; ex gold $51.78B (IMF International Liquidity, High).
- Import cover is 9.2 months (adjudicated prior document, Medium).
- Current account (IMF BOP, High), USD bn: 2025-Q2 5.17, Q3 4.06, Q4 1.40, 2026-Q1 4.98; last four quarters $15.61B. Goods and secondary income (remittances, $5.6B–$6.2B a quarter) carry it; services run at about -$3.7B and primary income at -$1.7B to -$3.3B.
- IMF CPI-based REER is 93.29 (Aug-26), 10-year z-score -0.51. There is no overvaluation signal (IMF, High). Commodity terms of trade are up 4.6% in the 12 months to May-26 (IMF CTOT, High).
- Upstream output was 1.68M bpd in Aug-26 and Dangote throughput 736,470 bpd, with fuel imports down 26% (NUPRC, Aug-26, Medium).
- Net usable reserves are unknown because the swap and forward book is not in the record (open item 5).

### Monetary policy and real rates
- MPR is 23.00% with a +50/-300 bps corridor from the 307th MPC on 22-Sep-26 (CBN, High). The IMF series shows 27.0% from Nov-25 to Apr-26 (High). The path between is unreconciled (open item 12).
- CPI was 15.39% y/y in Aug-26 (0.71% m/m), down from 15.93% in May-26 (NBS, High). MPR minus CPI is +761 bps, ex-post.
- ₦ per USD was 1,352.97 in Aug-26 against 1,379.98 in Mar-26 (BIS, High). Next MPC: 24-Nov-26 (CBN calendar, Medium).

### Fiscal and debt
- Total public debt was ₦166.79T ($120.93B) at 30-Jun-26: domestic ₦91.59T, external $54.52B (DMO, 25-Sep-26, High). Debt/GDP is 32.3% (DMO, Jun-26, Medium; federal versus total public label unclear).
- Federal interest absorbs more than 55% of retained revenue (IMF Article IV concluding statement, 01-Jun-26, Medium; basis unresolved).
- IMF WEO 2026E: fiscal balance -4.4% of GDP, real growth 4.1%, current account +5.8% of GDP, nominal GDP $377.4B (Medium).
- No primary balance, gross financing need or revenue/GDP figure is available, so the pb* gap cannot be computed. The 2027 budget and borrowing plan are unsourced (open item 10).

### Eurobonds and market access
- Eurobonds outstanding are $15.12B, 27.7% of external debt (DMO, 30-Jun-26, High).
- Nov-25 dual tranche raised $2.35B: 2036 at 8.63% and 2046 at 9.13%. The repo gives $1.15B/$1.20B (DMO); press gives $1.25B/$1.10B (gazettengr.com, https://gazettengr.com/nigeria-sells-2-35-billion-in-10-year-20-year-eurobonds/). Total and coupons agree; the split is unresolved (Medium).
- Next maturity: $1.50B 6.500% bullet, 28-Nov-27 (DMO, Sep-26, High on the repo record; not re-checked against the DMO bond list).
- No levels are available, so relative value cannot be assessed.

### Banking system
- Q2-26 FSIs, deposit takers: CAR 13.15%, Tier 1 11.43%, NPL 10.82% (10.59% in Q1), provisions/NPL 64.11% (51.45% in Q1), ROE 28.11%, FX open position -12.67% of capital (IMF FSI, High). NPLs are rising slowly while provisioning improves. The FSI definition may include forbearance.
