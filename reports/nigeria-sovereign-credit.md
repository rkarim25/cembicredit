## View
- Constructive on ability to pay through the 28-Nov-27 $1.50B bullet. All three agencies have a positive action in 2026: S&P B (15-May-26), Moody's B3/Positive (28/29-Aug-26), Fitch B/Positive (09-Oct-26) (press, Medium-High).
- External position is the strongest leg. Gross reserves were $55.07B at 09-Oct-26 (CBN, after a downward revision from about $58B). The current account surplus was $17.49B over the last four quarters (IMF BOP to 2026-Q2, High).
- Disinflation continues: CPI was 14.63% in Sep-26 (15.39% in Aug-26) against an MPR of 23%.
- Fiscal is the binding constraint. Debt is low at 32.3% of GDP (Fitch expects about 32% on average over 2026–28, against a 56% median for B-rated peers), but interest is projected at 53.7% of revenue in 2026 (Medium).
- Biggest risk: oil. The external surplus and the S&P upgrade rest on Brent near USD 100. The IMF Oct-26 WEO assumes USD 87.39 for 2027, with Hormuz reopening gradually from Mar-27. The 16-Jan-27 election is the second risk, through fiscal slippage.
- Reserve quality is below the headline. Press says a substantial share of inflows is portfolio money, unquantified (Medium-Low).
- No market levels, net reserves, IMF status or 2027 budget are sourced, so relative value cannot be judged. Confidence: Medium on fundamentals, Low on value.

## Analysis

### Ratings and reform trajectory
- Fitch affirmed B and revised the outlook to Positive on 09-Oct-26. It cited reserves of $54.9B at 25-Sep-26 against $32B in mid-April 2024, a 2026 current account surplus of 6.4% of GDP, and reserve cover of 6.3 months of current external payments by end-2026 (Nairametrics, 10-Oct-26; VON; ThisDay, 11-Oct-26, Medium-High; release not read).
- Fitch moved 11 Nigerian banks to Positive on 15–16-Oct-26, on lower sovereign risk (Nairametrics, 15-Oct-26; ThisDay, 16-Oct-26, Medium).
- Moody's affirmed B3 and moved the outlook to positive on 28/29-Aug-26 (Nairametrics, 29-Aug-26; Capmad, Medium). It cited reserves, growth and the current account, and flagged weak revenue capacity and debt affordability.
- S&P upgraded to B from B- (stable) on 15-May-26, assuming Brent at USD 100 for the rest of 2026 (Nairametrics, 16-May-26; AllAfrica, 19-May-26, Medium). S&P names reversal of FX or subsidy reforms and expansionary fiscal policy as downgrade triggers (position, Medium). S&P projected 2026 inflation of 17.7% in May-26, against Fitch's 15.4% (Medium).
- The IMF April reference oil scenario was USD 82 (IMF WEO, Apr-26, High), so the upgrade is oil-sensitive.

### External position
- Gross reserves were $55.07B at 09-Oct-26 (CBN via Nairametrics corrigendum, 14-Oct-26, Medium-High). A $55.26B print on 30-Oct-26 is reported in market coverage (Nairametrics, 03-Nov-26, Medium, secondary). The IMF series including gold was $54.85B in Aug-26 and $54.73B in Sep-26, up 27.2% in 12 months. Ex gold it was $51.85B in Sep-26 (IMF International Liquidity, High).
- The CBN revised an early-October print of about $58B down to $55.07B. This lowers confidence in headline prints (Medium).
- Current account (IMF BOP, High), USD bn: 2025-Q3 4.06, 2025-Q4 1.40, 2026-Q1 4.49, 2026-Q2 7.54; last four quarters $17.49B. 2025-Q4 shows the sensitivity. Primary income deficit was -$4.20B in 2026-Q2.
- Fitch projects a 2026 surplus of 6.4% of GDP, against 5.8% in the IMF WEO as cited earlier (Medium).
- The commodity terms-of-trade index rose 4.6% over 12 months and jumped in Mar-26, consistent with the oil shock (IMF CTOT, High).
- Quality caveat: press says a substantial portion of the reserve build is foreign portfolio inflow (Nairametrics, 03-Nov-26, Medium-Low). The share is unquantified, and net reserves are unknown (open item 5).
- Import cover is 9.2 months in the repo (Medium), against Fitch's 6.3 months of current external payments. The definitions differ and are not reconciled.
- REER (IMF, CPI-based) is 93.29 in Aug-26, 10-year z-score -0.51: no overvaluation signal (High). ₦ per USD was 1,352.97 in Aug-26 (BIS, High). Fitch cites about 7% naira appreciation in the first nine months of 2026 (ThisDay, 16-Oct-26, Medium).
- Upstream output was 1.68M bpd in Aug-26, with crude at the 1.50M bpd OPEC quota. Dangote throughput was 736,470 bpd (NUPRC, Aug-26, Medium). The quota caps the volume benefit of higher prices.

### Oil
- Brent was 112.44 on 30-Oct-26, up 13% in October (Trading Economics, repo log, Medium).
- The IMF Oct-26 WEO assumes USD 95.74 in 2026 and USD 87.39 in 2027, with Hormuz reopening gradually from Mar-27 (summaries of the WEO, 13-Oct-26, Medium).
- The only budget benchmark found is the 2025–27 MTEF at $75 Brent and ₦1,400/USD (Medium-Low, old). No 2027 budget proposal was found.
- Normalisation of Hormuz flows would erode the oil cushion just as the election and the 2027 budget approach.

### Monetary policy and real rates
- MPR is 23.00% from the 307th MPC on 22-Sep-26 (CBN, High). The path from 27.0% in the IMF series to Apr-26 is unreconciled (open item 12).
- CPI was 14.63% y/y in Sep-26 and 0.06% m/m. Food was 19.59% and core 12.95% (NBS release 15-Oct-26 via Legit.ng, Tekedia, VON, Medium; NBS report not read). MPR minus CPI is about +837 bps ex-post.
- Risk: a deep cut on 24-Nov-26 ahead of the election would raise the risk of an FX relapse (judgement).
- Next MPC: 24-Nov-26 (CBN calendar, Medium).

### Fiscal and debt
- Total public debt was ₦166.79T ($120.93B) at 30-Jun-26: domestic ₦91.59T, external $54.52B (DMO, 25-Sep-26, High). Debt/GDP is 32.3% (DMO, Jun-26, Medium).
- IMF Article IV: interest/revenue is 53.7% for 2026 and 52.4% for 2027. Public external debt is projected to rise from $51.9B in 2025 to $72.6B in 2027 (IMF Article IV, 01-Jun-26, via BusinessDay, Medium). The basis is unresolved and the repo says ">55%" (open item 9). Both figures are well above the framework's 30% danger line.
- S&P projects revenue at 12.4% of GDP in 2026 against 7.3% in 2023 (S&P, via position, Medium). This is the main structural improvement in the fiscal story.
- IMF WEO 2026E: fiscal balance -4.4% of GDP, real growth 4.1%, nominal GDP $377.4B (Medium).
- The IMF Article IV flags budget execution gaps (Guardian Nigeria, 2026, Medium-Low).
- No primary balance or gross financing need is available, so the pb* gap cannot be computed.

### Eurobonds and market access
- Eurobonds outstanding are $15.12B, 27.7% of external debt (DMO, 30-Jun-26, High).
- The Nov-25 dual tranche raised $2.35B, with more than $13B of orders (Nairametrics, 06-Nov-25, Medium). The 2036 priced at 8.63% and the 2046 at 9.13%. The split is disputed (open item 11).
- Next maturity: $1.50B 6.500% bullet, 28-Nov-27 (DMO, High). Near-term refinancing risk is low.
- BusinessDay reports Nigeria is seeking advisers for its first Eurobond sale since November. The date was not confirmed, so treat it as unverified (Low; open item 10).
- No levels are available, so relative value cannot be assessed.

### Politics
- The presidential election is reported for 16-Jan-27 and the governorship elections for 06-Feb-27 (AllAfrica, 27-Feb-26; Semafor, 16-Feb-26, Medium). APC zones backed Tinubu as sole candidate (AllAfrica, 26-Mar-26, Medium).
- Commentary is split on relief spending and borrowing (Investadvocate, 03-Nov-26, Low; Vanguard, Nov-26, Low). It is not weighted. Fitch cited reform continuity ahead of the election.

### Banking system
- Q2-26 FSIs: CAR 13.15%, Tier 1 11.43%, NPL 10.82% (10.59% in Q1), provisions/NPL 64.11% (51.45%), ROE 28.11%, FX open position -12.67% of capital (IMF FSI, High). NPLs rise slowly while provisioning improves. NPLs above 12% or the end of forbearance would change this view.
