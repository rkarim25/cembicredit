## View
- Neutral to modestly constructive. Near-term ability to pay in dollars is strong, but fiscal capacity is weak, so this stays a single-B credit (Medium confidence).
- External: gross reserves are $54.98B (05-Oct-26), up 30.8% in 12 months. The current account has run a $15.61B surplus over the last four quarters. Eurobonds total $15.12B, with no bullet before 28-Nov-27 ($1.50B). Rollover risk through 2027 is low.
- Ratings: S&P B/Stable (15-May-26), Fitch B/Stable (10-Apr-26), Moody's B3/Positive (Aug-26). Moody's is the likeliest to move up (not verified against agency pages).
- Fiscal is the binding constraint. Debt is about 32% of GDP, but interest takes over 55% of retained revenue, against the framework's 30% threshold where defaults cluster.
- The policy rate is 23.00% against CPI of 15.39%. That supports the naira and carry, but it keeps domestic financing costs and the interest burden high.
- Biggest risk: an oil price or output shock that hits FX inflows and revenue together.
- This run had no new captures and no live web verification. Ratings, NUPRC and DMO figures are carried over unverified. Confidence: Medium overall; Low on value, since there are no market levels.

## Analysis

### Ratings and reform trajectory
- S&P B/Stable (15-May-26), Fitch B/Stable (10-Apr-26), Moody's B3/Positive (Aug-26) (repo knowledge base, rating agencies; not verified against agency pages, Medium).
- The brief gives no rationale for the S&P upgrade or the Fitch affirmation. Statements about the drivers (subsidy removal, FX unification) are background, not sourced this run (Low).

### External position
- Gross reserves were $54.98B on 05-Oct-26 (CBN daily bulletin, High). The monthly series, including gold, was $54.85B in Aug-26 (IMF International Liquidity, High). Reserves excluding gold rose from $45.35B (Mar-26) to $51.78B (Aug-26) (IMF data portal, High).
- Reserves are up 30.8% in the 12 months to Aug-26 (IMF, derived, High). Import cover is 9.2 months (adjudicated prior document, IMF/CBN, Medium).
- Current account, IMF BPM6, USD bn: 2025-Q2 5.17, Q3 4.06, Q4 1.40, 2026-Q1 4.98; last four quarters $15.61B (IMF BOP, High). The Q4-25 dip shows volatility.
- Composition: goods surplus of $1.8B–$6.0B a quarter, services about -$3.7B, secondary income (remittances) $5.6B–$6.2B. The primary income deficit widened from -$1.72B (Q2-25) to -$2.83B (Q1-26), peaking at -$3.27B in Q4-25 (IMF BOP, High). The surplus therefore rests on goods and remittances.
- FDI liabilities are about $1.0B–$1.5B a quarter (IMF BOP, High).
- IMF CPI-based REER is 93.29 (Aug-26), z-score -0.51 against its 10-year mean. It has risen from 86.72 in Mar-26. There is no overvaluation signal (IMF, High).
- Commodity terms of trade are up 4.6% in the 12 months to May-26 (IMF CTOT, High).
- Upstream output was 1.68M bpd in Aug-26, with crude at the 1.50M bpd OPEC quota. Dangote throughput was 736,470 bpd, with fuel imports down 26% (NUPRC, Aug-26, Medium; regulator summary not checked against the primary document).
- Net usable reserves are unknown: the swap and forward book is not in the brief (see open item 6).

### Monetary policy and real rates
- MPR is 23.00% with a +50/-300 bps corridor, set at the 307th MPC on 22-Sep-26 (CBN, High). The IMF series shows 27.0% from Nov-25 to Apr-26 (IMF, High).
- CPI was 15.39% YoY in Aug-26 (0.71% MoM), down from 15.93% in May-26 (NBS, IMF data portal, High).
- MPR minus CPI is +761 bps. This is an ex-post real rate. The true ex-ante rate needs forward inflation (open item 5).
- BIS shows ₦1,352.97/USD in Aug-26, against ₦1,379.98 in Mar-26 (BIS, High).

### Fiscal and debt
- Total public debt was ₦166.79T ($120.93B) at 30-Jun-26: domestic ₦91.59T (54.9%), external $54.52B (DMO, 25-Sep-26, High). The implied FX rate is about ₦1,379/$.
- Debt/GDP is about 32.3% (DMO/IMF, Jun-26, Medium). The cross-check of $120.93B against IMF WEO 2026E nominal GDP of $377.4B gives about 32.0%. The 32.3% label is "federal government" in one place and "total public" in another; the definition is unclear.
- Interest absorbs over 55% of retained revenue (IMF Article IV, 01-Jun-26, Medium). The definition is unresolved (open item 10).
- IMF WEO 2026E: fiscal balance -4.4% of GDP, real growth 4.1%, current account +5.8% of GDP (IMF WEO, Medium).
- No primary balance, gross financing need or revenue/GDP figure is in the brief, so the pb* gap cannot be computed.
- Revenue strength is the constraint, not the debt ratio. A high policy rate lowers the carry cost of reserves but raises the domestic interest bill.

### Eurobonds and market access
- Eurobonds outstanding are $15.12B, 27.7% of external debt (DMO, Jun-26; ratio computed, High).
- DMO issued $2.35B in Nov-25: $1.15B 2036 at 8.63% and $1.20B 2046 at 9.13% (DMO, Nov-25, High on issue terms).
- Next maturity: $1.50B 6.500% bullet on 28-Nov-27 (DMO, Sep-26, High). No Eurobond amortisation is flagged for 2026.
- No current levels are available, so relative value cannot be assessed.

### Banking system
- Q2-26 IMF FSIs (deposit takers): CAR 13.15%, Tier 1 11.43%, NPL 10.82% (10.59% in Q1), provisions/NPL 64.11% (51.45% in Q1), ROE 28.11%, FX open position -12.67% of capital (IMF FSI, High).
- NPLs are rising slowly while provisioning improved. The system is adequately capitalised.
