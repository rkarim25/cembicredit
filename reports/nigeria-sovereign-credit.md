- Sovereign credit ratings sit consolidated in the single-B tier at S&P B (Stable, 15-May-26), Fitch B (Stable, 10-Apr-26), and Moody's B3 (Positive, Aug-26) (Rating Agencies, Aug-26, High).
- Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) gross external reserves reached $54.98B as of 05-Oct-26, up from under $33B in 2023, bolstered by oil exports, remittance formalization, and portfolio inflows (CBN, 05-Oct-26, High).
- Dangote Refinery ramped to 736k bpd in August 2026, cutting nationwide refined petroleum imports by 26% and structurally reducing sovereign FX leakage (NUPRC / midstream data, Aug-26, High).
- Headline CPI slowed to 15.39% YoY in August 2026, anchoring an ex-ante real policy rate above +760 bps following CBN's MPR reset to 23.00% (NBS / CBN, Sep-26, High).

## Analysis

### Sovereign credit ratings and reform trajectory
- Nigeria's credit trajectory is anchored by the decisive elimination of universal PMS fuel subsidies and the unification of multiple foreign exchange windows into the Nigerian Autonomous Foreign Exchange Market (NAFEM) (IMF, 01-Jun-26, High).
- S&P Global Ratings upgraded Nigeria from B- to B with a Stable outlook on 15-May-26, citing improved foreign exchange availability, reserve accumulation, and fiscal reform adherence (S&P Global Ratings, 15-May-26, High).
- Fitch Ratings affirmed Nigeria at B with a Stable outlook on 10-Apr-26, balancing enhanced monetary credibility against high debt service burdens (Fitch Ratings, 10-Apr-26, High).
- Moody's maintains a B3 rating with a Positive outlook (updated August 2026), signalling potential upward convergence toward the B2/B tier if external liquidity gains prove structural (Moody's Investors Service, Aug-26, High).

### External position, hydrocarbon production, and FX liquidity
- Central Bank of Nigeria gross external reserves stood at $54.98B as of 05-Oct-26, up from $54.92B on 29-Sep-26 and below $33B in mid-2023 (CBN, 05-Oct-26, High).
- Reserve accumulation has been driven by increased crude receipts, formalization of diaspora remittances via International Money Transfer Operators (IMTOs), and foreign portfolio inflows into high-yielding OMO bills (CBN, Sep-26, High).
- Upstream crude and condensate output averaged 1.68M bpd in August 2026, with pure crude production meeting Nigeria's OPEC quota of 1.50M bpd due to intensified pipeline surveillance in the Niger Delta (NUPRC, Aug-26, High).
- The Dangote Refinery processed 736,470 bpd (105.2% nameplate capacity) in August 2026, lowering national refined petroleum imports by 26% and reducing foreign currency drainage by an estimated $1.2B per month (NUPRC / midstream data, Aug-26, High).
- The gross reserve buffer covers approximately 9.2 months of imports of goods and services, though exact net usable liquid reserves remain encumbered by commercial bank FX swap obligations and forward contracts estimated at $8B to $12B (IMF / CBN, Jun-26, Medium).

### Monetary policy, disinflation, and real rates
- The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) conducted an operational realignment at its 307th meeting on 22-Sep-26, resetting the Monetary Policy Rate (MPR) by 350 bps to 23.00% with a symmetric corridor of +50/-300 bps (CBN, 22-Sep-26, High).
- Disinflation has accelerated: the National Bureau of Statistics reported August 2026 headline inflation slowing to 15.39% YoY (0.71% MoM), compared to peak levels above 33% in early 2024 (NBS, 15-Sep-26, High).
- Food inflation decelerated to 17.84% YoY, aided by domestic harvest inflows and reduced transport logistics costs following local refinery output (NBS, 15-Sep-26, High).
- The ex-ante real policy rate stands deeply positive at +761 bps (23.00% MPR minus 15.39% CPI), providing strong nominal protection that anchors exchange rate stability around ₦1,380/USD on the NAFEM window (CBN / NBS, Sep-26, High).

### Fiscal dynamics, debt sustainability, and interest absorption
- Total public debt stood at ₦166.79T ($120.93B) at end-June 2026 according to the Debt Management Office release on 25-Sep-26 (DMO, 25-Sep-26, High).
- Domestic debt represents 54.9% (₦91.59T), while external debt accounts for 45.1% ($54.52B / ₦75.20T) (DMO, 25-Sep-26, High).
- Public debt-to-GDP remains moderate at ~32.3%, well below the 50% DMO self-imposed ceiling and the 70% ECOWAS convergence benchmark (IMF / DMO, Jun-26, High).
- Debt service affordability is Nigeria's core credit constraint: federal interest payments absorb over 55% of retained revenue, reflecting elevated local borrowing yields on FGN bonds and treasury bills (IMF Article IV, 01-Jun-26, High).
- Non-oil revenue mobilization has improved to ~9.4% of GDP through tax administration reforms and automation, but remains below the 15% sub-Saharan African average required to structurally alleviate interest strain (Budget Office, Jun-26, Medium).

### Eurobond debt structure and international market access
- External commercial Eurobonds total $15.12B across 12 tranches, representing 27.7% of external debt, with the remainder held by concessional multilaterals (World Bank/IDA, AfDB) and bilaterals (DMO, 25-Sep-26, High).
- Market access was reaffirmed by the DMO's successful $2.35B dual-tranche offering in November 2025: $1.15B 10-year notes due 2036 priced at 8.63% and $1.20B 20-year notes due 2046 priced at 9.13% (DMO, Nov-25, High).
- Proceeds from the 2025 issuance retired the $1.12B 7.625% Eurobond that matured in November 2025 without drawing down central bank foreign reserves (DMO, Nov-25, High).
- Near-term external redemption pressure is low: no Eurobond amortisations fall due in 2026, with the next bullet maturity being the $1.50B 6.500% Eurobond due in November 2027 (DMO, Sep-26, High).
