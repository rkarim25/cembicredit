# UAE sovereign credit

_Updated 09-Oct-26 · run 2026-10-09_0725_uae-sovereign-credit_

## View
- UAE (federal and Abu Dhabi) remains a top-tier GCC sovereign credit. Press reports show S&P AA/stable (06-Mar-26) and Fitch AA-/stable (May-26) affirmed despite the regional conflict.
- Credit strength rests on sovereign asset buffers, not on reserves or federal debt. Fitch, via press, puts Abu Dhabi government financial assets above 300% of GDP and sovereign foreign assets near 164% of UAE GDP (2025).
- IMF WEO 2026E shows general government debt of 31.4% of GDP, a current account surplus of 11.4% and a fiscal surplus of 4.9%. The vintage may pre-date the Iran war.
- Biggest risk: prolonged Hormuz disruption. Exports have partly rerouted via the Habshan–Fujairah pipeline; Fitch expected a gradual reopening from July. IMF staff (Jul-26) expect the external surplus to persist but narrow.
- Gross reserves fell 14.1% from USD 302.4bn (Feb-26) to USD 259.7bn (Jul-26), with the AED peg unchanged at 3.6725. The cause is unexplained; this is the main data anomaly.
- Banks are sound (CAR 16.8%, NPL 2.8%, 2026-Q1). The FX open position rose to 78.6% of capital from 71.9%.
- Market pricing and relative value are not assessed; they need Bloomberg.
- Confidence: Medium on the credit view. The rating and conflict claims rest on press summaries of primary documents that were not opened.

## Analysis
### Ratings and buffers
- S&P affirmed AA/A-1+ stable on 06-Mar-26 (Khaleej Times/Enterprise press reports, Medium). Fitch affirmed AA- stable in May-26 (Enterprise, 06-May-26; Khaleej Times, Medium). Moody's was not verified.
- Fitch, via press: Abu Dhabi sovereign foreign assets are about 164% of UAE GDP (2025) and government financial assets exceed 300% of GDP at end-2025 (Medium; agency release not opened).
- IMF WEO 2026E: general government debt is 31.4% of GDP (repo data record, 09-Oct-26, High on the figure).
- The federal budget surplus entering 2026 was AED 17.4bn (press, Medium).

### Hormuz and the oil shock
- Exports continued via the Habshan–Fujairah pipeline, and Fitch expected a gradual reopening from July (press summary, May-26, Medium).
- The IMF staff visit (Jul-26, via WAM) expects the external surplus to continue but narrow, on weaker non-oil trade and disrupted energy shipments (Medium).
- Commodity terms of trade are up 13.2% over 12 months to May-26 (IMF CTOT, High). The index jumped from 87.5 (Feb-26) to 99.0 (Mar-26), consistent with the oil price shock.
- Status of Hormuz and Fujairah volumes since Jul-26 is unknown; this is an open item.

### External position
- WEO 2026E: current account +11.4% of GDP, growth 3.1%, nominal GDP USD 621.5bn (IMF WEO, High on the figures; vintage unverified).
- Gross reserves including gold (IMF International Liquidity, High):

| Date | USD bn |
|---|---|
| Feb-26 | 302.4 |
| Mar-26 | 276.7 |
| Apr-26 | 276.8 |
| May-26 | 267.4 |
| Jun-26 | 257.5 |
| Jul-26 | 259.7 |

- Reserves excluding gold fell from USD 289.8bn (Feb-26) to USD 250.0bn (Jul-26). Gold fell from USD 12.6bn to USD 9.7bn.
- Twelve-month change in gross reserves is -3.1% (Jul-25 to Jul-26). The Feb–Jul fall is therefore a recent move, with the largest step in Mar-26 (-USD 25.7bn).
- Import cover was 5.2 months (2025, World Bank WDI). Net FDI was 8.3% of GDP (2024, World Bank WDI).
- The AED/USD rate is 3.6725 in every month Apr–Sep 2026. The peg shows no strain in the data.
- The cause of the reserve fall is unexplained. Benign explanations (transfers to government or sovereign funds, liquidity operations, valuation) and adverse ones (peg defence, outflows) cannot be distinguished from the repo data. Given the asset buffers, the stock is not a solvency signal, but the flow matters for reading peg stress.
- Broad real effective exchange rate: 97.08 (Aug-26), z-score -1.12 versus the 10-year mean (BIS, High). This implies no overvaluation.

### Fiscal and inflation
- WEO 2026E fiscal balance is +4.9% of GDP (High on the figure; vintage unverified). Oil price strength and rerouted volumes offset by the shipping disruption leave the 2026 outturn uncertain.
- CPI was 2.05% y/y in Dec-25 (IMF CPI database, High). This is the latest available print.

### Banking system
- IMF FSI (deposit takers, High): 2026-Q1 CAR 16.83% (Q4-25 17.03%), tier 1 15.65% (15.84%), NPL 2.79% (2.94%), provisions to NPL 51.76% (51.31%), ROA 2.13% (2.15%), ROE 19.38% (15.01%).
- FX open position to capital was 78.62% in Q1-26 (71.88% in Q4-25). The rise is a watch point; its drivers are not identified.

### Not covered in this run
Primary rating agency releases, the IMF 2026 Article IV, CBUAE monetary data for Q2-26, Abu Dhabi budget and ADIA/Mubadala movements, Eurobond issuance and index weights, and market spreads were not reviewed. Searching was limited to headline-level results.
