## View
- Gulf support is a conditional anchor. It has moved from open-ended deposit rollovers to longer tenors, conversion into investments and IMF-gated commitments (Medium).
- Pakistan is the strongest anchor. Saudi Arabia rolled USD 5bn for three years to Dec-28, on top of USD 3bn of fresh deposits in Apr-26. S&P upgraded Pakistan to B on 22-Jul-26. The anchor still covers only part of the USD 21.5bn external financing need (Medium–High).
- Egypt is the live test. The deposit lock is tied to the EFF, which expires 15-Dec-26, and the Saudi USD 5.3bn medium-term deposit matured this month with no confirmed outcome. Conversion into investments is the likelier route, but this is inference (Low–Medium).
- Anchors change the recovery distribution as well as default probability. Gulf creditors prefer equity and asset stakes, so bondholders cannot assume pari passu treatment (framework High; application to Egypt Low).
- Donor capacity is lower but intact. Gulf growth is weak after the Iran war, while Saudi Arabia (Aa3), Kuwait and the UAE remain highly rated. Saudi–UAE divergence makes support more selective (Medium).
- Biggest risk: the Egypt deposits lapse or are converted on poor terms as the IMF programme ends in Dec-26.
- Confidence: Medium. Deposit and IMF figures rest on press reports and search summaries. The IMF Egypt Country Reports were not read in full.

## What changed
- Egypt: the IMF Board completed the seventh EFF review on 30-Jul-26. The EFF expires 15-Dec-26, and the final review is expected in Q4-26. The USD 18.3bn deposit lock now has an IMF-assurance source (Country Report 26/224, via summary) in addition to the New Arab.
- Egypt: Saudi–Egypt investment talks of nearly USD 10bn (15-Sep-26) and the move from deposits to energy logistics were added. These support the conversion thesis, which remains unconfirmed.
- Pakistan: added the USD 12.5bn of Saudi, Chinese and UAE deposits held under the IMF programme to Sep-27, and the SBP expectation of reserves above USD 20bn by Dec-26 (both Medium).
- Bahrain: ratings are now largely resolved. Moody's B2 negative (Apr-26) and S&P B stable with a UAE swap are sourced, and Fitch B+ negative is from Feb-26. The previous open item narrows to actions since May-26.
- Open items 1, 4, 5, 6 and 9 are kept. Items 2 and 3 are replaced by narrower versions. Items 8 and 10 are new.

## Analysis
### Pakistan
- SBP Governor Jameel Ahmad said Saudi Arabia rolled USD 5bn for three years, to Dec-28. Total Saudi deposits are USD 8bn, including USD 3bn received in Apr-26 (Tribune, 29-Jul-26, https://tribune.com.pk/story/2620947/ksa-rolled-over-5-billion-for-three-years-sbp-governor-says; Geo, 30-Jul-26, https://www.geo.tv/latest/675357-saudi-arabia-rolls-over-5bn-deposits-with-pakistan-for-three-years; ProPakistani, 30-Jul-26, https://propakistani.pk/2026/07/30/saudis-roll-over-5-billion-to-pakistan-for-3-years/) (Medium–High).
- Press summaries say USD 12.5bn of Saudi, Chinese and UAE deposits are to stay with the SBP until Sep-27 under the IMF programme. No primary source was read (undated, Medium).
- External payments are USD 21.5bn, of which USD 18bn is principal. About USD 11bn is expected to be rolled this fiscal year. Reserves were USD 17.3bn on 17-Jul-26, and the SBP expects over USD 20bn by Dec-26 (SBP via Tribune, 29-Jul-26; aaj.tv, Jul-26, https://english.aaj.tv/news/330465701) (Medium).
- S&P upgraded Pakistan to B, stable, citing IMF-backed reforms (Profit/Pakistan Today, 22-Jul-26, https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/07/22/sandp-upgrades-pakistans-sovereign-credit-rating-to-b-on-imf-backed-reforms) (Medium).
- Reserves include deposits, so usable reserves are smaller than the headline. This is inference from the framework (Medium).
- The tenor lifts the Saudi cliff past the typical IMF horizon, but the anchor covers only part of the financing need. Chinese and commercial rollovers still matter.

### Egypt
- The IMF Board completed the seventh EFF review on 30-Jul-26, releasing about USD 1.8bn. The EFF expires 15-Dec-26, and the final review is expected in Q4-26 with about USD 2.3bn available (ET Business View, 02-Oct-26, https://etbusinessview.com/2026/10/02/imf-says-egypts-final-eff-review-expected-in-fourth-quarter/) (Medium–High).
- IMF Country Report 26/224 reportedly gives an assurance that about USD 18.3bn of GCC deposits will not be withdrawn before the EFF ends in Dec-26, except for equity purchases (https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/english/1egyea2026002.pdf; read via search summary only). The New Arab gives the same USD 18.3bn figure (22-Sep-26, https://www.newarab.com/news/talks-over-saudi-deposit-egypts-bank-amid-iran-yemen-wars) (Medium).
- The "equity purchases" carve-out is the route through which a lock can end in conversion rather than cash repayment. No source states that this is the plan (inference, Low–Medium).
- The CBE holds about USD 10.3bn of Saudi deposits: USD 5bn short-term, renewed annually, and about USD 5.3bn medium-term maturing Oct-26. The New Arab gives USD 5.434bn under negotiation for renewal or conversion. A swap into real estate and other sectors was under discussion (Enterprise, 25-May-25, https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2025/05/25/saudi-deposits-could-be-converted-into-new-investments-in-real-estate-among-other-sectors/; New Arab, 22-Sep-26) (Medium). No outcome was found (Open item 1).
- Saudi Arabia and Egypt discussed nearly USD 10bn of investment on 15-Sep-26 (ORF Middle East, 13-Oct-26, https://orfme.org/expert-speak/egypts-gulf-anchor-from-financial-lifeline-to-energy-buffer/) (Medium–Low).
- Gulf deposits at the CBE peaked at USD 29.9bn in 2023 and are about USD 18.3bn now. The SUMED pipeline is at its 2.5m b/d capacity, and the Fujairah-Alamein Oil and Gas Company was created in Aug-26. Gulf commentators question Egypt's lack of military support in the Iran confrontation (ORF Middle East, 13-Oct-26; Arab Center Washington, https://arabcenterdc.org/resource/egypt-and-the-gulf-a-relationship-under-pressure/, undated) (Medium–Low, think-tank commentary).
- Kuwait's USD 2bn deposit was renewed in Dec-24, and a further renewal was being sought in Apr-26. No outcome was found (AGBI, Dec-24, https://agbi.com/economy/2024/12/kuwait-renews-2bn-deposit-with-central-bank-of-egypt; Enterprise, 09-Apr-26, https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/04/09/central-bank-of-egypt-looks-to-renew-usd-2-bn-kuwaiti-deposit-as-regional-conflict-stalls-fdi-conversions/) (Medium).
- Ratings: S&P B/B stable (Enterprise, 14-Apr-26, https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/04/14/sp-global-affirms-egypts-rating-at-b-b-stable-outlook/) (Medium). Moody's Caa1, positive, is from an undated secondary source (Low–Medium). No 2026 Fitch action was found.
- Conversion into equity or real estate cuts official liabilities but gives the Gulf creditor an asset claim outside the bond waterfall. The Ras El-Hekma ADQ deal (Feb-24, about USD 35bn) is the template (inference, Medium).

### Bahrain
- Moody's changed the outlook to negative and affirmed B2 on war fallout in Apr-26 (The National, 18-Apr-26, https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/economy/2026/04/18/moodys-changes-bahrain-and-iraqs-outlook-to-negative-over-war-fallout/) (Medium).
- S&P affirmed B/B, stable, on GCC support expectations. It cited a five-year UAE central bank currency swap of BHD 2bn in Apr-26 (Investing.com summary, https://au.investing.com/news/world-news/sp-affirms-bahrain-bb-ratings-on-gcc-support-expectations-93CH-4453088). A further press item has S&P at B stable on 23-May-26 (The National) (Medium).
- Fitch affirmed B+, outlook negative, in Feb-26. Its rating includes "exceptionally strong support" from GCC partners, mainly Saudi Arabia and the UAE. It projects debt rising from 130% of GDP in 2024 to about 136% in 2026, and a deficit near 9% of GDP (arab.news, Feb-26, https://arab.news/4zuc4) (Medium).
- A Stimson commentary reportedly says Bahrain may need external help if conditions do not improve quickly. The page returned 403, so this is a search summary only (Low).
- Two of three agencies have a negative outlook and the stable one cites GCC support explicitly. Bahrain is the clearest case of rating-embedded Gulf support.

### Gulf capacity and politics
- Fitch affirmed UAE at AA-. Moody's holds Saudi Aa3, Qatar Aa2 and Kuwait A1. Fitch projects 2026 real GDP of -4.8% for the UAE and -1.7% for Saudi Arabia (The National, 23-May-26, https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/economy/2026/05/23/gulf-countries-maintain-credit-ratings-and-stable-outlooks-amid-iran-war-uncertainties/) (Medium). A search summary of Stimson/Middle East Council puts the 2026 GCC contraction at 4.3% (undated, not verified, Low–Medium).
- Fitch put Qatar and Ras Al Khaimah on Rating Watch Negative (Gulf Daily News, date unconfirmed, Low–Medium).
- Gulf SWFs committed USD 53.9bn across 108 deals in H1-26 (Khaleej Times, Medium). Capital is still deployed, with a tilt toward equity and strategic assets over deposits (inference, Medium).
- Foreign Policy describes a Saudi-led grouping with Qatar, Turkey, Pakistan and Egypt, with the UAE outside it (01-Jul-26, https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/07/01/saudi-qatar-pakistan-turkey-egypt-uae-axis-alliance-iran-middle-east/) (Medium). A Commons Library briefing is titled "The UAE exits OPEC and Saudi-UAE tensions in 2026" (https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10833/); only the title was read (Low–Medium).
- Saudi-linked recipients (Pakistan, Egypt) and UAE-linked recipients (Bahrain swap) carry different political risks. This is inference (Low).
- Gulf investment and Aqaba trade rerouting are reported to support Jordan (AGBI, Sep-26, https://www.agbi.com/analysis/economy/2026/09/gulf-investment-gives-jordan-vital-support-during-war/; headline and summary only) (Low–Medium).

### Reserves treatment
- The framework requires gross reserves, reserves net of one-year drains, and the central bank's own net definition (knowledge/frameworks/sovereign-credit.md, section 10a, High as method).
- Rolled Gulf deposits behave as quasi-reserves. They are conditional, not senior debt, and lapse risk concentrates at maturity dates (Medium).
