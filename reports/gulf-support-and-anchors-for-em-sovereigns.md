# Gulf support and anchors for EM sovereigns

_Updated 29-Oct-26 · run 2026-10-29_0800_gulf-support-and-anchors-for-em-sovereigns_

## View
- Gulf support is a conditional anchor. It has moved from open-ended deposit rollovers to longer tenors, conversion into investments and IMF-gated commitments (Medium).
- Pakistan is the strongest anchor. Saudi Arabia rolled USD 5bn to Dec-28 and added USD 3bn in Apr-26. SBP reserves are now about USD 21.5bn, up from USD 17.3bn in July. Deposits remain a large part of that stock (Medium–High).
- Egypt is the live test. The lock on about USD 18.3bn of GCC deposits ends with the EFF on 15-Dec-26. The Saudi USD 5.3–5.4bn maturity reportedly has a preliminary rollover plus investment agreement, but nothing official confirms it (Low–Medium).
- Anchors change the recovery distribution as well as default probability. Gulf creditors prefer equity and asset stakes, so bondholders cannot assume pari passu treatment (framework High; application to Egypt Low).
- Bahrain is the clearest case of rating-embedded Gulf support (UAE BHD 2bn swap, S&P). The Fitch rating is disputed between sources and is open (Medium).
- Biggest risk: the Egypt deposits lapse or convert on poor terms as the IMF programme ends, with Gulf capacity weakened by the Iran war.
- Confidence: Medium. This run added no new independent web research. The adjudication rests on the previous document and one position built on press reports and search summaries; IMF reports were not read in full.

## What changed
- Pakistan: SBP reserves updated to USD 21.470bn on 09-Oct-26, with total liquid reserves of USD 26.863bn (ProPakistani, 15-Oct-26). This supersedes USD 17.3bn on 17-Jul-26.
- Egypt: added a reported preliminary agreement of 16-Sep-26 to roll the USD 5.434bn deposit with over USD 10bn of new Saudi investment. It is unconfirmed and conflicts with the New Arab's "no decisive results" (22-Sep-26). Kuwait's plan to convert part of its USD 2bn deposit toward a USD 3bn investment package was added.
- Bahrain: a Fitch downgrade to B, stable, on 23-Feb-26 was reported by one source. It conflicts with the "B+, negative, affirmed" line, so the rating is open rather than replaced.
- Open items: previous items 1–5 are kept in merged form. Items 6, 7 and 8 (CBE reserves, bond spreads) are new or sharpened. The Bahrain rating question is now item 2.

## Analysis
### Pakistan
- SBP reserves were USD 21.470bn and total liquid reserves USD 26.863bn on 09-Oct-26 (ProPakistani, 15-Oct-26, https://propakistani.pk/2026/10/15/total-reserves-near-27-billion/) (High on level, SBP data via press). The SBP figure is reported at USD 21.489bn on 16-Oct-26 (pkrevenue, search summary, Medium).
- Saudi Arabia rolled USD 5bn for three years, to Dec-28. Total Saudi deposits are USD 8bn including USD 3bn received in Apr-26 (Tribune, 29-Jul-26; Geo, 30-Jul-26) (Medium–High). On 17-Jul-26 the rollover was still unconfirmed, with the Finance Minister saying "all good" (Profit, 17-Jul-26) (Medium).
- Press summaries say USD 12.5bn of Saudi, Chinese and UAE deposits stay with the SBP until Sep-27 under the IMF programme. No primary source was read (Medium). The UAE's USD 2bn was reportedly rolled for one to two months at a time in Feb-26 (search summary, Low–Medium).
- External payments are USD 21.5bn, of which USD 18bn is principal. About USD 11bn is expected to be rolled this fiscal year (SBP via Tribune, aaj.tv, Jul-26) (Medium).
- S&P upgraded Pakistan to B, stable, on 22-Jul-26 (Profit/Pakistan Today) (Medium).
- Reserves include deposits, so usable reserves are lower than the headline (inference, Medium).

### Egypt
- The IMF Board completed the seventh EFF review on 30-Jul-26, releasing about USD 1.8bn. The EFF expires 15-Dec-26, and the final review in Q4-26 has about USD 2.3bn available (ET Business View, 02-Oct-26) (Medium–High).
- About USD 18.3bn of GCC deposits cannot be withdrawn before the EFF ends, except for purchases of public-asset stakes, with the foreign-currency proceeds kept in reserves (New Arab, 22-Sep-26; IMF Country Report 26/224 via summary) (Medium).
- Saudi deposits at the CBE total about USD 10.3bn: USD 5bn short-term, renewed annually, and about USD 5.3bn medium-term maturing Oct-26 (USD 5.434bn with return) (Enterprise, 25-May-25; New Arab, 22-Sep-26) (Medium). A 16-Sep-26 report relayed in search summaries describes a preliminary agreement to renew and add over USD 10bn of investments (Low–Medium). The New Arab says the sides "have not yet reached decisive results" (22-Sep-26). Conversion is the likelier route, but that is inference (Low–Medium).
- Kuwait's USD 2bn deposit was renewed in Dec-24 (AGBI). A further renewal, with part to be converted into a USD 3bn investment package, was being sought in Apr-26 (Enterprise, 09-Apr-26). No outcome was found (Medium).
- Gulf deposits peaked at USD 29.9bn in 2023 and are about USD 18.3bn now (ORF Middle East, 13-Oct-26) (Medium–Low).
- S&P affirmed B/B, stable (Enterprise, 14-Apr-26) (Medium). The rating is not visibly tied to the deposit decision. The risk shows in reserves and FX (Medium–Low). No 2026 Fitch or Moody's action was verified.
- Conversion into equity or real estate cuts official liabilities but gives the Gulf creditor an asset claim outside the bond waterfall. The Ras El-Hekma ADQ deal (Feb-24) is the template (inference, Medium).

### Bahrain
- Moody's changed the outlook to negative and affirmed B2 in Apr-26 (The National, 18-Apr-26) (Medium).
- S&P affirmed B/B, stable, on GCC support expectations. It cited a five-year UAE central bank swap of BHD 2bn dated 08-Apr-26 (Investing.com, Apr-26) (Medium).
- Fitch is disputed. arab.news (Feb-26) has B+, outlook negative, affirmed, with "exceptionally strong" GCC support, debt of about 136% of GDP in 2026 (130% in 2024) and a deficit near 9% of GDP. TradingView (Trading Economics feed) reports a cut to B, stable, on 23-Feb-26 (both tier 3, Medium–Low). Neither was confirmed on Fitch's own page (Open item 2).
- Two agencies describe the rating as dependent on GCC support, so Bahrain is the clearest case of rating-embedded Gulf backing.

### Gulf capacity and politics
- Moody's holds Saudi Aa3, Qatar Aa2 and Kuwait A1, and Fitch affirmed the UAE at AA-. Fitch projects 2026 real GDP of -4.8% for the UAE and -1.7% for Saudi Arabia (The National, 23-May-26) (Medium).
- Gulf SWFs committed USD 53.9bn across 108 deals in H1-26 (Khaleej Times) (Medium). Capital is still deployed, tilted toward equity and strategic assets (inference, Medium).
- Foreign Policy (01-Jul-26) describes a Saudi-led grouping with Qatar, Turkey, Pakistan and Egypt, with the UAE outside it (Medium). Saudi-linked and UAE-linked recipients may carry different political risk (inference, Low).
- A New Arab report of Houthi attacks on Saudi oil facilities is single-source and unconfirmed (Low).

### Reserves treatment
- Under the framework, reserves are shown three ways: gross, net of one-year drains, and the central bank's own net definition (knowledge/frameworks/sovereign-credit.md, section 10a, High as method).
- Rolled Gulf deposits behave as quasi-reserves. They are conditional, not senior debt, and lapse risk concentrates at maturity dates (Medium).

### Not found
- Gulf deposit or swap developments for Jordan, Turkey, Oman and Sri Lanka in the last 90 days.
- A confirmed outcome for the Oct-26 Saudi deposit at the CBE or the Kuwaiti renewal.
- Fitch or Moody's 2026 actions on Pakistan or Egypt, and Bahrain actions after May-26.
- A 2026 Brent level. Paywalled Bloomberg, FT and Reuters coverage was not accessible.

## Where the models disagreed
1. Fitch's Bahrain rating. One side cited the knowledge base (B+, negative, affirmed Feb-26). The other cited TradingView (cut to B, stable, 23-Feb-26). Both are tier 3 and neither was confirmed on Fitch's page, so the point is unresolved and recorded as Open item 2 (rule: no winner without a source above tier 4 or a clear tier gap).
2. Egypt Saudi deposit outcome. The position leaned to a preliminary rollover plus USD 10bn of investment. The previous document recorded no outcome. The resolution keeps it unconfirmed with the reported agreement noted at Low–Medium (rule: only secondary, conflicting tier 3 sources).
3. Pakistan reserves. The position's USD 21.47bn (09-Oct-26) is more recent and specific than the knowledge base's USD 17.3bn (17-Jul-26), so it was adopted (rule: more recent and more specific source wins).
