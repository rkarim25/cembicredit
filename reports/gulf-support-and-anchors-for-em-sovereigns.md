## View
- Gulf support is a conditional anchor, not an unconditional one. This is a framework-level judgement and is not refreshed for 2026 (Medium).
- Since the 2023–24 Egypt episode, Gulf support has shifted from open-ended deposits towards asset-linked, equity-for-debt and programme-gated transactions (training knowledge, Medium).
- The anchor changes the recovery distribution as well as default probability, as the sovereign framework states (knowledge/frameworks/sovereign-credit.md, section 6, High as a method).
- Gulf deposits are rolled rather than repaid, so they behave as quasi-reserves and should be netted from usable reserves. They are not senior debt (Medium).
- Anchored names (Egypt, Pakistan, Bahrain, Jordan, Oman) should carry a lower default probability but a wider conditional loss if a rollover is withdrawn (Low, inferred).
- Key drivers: oil and Gulf fiscal breakevens, Gulf domestic reprioritisation, IMF programme status as the gate, and geopolitical alignment.
- Biggest risk: weaker oil reduces Gulf willingness to roll at the same time as a recipient's IMF programme slips.
- Confidence in the overall view: Low to Medium. This run had no new captures and no verified web research after the Jun-26 knowledge cutoff, so no 2026 figures are stated.

## Analysis
### Nature of the anchor
- Saudi Arabia said in Jan-24 that it would change how it gives aid, moving from grants and deposits towards investment (Saudi finance ministry remarks at Davos, Jan-24, from memory, Medium).
- The Ras El-Hekma transaction with ADQ (announced Feb-24, about USD 35bn, part of it a conversion of existing deposits) is the template for asset-linked support (training knowledge, not re-verified, Medium).
- Support is increasingly tied to IMF programme delivery and asset or equity access. It is no longer a cheque without conditions (Medium).
- Inference: the Gulf states prefer tradable equity and asset stakes, which gives them recovery value outside a bond restructuring. Bondholders cannot assume Gulf creditors accept pari passu treatment (Low).

### Treatment in reserves
- The framework asks for three reserve numbers: gross, net of predetermined one-year drains, and the central bank's own net definition (knowledge/frameworks/sovereign-credit.md, section 10a, High as method).
- Gulf deposits sit inside gross reserves at the SBP and CBE. They are repeatedly rolled, so the usable cushion is smaller than gross reserves suggest (Medium).
- Current deposit stocks and maturities are not sourced in this run and are not stated (Open items 3 and 8).

### IMF gate
- IMF staff reports for Egypt and Pakistan have cited Gulf rollover commitments in the financing-assurances paragraph (staff reports 2024–25, from memory, Medium).
- A missed review is therefore the main trigger for non-renewal. The test of this view is a Gulf rollover given despite a missed review.

### Oil and capacity to support
- Weaker oil raises the chance of selective support, favouring strategic names (Egypt, Bahrain, Jordan) over Pakistan, Sri Lanka and African frontier credits (Low, inferred).
- The oil level and Gulf fiscal breakevens were not checked this run (Open item 6).

### Where the anchor drives spreads
- Bahrain: the 2018 GCC package of USD 10bn (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait) is the model case of a Gulf backstop priced into sovereign spreads (from memory, Medium).
- Oman and Jordan are second-order cases (Low).
- Egypt and Pakistan are the largest programme-linked cases.

### Pricing implication
- Spreads on anchored names should embed lower default probability and wider conditional loss. Whether the market prices this correctly cannot be assessed without Bloomberg levels (Open items 4 and 5).
