## View
- Angola's sovereign credit trajectory is inflecting positively from acute debt distress toward stabilization, reflected in Moody's outlook revision to Positive at B3 on 25-Sep-26 alongside B- stable ratings from S&P (14-Aug-26) and Fitch (08-May-26) (Rating Agencies, Sep-26, High).
- External liquidity pressure has eased following proactive liability management, including tender buybacks of ~$750M of 2028/2029 notes and new 2031/2037 debt issuance, capping total outstanding Eurobond stock at $13.06B (MINFIN / UGD, Sep-26, High).
- Structural balance-sheet risk is retreating as bilateral Chinese oil-collateralized loans fell from $16.3B in 2020 to $6.83B in mid-2026, releasing restricted cash from Debt Service Reserve Accounts (DSRAs) ahead of complete liquidation by 2028 (MINFIN / IMF, May-26, High).
- Upstream crude output has stabilized at 1.00–1.05M bpd following Angola's OPEC exit in Jan-2024, supporting an estimated 2026 current account surplus of 2.2% of GDP and real GDP growth of 3.6% (ANPG / BNA / IMF, May-26, High).
- Central bank FX reserves stand at $14.9B in Q2 2026 (~6.8 months import cover), providing an adequate buffer against balance-of-payments shocks (BNA, Jun-26, High).
- Exchange rate stability around 912–913 AOA/USD drove August 2026 headline inflation down to single digits at 8.78% YoY, enabling Banco Nacional de Angola (BNA) to cut the benchmark policy rate by 100 bps to 14.75% while maintaining a restrictive +6.0% ex-ante real rate (INE / BNA, Sep-26, High).
- Public debt-to-GDP consolidated at ~49.2% at end-2025, complying with the 60% statutory threshold under the Fiscal Sustainability Law, supported by primary fiscal surpluses (MINFIN / IMF, May-26, High).
- Gross financing needs will rise into 2027 as domestic Treasury bond maturities accelerate, requiring continued rollover support from domestic commercial banks (IMF, May-26, High).
- Pre-eminent risk: A sustained slump in Brent crude below $65/bbl or accelerated deepwater field depletion that curtails Sonangol FX transfers, drives Kwanza depreciation, and spikes external debt ratios (Rating Agencies / IMF, Sep-26, High).

## Analysis

### Sovereign credit inflection and positive rating momentum (B3 Positive / B- Stable)
- Angola's sovereign credit trajectory is inflecting positively, marking a transition from distressed restructuring risk toward macroeconomic stabilization (Moody's Ratings, 25-Sep-26, High).
- Moody's Ratings affirmed Angola's B3 foreign-currency long-term issuer rating on 25-Sep-26 and revised the rating outlook to Positive from Stable, citing sustained fiscal consolidation, disciplined expenditure management, and the rapid paydown of high-cost bilateral debt (Moody's Ratings, 25-Sep-26, High).
- S&P Global Ratings affirmed Angola at B- with a Stable outlook on 14-Aug-26, emphasizing the country's manageable short-term commercial amortisation profile and stable international reserve position (S&P Global Ratings, 14-Aug-26, High).
- Fitch Ratings affirmed its B- rating with a Stable outlook on 08-May-26, noting that higher non-oil tax mobilization and ongoing primary surpluses have strengthened fiscal buffers despite crude price volatility (Fitch Ratings, 08-May-26, High).

### Liability management and Eurobond maturity smoothing ($13.06B outstanding)
- The Ministério das Finanças and the Unidade de Gestão da Dívida Pública (UGD) have executed aggressive liability management operations to eliminate near-term refinancing cliffs (MINFIN / UGD, Sep-26, High).
- Through early 2026, the authorities completed debt tender offers repurchasing approximately $750M of the 8.250% 2028 and 8.000% 2029 Eurobonds, partially funded by new commercial notes maturing in 2031 and 2037 (UGD, Sep-26, High).
- Total outstanding sovereign Eurobond debt stands at approximately $13.06B across benchmark USD tranches, with the principal repayment schedule smoothed significantly ahead of 2028 (MINFIN, Sep-26, High).
- Outstanding benchmark Eurobond tranches comprise: $1.75B 8.250% due 09-May-28 (XS1819680288), $1.50B 8.000% due 26-Nov-29 (XS2083302419), $1.75B 8.750% due 14-Apr-32 (XS2446175577), $1.25B 9.375% due 08-May-48 (XS1819680528), and $1.75B 9.125% due 26-Nov-49 (XS2083302500) (UGD / Market, Sep-26, High).

### Unwinding of Chinese oil-backed debt and DSRA escrow releases ($6.83B remaining)
- A key structural tailwind in Angolan public finances is the steady liquidation of legacy oil-collateralized bilateral facilities with Chinese state lenders (China Development Bank and Exim Bank of China) (MINFIN / IMF, May-26, High).
- Chinese oil-backed loan obligations have decreased from $16.3B in 2020 to $6.83B as of mid-2026, with MINFIN projecting full retirement of oil-collateralized facilities by end-2028 (MINFIN, Sep-26, High).
- The accelerated debt amortization has enabled the repatriation of foreign currency locked in Debt Service Reserve Accounts (DSRAs) held in Beijing, bolstering general Treasury liquidity and mitigating dollar rationing (MINFIN / IMF, May-26, High).

### Upstream oil production stabilization post-OPEC exit and external balance
- Angola's exit from the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) in January 2024 removed output restrictions, allowing the Agência Nacional de Petróleo, Gás e Biocombustíveis (ANPG) to implement field-life extension programs across deepwater Blocks 15, 17, and 31 (ANPG, Jun-26, High).
- Upstream crude oil production stabilized between 1.00M and 1.05M bpd through H1 2026, supported by accelerated short-cycle infill drilling and tie-backs by major operators (TotalEnergies, Chevron, Azule Energy) (ANPG, Jun-26, High).
- Crude oil exports anchored a current account surplus estimated at 2.2% of GDP for full-year 2026, recovering from compressed surpluses of 0.4%–1.1% in 2024–2025 (IMF / BNA, May-26, High).
- Banco Nacional de Angola (BNA) gross international reserves stood at $14.9B at the end of Q2 2026, providing 6.8 months of import coverage, up from the historical low of 5.5 months in 2023 (BNA, Jun-26, High).

### Monetary disinflation, exchange rate stability, and BNA rate easing (14.75%)
- Domestic foreign exchange trading has stabilized, with the Angolan Kwanza (AOA) trading in an orderly band between 912 and 913 AOA/USD throughout mid-2026, terminating the severe depreciation pressures observed in 2023–2024 (BNA, Sep-26, High).
- FX stability has driven rapid disinflation: the Instituto Nacional de Estatística (INE) reported August 2026 headline inflation slowing to 8.78% YoY, marking the first single-digit print recorded since 2015 (INE, Aug-26, High).
- In response to declining inflation expectations, the BNA Monetary Policy Committee reduced the benchmark policy rate by 100 bps to 14.75% at its September 2026 meeting (its third cut of 2026), leaving the ex-ante real policy rate at an attractive +6.0% (BNA, 18-Sep-26, High).

### Fiscal consolidation, debt-to-GDP (~49.2%), and domestic rollover risks
- Angola's public debt-to-GDP declined to approximately 49.2% at end-2025, operating comfortably below the statutory 60% threshold mandated by the Lei de Sustentabilidade Fiscal (Fiscal Sustainability Law) (MINFIN / IMF, May-26, High).
- Ongoing primary fiscal surpluses (averaging 2.5%–3.0% of GDP) and nominal GDP expansion have offset debt service costs, though IMF projections indicate gross financing needs will rise into 2027 (IMF, May-26, High).
- The domestic banking sector holds substantial portfolios of Obrigações do Tesouro (OTs) and Bilhetes do Tesouro (BTs); managing domestic rollover requirements without crowding out commercial credit represents a crucial near-term policy challenge (IMF, May-26, Medium).

### Political continuity and MPLA institutional governance
- Political authority remains consolidated under President João Lourenço and the ruling Movimento Popular de Libertação de Angola (MPLA) following their victory in the August 2022 general elections (Government of Angola, May-26, High).
- Despite social discontent regarding fuel subsidy rationalization and living costs, the administration maintains institutional control, with the next general elections scheduled for August 2027 (Government of Angola, May-26, High).
