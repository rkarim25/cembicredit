## View
Mozambique is a distressed CC/CCC/Caa3 sovereign credit facing acute fiscal illiquidity, elevated public debt (~94% of GDP), and an amortizing Eurobond maturity cliff starting in 2028.
External debt restructuring risk is elevated as Fitch (CC, 24-Jul-26) and S&P (CCC/SD, 25-Sep-26) flag that securing a successor IMF program will likely require a distressed debt exchange on the sole US$900m Eurobond (XS2051203862).
The 2022 Extended Credit Facility (SDR 340.8m) lapsed in April 2025 with uncompleted reviews, leaving negotiations for a new IMF arrangement ongoing following a September 2026 mission to Maputo.
A transformative medium-term credit anchor is the resumption of TotalEnergies' US$20bn Mozambique LNG project (Area 1), where force majeure was lifted in November 2025 and site works resumed in January 2026 targeting first gas in 2029.
Domestic monetary easing has concluded as Banco de Moçambique kept its MIMO policy rate at 9.25% in September 2026, balancing exchange rate pressures against 6.5% YoY inflation.
S&P maintains its local currency rating at SD due to persistent domestic payment delays and treasury cash rationing, despite gross international reserves covering ~6.5 months of imports.
The political transition concluded with President Daniel Chapo taking office in January 2025, but post-election protests demonstrated underlying social friction.
The primary credit risk is that near-term fiscal illiquidity forces a preemptive Eurobond restructuring before LNG fiscal windfalls materialize in 2029–2030.
Overall view confidence: Medium.

## Analysis

### 1. External debt profile and 2028–2031 maturity cliff
Mozambique's commercial external debt consists solely of the US$900m 9.00% Eurobond maturing on 15-September-2031 (XS2051203862) (MEF, Jun-26, High). The bond originated from the 2019 restructuring of the defaulted "hidden debt" notes and features an amortising structure with four equal annual principal instalments of US$225m payable in September of 2028, 2029, 2030, and 2031 (Prospectus, Sep-19, High). While coupon service (US$81m annually) has remained current, rating agencies view the 2028 principal cliff as unmanageable without external debt reprofiling, given commercial market exclusion and tight foreign reserve liquidity (Fitch, 24-Jul-26, High). Total external public debt stands at approximately US$10.4bn, of which multilateral and bilateral concessional creditors (chiefly the World Bank, AfDB, and China Exim Bank) represent over 80% of the total (MEF, Jun-26, High).

### 2. Fiscal illiquidity, domestic arrears, and selective default
Fiscal pressures are acute, driven by an unsustainable public sector wage bill resulting from the Single Salary Table (TSU) reform, elevated security spending in Cabo Delgado, and high domestic debt servicing costs (IMF, 26-Sep-26, High). S&P Global maintains Mozambique's local-currency sovereign credit rating at SD (Selective Default) due to chronic delays and arrears on domestic debt securities and treasury bills (S&P, 25-Sep-26, High). Domestic debt stock has expanded to over 30% of GDP, with local bank holding capacity reaching saturation and yields on Treasury bonds exceeding 16% (Banco de Moçambique, 25-Sep-26, High). In addition, verified domestic supplier arrears exceed US$500m (~2.5% of GDP), causing severe liquidity drag on the formal non-resource private sector (MEF, Jun-26, Medium).

### 3. IMF engagement and multilateral financing framework
The previous 36-month Extended Credit Facility (ECF) approved in May 2022 (SDR 340.8m) expired in April 2025 without completion of the fifth and sixth program reviews due to fiscal slippages and reporting irregularities (IMF, 26-Sep-26, High). An IMF staff mission visited Maputo in September 2026 to initiate discussions on a prospective successor arrangement (IMF, 26-Sep-26, High). However, the Fund's updated Debt Sustainability Analysis (DSA) classifies Mozambique in debt distress due to domestic arrears and unsustainable debt trajectories without LNG flows (IMF, Sep-26, High). Securing Executive Board approval will likely require upfront financing assurances, including domestic fiscal retrenchment and comparability-of-treatment restructuring of external liabilities under the G20 Common Framework (Fitch, 24-Jul-26, High).

### 4. Natural gas infrastructure and TotalEnergies LNG restart
Mozambique's medium-term economic transformation hinges on the exploitation of offshore natural gas in the Rovuma Basin (TotalEnergies, 29-Jan-26, High). TotalEnergies formally lifted force majeure on its US$20bn Area 1 Mozambique LNG project (13.1 mtpa capacity) in November 2025, with full onshore construction remobilising at Afungi in January 2026 following improved security conditions provided by Rwandan military forces (TotalEnergies, 29-Jan-26, High). Construction is approximately 40% complete, with commercial export targeted for late 2029 (TotalEnergies, 29-Jan-26, High). Meanwhile, the operational Coral South FLNG project (Eni, Area 4) continues to produce at full capacity (3.4 mtpa), providing steady royalty and tax receipts to the sovereign, though insufficient to resolve sovereign liquidity strains (MEF, Jun-26, High). The ExxonMobil-led Rovuma LNG onshore project (Area 4, 18 mtpa) targets a final investment decision (FID) in late 2026 or 2027 (ExxonMobil, Jun-26, Medium).

### 5. Monetary policy, banking liquidity, and foreign reserves
Banco de Moçambique maintained its benchmark MIMO policy rate at 9.25% in September 2026, holding the rate steady for four consecutive policy sessions following 800 bps of cumulative easing since early 2024 (Banco de Moçambique, 25-Sep-26, High). Annual headline inflation stabilized at 6.5% YoY in August 2026, contained by a stable Metical (MZN/USD ~63.9) and subdued food prices (INE, 10-Sep-26, High). Gross international reserves stood at US$3.9bn in August 2026, providing 6.5 months of import cover excluding megaprojects (Banco de Moçambique, 31-Aug-26, High). However, net usable reserves are significantly lower when accounting for commercial banks' mandatory foreign currency reserve requirements and central bank swap liabilities (Banco de Moçambique, Aug-26, Medium).

### 6. Political consolidation and Cabo Delgado security architecture
Political transition concluded following the October 2024 general elections, with Daniel Chapo of the ruling FRELIMO party inaugurated as President on 15-January-2025 (Constitutional Council, 15-Jan-25, High). The transition was marred by post-election demonstrations and opposition strikes led by Venâncio Mondlane (PODEMOS), highlighting domestic social fragility and public discontent with governance standards (Local Reports, Feb-25, Medium). In the northern province of Cabo Delgado, the security environment has stabilised around key gas production perimeters due to bilateral security deployments by Rwanda and Mozambican armed forces, although sporadic insurgent raids persist in rural districts (Security Assessment, Aug-26, Medium).

### 7. Relative value, bond documentation, and recovery framework
Mozambique's credit ratings remain deeply distressed: Fitch rates Mozambique CC (affirmed 24-Jul-26), S&P rates Mozambique CCC / SD (affirmed 25-Sep-26), and Moody's rates Mozambique Caa3 Stable (affirmed 18-Oct-24) (Rating Agencies, Sep-26, High). The 2031 Eurobond documentation (XS2051203862) includes single-limb aggregated collective action clauses (CACs) under English law, allowing an aggregated restructuring threshold across bond series or single-series voting at 75% (Prospectus, Sep-19, High). Given that the 2031 note is the sole outstanding Eurobond, single-series CAC dynamics will govern any liability management exchange. In a potential restructuring, bondholders would likely face maturity extension into the early 2030s to bridge the gap until Rovuma Basin LNG revenues ramp up, with potential recovery values heavily tied to oil/gas macro fundamentals (Credit Desk Assessment, Oct-26, Medium).
