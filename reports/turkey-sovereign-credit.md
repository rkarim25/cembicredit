## View
- Neutral to modestly constructive on hard-currency Turkey at BB-/Ba3, all Stable. No catalyst for a rating change in the next 90 days. S&P's scheduled review on 16-Oct-26 reportedly left ratings unchanged (Medium).
- This is a liquidity and credibility credit, not a solvency one. Central government debt is 29.5% of GDP and external debt is 31.8% of GDP (HMB, High). The FY26 deficit is about 5.2% of GDP (Medium).
- The external buffer is weaker than in August. Gross reserves incl. gold fell from USD 188.2bn (28-Aug-26) to USD 167.4bn (week to 02-Oct-26) (TCMB, Medium). The 4-quarter current account deficit is USD 38.9bn (IMF BOP, High). Short-term external debt is USD 170.7bn, so gross cover is about 0.98x. Net reserves ex swaps are not in hand.
- Policy is orthodox. The repo rate is 37.00% against CPI of 29.73% (Sep-26), an ex-ante real rate of about 12.5pp. Disinflation is slow, and a 100 bp cut is expected on 22-Oct-26 (Medium). The cushion is being drawn down gradually, not abruptly.
- Biggest risk: reserve depletion combined with a domestic confidence shock from the liquidation of 131 funds (about USD 18.3bn). S&P said on 02-Oct-26 that the fund probe would not pressure the rating (press, Medium).
- Overall confidence: Medium. High on official levels, Low on market pricing (no levels in hand) and on bank exposure to the fund crisis.

## Analysis

### Ratings
- S&P BB-/Stable. It was affirmed on 17-Apr-26 (Hürriyet Daily News, 19-Apr-26, Medium). The 16-Oct-26 scheduled review reportedly produced no change (Turkish press, 17-Oct-26, Medium; primary release not read). The April stable outlook assumed tight monetary and wage policy and no further depletion of FX reserves.
- Fitch BB-/Stable, affirmed 17-Jul-26, with forecasts of 2.8% growth and 29.5% CPI at end-2026 (Fitch via Voice of Emirates, 18-Jul-26, Medium). Fitch flags a possible early election in late 2027.
- Moody's Ba3/Stable. The last action was "Jan-26", and a periodic review without action took place on 24-Jul-26 (Hürriyet Daily News, Jul-26, Medium).
- Possible conflict: a search hit mentions a Fitch outlook change to positive on 23 January with an unconfirmed year, so it is unusable until checked (open item 9).

### External position
- Gross external debt was USD 539.0bn, 31.8% of GDP, at 30-Jun-26 (HMB, High). Short-term external debt by remaining maturity was USD 170.7bn (TCMB, High). GEFR is about USD 208bn (TCMB/HMB, Jun-26, Medium).
- Reserve measures, which must not be mixed:

| Measure | Value | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross incl. gold | USD 188.2bn | 28-Aug-26 | TCMB, Medium |
| Gross incl. gold | USD 174.4bn | 18-Sep-26 | TCMB, Medium |
| Gross incl. gold | USD 167.4bn (FX 60.3bn, gold 107.1bn) | week to 02-Oct-26 | TCMB via Türkiye Today, Medium |
| Gross incl. gold | USD 186.7bn | Aug-26 | IMF, High |
| Net of predetermined 1y drains | USD 137.1bn | Aug-26 | IMF template, High |

- Cover (my arithmetic, Medium): USD 167.4bn is about 0.98x short-term debt (USD 170.7bn) and about 0.80x GEFR (USD 208bn). The data record's 119% GEFR/reserves ratio is the inverse and uses the 18-Sep-26 level.
- Reserves are 12-month lower by 7.8% at Sep-26 (IMF, High). Part of the Aug-Sep fall reflects gold valuation (gold USD 117.1bn in Aug-26 to USD 107.1bn in Sep-26). The FX component also fell, from USD 69.6bn to USD 59.0bn (IMF, High). The split between intervention, swaps and valuation is unknown.
- The current account was USD -23.6bn in 2026-Q1 and USD -10.9bn in 2026-Q2. The 4-quarter total is USD -38.9bn (IMF BOP, High). TCMB projects 2.6% to 2.8% of GDP for 2026 (TCMB, Jul-26, Medium). FDI liabilities were USD 1.2-1.3bn a quarter in 2026 and 0.81% of GDP in 2025 (IMF, World Bank, High), so financing leans on portfolio flows and bank rollovers (my inference, Medium).
- The lira moved from 44.80 (Apr-26) to 48.65 (Sep-26), about 8.6% depreciation against CPI near 30% (BIS, High). The BIS broad REER is 108.09 (Aug-26) with a 10-year z-score of +0.31 (BIS, High). It is not stretched against its own history.

### Monetary policy
- CPI was 29.73% y/y (+1.84% m/m) and core 28.70% in Sep-26 (TurkStat, 03-Oct-26, High). The repo rate was 37.00%, held for the fifth time (TCMB, Sep-26, High; Intellinews, Sep-26, Medium). The IMF series shows 35.5%, an unresolved definitional gap (open item 6).
- Expectations: 12-month 24.5% in the Sep-26 survey (TCMB, Medium), the real rate 12.5pp. The October survey shows 23.89% (Hürriyet Daily News, Medium). The TCMB raised its end-2026 inflation forecast to 28% from 26% (Intellinews, Sep-26, Medium).
- The 22-Oct-26 and 10-Dec-26 MPCs are the last of 2026. A 100 bp cut at each is expected (Intellinews, Medium; open item 7). Cuts of this size leave the real rate above 11pp. The policy risk is a larger or faster easing while reserves are falling.

### Fiscal
- Central government debt was 29.5% of GDP at Q2-26 (HMB, High). The FY26 deficit is tracking about 5.2% of GDP (HMB, Aug-26, Medium; "approximate"). The stock is low and the flow is wide, so debt rises on current settings.
- Primary balance, interest-to-revenue and the debt-stabilising balance are not computable from the sources in hand (open item 12).
- Market access: EUR 2bn 8-year Eurobond due Mar-34 at a 5.15% coupon, EUR 6.5bn orders (AGBI, Feb-26, Medium). Pricing after the September turmoil is not sourced.

### Banking and the fund liquidation
- Deposit-taker FSIs, Q4-25 to Q1-26 (IMF, High): CAR 19.69% to 16.52%, Tier 1 16.89% to 14.04%, NPL 2.30% to 2.48%, provisions/NPL 74.77% to 75.75%. The one-quarter fall in CAR is unexplained (open item 11).
- SPK ordered 131 funds (about USD 18.3bn, TL890bn, 7.7% of fund portfolio value) liquidated on 17-18 Sep-26. 17 funds settled on 08-Oct-26, with payments to about 44,000 investors. Payouts are capped at TL1m per investor, and Is Bankasi and Ziraat are named as liquidators for some funds (Daily Sabah, 12-Oct-26, Medium).
- Borsa Istanbul had its worst month since 2008 in Sep-26 (Daily Sabah, Oct-26, Medium). S&P said on 02-Oct-26 the investigation would not pressure the rating (Turkish press, 19-Oct-26, Medium).
- Judgement: this is a domestic confidence and banking-channel risk. It becomes a sovereign event only if state banks fund payouts at scale, deposit dollarisation rises, or fund redemptions convert to FX demand (Low, since bank exposure is unknown).
