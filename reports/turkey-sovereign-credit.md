## Analysis

### External debt and rollover requirements
- Turkey's gross external debt stock stood at $539.0B (31.8% of GDP) as of 30-Jun-26 (Ministry of Treasury and Finance / HMB, 30-Jun-26, High).
- Short-term external debt by remaining maturity across all sectors totalled $170.7B as of 30-Jun-26 (TCMB, 30-Jun-26, High).
- The private banking sector accounts for approximately 52% of short-term external debt rollover requirements, maintaining external debt rollover ratios above 100% year-to-date (TCMB, Jun-26, High).
- Non-financial corporates hold $48.2B in short-term external obligations, largely mitigated by operational FX export revenues and domestic FX liquidity (TCMB, Jun-26, Medium).

### Foreign exchange reserves and external financing
- Gross foreign exchange reserves were reported at $61.56B as of 25-Sep-26 (TCMB, 25-Sep-26, High).
- Gross reserves provide 0.36x coverage of short-term external debt by remaining maturity, well below the Greenspan-Guidotti rule benchmark of 1.0x (TCMB, 25-Sep-26, High).
- The current account balance recorded a slight surplus of $36M in July 2026, driven by summer tourism receipts and compression of non-energy imports (TCMB, Jul-26, High).
- Full-year FY26 current account deficit is projected to settle between 2.6% and 2.8% of GDP ($36B to $39B), down from over 4.0% in FY23 (HMB / IMF, Aug-26, Medium).
- Gross external financing requirement (GEFR) for the next 12 months equals approximately $208B (amortisations plus current account deficit), requiring continued market access and rollover by commercial borrowers (TCMB / HMB, Jun-26, High).

### Monetary policy and inflation trajectory
- TurkStat reported headline CPI slowed to 29.73% YoY in September 2026, down from 31.51% YoY in August 2026, with monthly inflation printing at +1.84% MoM (TurkStat, 03-Oct-26, High).
- Core inflation (CPI-C) registered 28.70% YoY (+2.10% MoM), reflecting continued stickiness in services and shelter (TurkStat, 03-Oct-26, High).
- TCMB maintained its benchmark 1-week repo auction rate at 37.00% at its September 2026 Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) meeting (TCMB, Sep-26, High).
- Ex-ante real policy rate against 12-month forward inflation expectations (tracking ~24-25%) remains positive at over +700 bps, preserving real returns for local deposit holders and foreign portfolio investors (TCMB / Survey of Market Participants, Sep-26, High).
- Next scheduled MPC meeting is set for 22-Oct-26, with market consensus divided between a pause and an initial 100 bps easing step (TCMB calendar, Sep-26, Medium).

### Fiscal dynamics and sovereign debt profile
- Central government gross debt stock stood at 29.5% of GDP as of Q2 2026, representing a relatively low debt-to-GDP burden compared to emerging market peers (HMB, 30-Jun-26, High).
- The central government budget deficit is running at approximately 5.2% of GDP for FY26, driven by capital expenditure for southeastern earthquake zone reconstruction and indexation of public sector wages (HMB, Aug-26, Medium).
- Domestic debt stock composition is approximately 62% fixed-rate TRY, 23% CPI-indexed TRY, and 15% FX-denominated/linked debt, reducing direct FX vulnerability on domestic liabilities compared to the 2018-2021 period (HMB Public Debt Management Report, Jun-26, High).
- The debt-stabilising primary balance is estimated at approximately -1.2% of GDP, meaning current fiscal deficits require continued consolidation once reconstruction spending winds down (IMF Article IV Consultation, Jun-26, Medium).

### Sovereign credit ratings and market access
- Fitch affirmed Turkey at BB- with a Stable outlook on 17-Jul-26, citing orthodox macroeconomic policy execution balanced against low reserve coverage of external debt (Fitch Ratings, 17-Jul-26, High).
- S&P affirmed Turkey at BB- with a Stable outlook on 17-Apr-26 (S&P Global Ratings, 17-Apr-26, High).
- Moody's affirmed Turkey at Ba3 with a Stable outlook in January 2026 (Moody's Investors Service, Jan-26, High).
- The Republic of Turkey maintains unimpeded access to international capital markets, having issued $10.0B in Eurobonds and sukuk year-to-date across 5Y, 8Y, and 10Y tenors (HMB Borrowing Program, Sep-26, High).
