## View
- Confidence is Medium. The 3Q26 growth print is now confirmed from an NBS relay. Trade, commodity and creditor data are still secondary press or vendor relays, and the creditor flow data are 2025 vintage.
- China is growing at about 4.5% on exports and industry, not domestic demand. 3Q26 GDP was +4.5% y/y and January–September +4.6%. Retail sales were +1.1% y/y and FAI -7.2% year-to-date.
- The IMF forecasts 4.5% for 2026 and 4.0% for 2027 (14-Oct-26).
- September exports were +36.3% y/y and the surplus US$133.5bn (US$938bn for January–September). This is a competition and disinflation shock for EM manufacturers and raises the odds of a tariff response.
- Commodity pull is mixed and weaker than import values suggest. Iron ore was -5.6% y/y in September, though January–September was +4.1%. Copper cathode imports were -23.8% y/y in September and -16.3% year-to-date. Cathode is the weakest series found, but the form of copper imports is shifting (see Commodity demand).
- As creditor, China is a net collector from developing countries. New engagement is announcement-led (Africa BRI, RMB credit lines) and concentrated by country. Announcements are not disbursements.
- China's own sovereign ratings are stable at all three agencies and are not an EM stress source.
- Biggest risk: a trade-measure response to the export surge, or a property-driven import slump hitting metals exporters (Chile, Peru, Zambia, Indonesia).

## Analysis

### Growth
- 3Q26 GDP was +4.5% y/y and January–September +4.6%, against 4.4% consensus (NBS via Mysteel, 19-Oct-26, Medium: tier 2 data via vendor relay). H1-26 was +4.7%, so the 3Q print is below the H1 average (NBS via SMM, 24-Jul-26, Medium).
- September retail sales were +1.1% y/y and industrial output +5.5% (Seoul Economic Daily, Outlook India, SBS, 19-Oct-26, Medium).
- FAI was -7.2% January–September, property investment is still falling and CPI is about +0.8% (press summaries of NBS data via FreePressJournal, 19-Oct-26, Medium: wording on CPI is loose).
- The IMF projects 4.5% for 2026 and 4.0% for 2027, citing energy prices linked to the Iran war and weak domestic demand (IMF WEO via The Star, 14-Oct-26, Medium). Fitch's 2026 forecast is 4.6% (rating-agency summary, Aug/Sep-26, Medium).
- A 4.4% a year average for 2026–30 implied by the 15th Five-Year Plan was reported in a source dated 28-Oct-25. It was not checked against the NPC text and is not relied on (Low).
- No PBoC or MoF release was read. The policy-rate level and any stimulus size are unconfirmed.

### Trade and competition
- September exports were US$447.18bn (+36.3% y/y), imports US$313.66bn (+31%), and the surplus US$133.53bn. The January–September surplus was US$938.11bn (GAC via Trading Economics and political.org, 13-Oct-26, Medium).
- Trading Economics links the import jump to commodity and AI-related demand. Growth rates of this size may be partly price-driven, and no GAC volume index was read (Low-Medium).
- The surge is a disinflation and REER channel for EM manufacturers rather than a demand pull. It raises the probability of US and EU trade measures, but none was found in the last 90 days (Low-Medium).
- The view would change on new tariff or quota action, a sharp reversal of the monthly surplus, or material RMB appreciation.

### Commodity demand
- Iron ore imports were 109.8mt in September (-5.6% y/y), and January–September was +4.1% (SteelOrbis, Oct-26, Medium). Another source shows 110.54mt, so the level is not fully reconciled but the direction is the same.
- Copper cathode imports were 254.6kt in September (-23.84% y/y) and 2.1383mt January–September (-16.34% y/y). Cathode exports were up 81% y/y and anode imports up 58% y/y, which suggests a shift in the form of copper imports (SMM, Oct-26, Medium).
- Other press shows total copper imports of 722,450t in September against 668,486t in August (Low-Medium). The series definition (unwrought, semis, concentrate) and the y/y base are unconfirmed. The cathode series and the total series cannot be reconciled from the data held.
- Earlier 2026 data are soft: August copper about -10% y/y and crude +6.2% m/m (Trading Economics, 08-Sep-26, Medium). January–May crude imports were -4.8% y/y (GAC via SMM, Medium).
- Volume direction is mixed by commodity. Commodity prices were not retrieved, so import value cannot be separated from price.

### China as a creditor
- The poorest 75 countries faced record repayments of about US$22bn to China in 2025 (Lowy Institute, May-25, Medium).
- Net transfers from developing countries to China turned negative in 2022–23, at about US$3.9bn a year (Boston University GDP Center via Reuters, 2025, Medium). No 2026 update was found.
- Africa BRI announcements were US$33.5bn in 1H26 (+254% y/y), with Ethiopia (US$14.8bn) and Egypt (US$12.2bn) over 80% of the total (Green Finance & Development Center via Africa.com/Stratfor, 28-Jul-26, Low-Medium). About US$10bn of RMB credit lines were pledged to Latin America at the China-CELAC meeting (ProActivo, 2026, Low-Medium). Neither is a disbursement.
- An SCMP headline reports Chinese lending to Africa nearly halved in a year. It was not read in full and conflicts with the announcement data (Low).
- Zambia's creditor committee deal covers US$6.3bn over 20 years, and took two years from application (Cleary, Atlantic Council, GlobalCapital search extracts, undated, Low-Medium). It shows that relief is slow and tied to multilateral burden-sharing.
- Inference: China is a net collector from the poorest borrowers, and new engagement is investment- and RMB-led. This lowers direct sovereign exposure but concentrates it by country. Disbursement data would confirm or reverse this.
- In Common Framework cases, China has preferred maturity extension and interest relief to principal write-downs (Zambia, Sri Lanka, Ghana pattern; no primary source retrieved, Medium, carried forward).

### China sovereign credit
- Moody's A1, outlook moved from Negative to Stable on 27-Apr-26. S&P A+ stable, affirmed Aug-26. Fitch A stable (Hellenic Shipping News and iTiger, 2026, Medium: secondary press, agency pages not read).

### Exporter exposure
- Zambia, Chile, Peru, Indonesia and Angola are most exposed to a slowdown in Chinese bulk and copper demand (inference, Low-Medium). Weak cathode and iron ore volumes support this, but price confirmation is missing.
- Ethiopia and Egypt are the main recipients of announced Chinese investment (Low-Medium).

- Stimulus or property-policy announcements in the last 90 days.
- 2026 disbursement and repayment data for developing countries.

- Tariff and trade-truce status.
- The October 2026 trade release was not out at the cut-off.

## Where the models disagreed
There was one position and no rebuttals, so no disputes arose. Two minor reconciliation gaps (iron ore tonnage 109.8mt vs 110.54mt, and copper cathode vs total copper imports) are recorded in the analysis and not resolved. Neither changes the direction of the conclusion.
