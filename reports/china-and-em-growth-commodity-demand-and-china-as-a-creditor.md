## View
- Confidence is Medium-Low. Growth and trade figures are dated but come from wire and aggregator press, not the NBS or GAC pages. Creditor flow data are 2025 vintage.
- China is growing on exports, not domestic demand. 3Q26 GDP was +4.5% y/y against 4.3% in 2Q and 4.4% expected. September retail sales were only +1.1% y/y and industrial output +5.5% (Seoul Economic Daily, 19-Oct-26).
- The IMF cut its China forecast to 4.5% for 2026 and 4.0% for 2027, citing weak domestic demand and energy prices (14-Oct-26, via The Star).
- September exports were +36.3% y/y and imports +31%. The surplus was US$133.5bn and the nine-month surplus US$938bn. This is a competition and disinflation shock for EM manufacturers and raises the risk of a tariff response.
- Commodity pull is weaker than import values suggest. September iron ore imports were -5.6% y/y, although January–September was +4.1%. Copper volumes are mixed.
- As creditor, China remains a net collector from developing countries. New engagement is shifting to announced investment and RMB credit lines, concentrated by country. Announcements are not disbursements.
- China's own sovereign rating is stable at all three agencies. It is not a stress source for EM.
- Biggest risk: a disorderly trade response to the export surge, or a property-driven import slump hitting metals exporters.

## What changed
- The 3Q26 GDP release came in at +4.5% y/y, above consensus and above 2Q. The deceleration call is softened to "low-impulse, export-led". The earlier trigger (a print at or above 4.8% with firm retail sales) was not met, because retail sales were +1.1%.
- September trade data replaced August data. The surplus widened from US$119.1bn to US$133.5bn, and exports accelerated from +25.0% to +36.3% y/y. Trade friction is now an explicit risk.
- The IMF WEO cut (14-Oct-26) and sovereign ratings (Moody's moved to Stable on 27-Apr-26) were added.
- The Iran war is now identified from the IMF as an energy-price factor. The earlier open question on what the "war" was is closed. Its effect on Chinese demand remains open.
- New creditor evidence added on Africa BRI announcements and Latin America RMB credit lines. The core net-collector finding is unchanged but remains 2025 vintage.
- Open items 1 and 8 of the prior version are closed or narrowed. Items 2–7, 9–11 are carried and sharpened. A new item asks about the portfolio's China exposure mix.

## Analysis

### Growth
- 3Q26 GDP was +4.5% y/y, against 4.3% in 2Q26 and 4.4% consensus. September industrial output was +5.5% and retail sales +1.1% y/y (Seoul Economic Daily, Outlook India, SBS, 19-Oct-26, Medium: wire-based press, NBS not read).
- H1-26 GDP was +4.7% y/y (NBS via SMM, 24-Jul-26, Medium). The 3Q print is below the H1 average. The "beat" is against a lowered consensus.
- The IMF projects 4.5% for 2026 and 4.0% for 2027, with energy prices linked to the Iran war and weak domestic demand as drags (IMF WEO, 14-Oct-26, via The Star, Medium). Fitch's 2026 forecast is 4.6% (rating-agency summary, Aug/Sep-26, Medium).
- Property investment, sales and starts, and any PBoC or MoF stimulus in the last 90 days, were not retrieved. The domestic-demand conclusion rests on retail sales alone.

### Trade and competition
- September exports were US$447.18bn (+36.3% y/y). Imports were a record US$313.66bn (+31%). The surplus was US$133.53bn and the January–September surplus US$938.11bn, on track above US$1tn (GAC via political.org, 13-Oct-26; Trading Economics, Oct-26, Medium).
- The surge is a disinflation and REER channel for EM manufacturers, not demand pull. It raises the probability of US and EU trade measures, but none was found in the last 90 days (Low-Medium).
- The view would change on new tariff or quota action, a sharp monthly reversal of the surplus, or material RMB appreciation.

### Commodity demand
- Import values overstate physical demand. September iron ore imports were 109.8mt, -5.6% y/y, while January–September was +4.1% (SteelOrbis, Oct-26, Medium).
- Copper imports were 722,450t in September against 668,486t in August (press, Oct-26, Low-Medium). The series definition (unwrought, semis, concentrate) and the y/y base were not confirmed.
- Earlier 2026 data remain soft: August copper about -10% y/y and crude +6.2% m/m (Trading Economics, 08-Sep-26, Medium). January–May crude imports were -4.8% y/y (GAC via SMM, Medium).
- Sources conflict on units and base periods. Volume direction by commodity is unresolved until GAC tables are read.

### China as a creditor
- The poorest 75 countries faced record repayments of about US$22bn to China in 2025 (Lowy Institute, May-25, Medium).
- Net transfers turned negative in 2022–23, with about US$3.9bn a year more repaid than lent (Boston University GDP Center via Reuters, 2025, Medium). No 2026 update was found.
- Africa BRI announcements were US$33.5bn in 1H26 (+254% y/y), with Ethiopia (US$14.8bn) and Egypt (US$12.2bn) over 80% of the total (Green Finance & Development Center via Africa.com/Stratfor, 28-Jul-26, Low-Medium). Announcements are not disbursements.
- About US$10bn of RMB credit lines were pledged to Latin America at the China-CELAC meeting (ProActivo, 2026, Low-Medium). Terms and drawdown were not read.
- An SCMP headline reports Chinese lending to Africa nearly halved in a year. It was not read in full and conflicts with the announcement data (Low).
- Inference: China is a net collector from the poorest borrowers and new engagement is investment- and RMB-led. This lowers direct sovereign exposure but concentrates it by country. Disbursement data would reverse or confirm this.
- In Common Framework cases China prefers maturity extension and interest relief to principal write-downs (Zambia, Sri Lanka, Ghana pattern; no primary source retrieved, Medium, carried forward).

### China sovereign credit
- Moody's A1, outlook moved from Negative to Stable on 27-Apr-26. S&P A+ stable, affirmed Aug-26, expecting growth of 4% or more over one to two years. Fitch A stable (Hellenic Shipping News and iTiger, 2026, Medium: secondary press).

### Exporter exposure
- Zambia, Chile, Peru, Indonesia and Angola are most exposed to a slowdown in Chinese bulk demand (inference, Low-Medium). Iron ore volumes are weak, copper is mixed, and price confirmation is missing.
- Ethiopia and Egypt are the main recipients of announced Chinese investment (Low-Medium).

### Data gaps
- NBS and GAC primary pages were not read.
- 3Q26 q/q GDP, property data, and commodity price levels.
- Chinese stimulus or property-policy announcements in the last 90 days.
- 2026 disbursement and repayment data for developing countries.
