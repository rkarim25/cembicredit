## View
- Confidence is Medium. Official data are read through vendor and press relays, and no NBS, GAC or PBoC primary page was read in full. Creditor flow data are 2025 vintage.
- China is growing at about 4.5% on exports and industry, not domestic demand. 3Q26 GDP was +4.5% y/y and January–September +4.6%. Retail sales were +1.1% y/y and FAI -7.2% year-to-date.
- The October official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.3 from 50.1, against 50.0 expected. This is the first fresh datapoint since the last run and points to softer momentum in 4Q.
- September exports were +36.3% y/y and the surplus US$133.5bn. This is a competition and disinflation shock for EM manufacturers, and it raises the odds of trade measures.
- Commodity pull is weak in volume. Iron ore imports were -5.6% y/y and copper cathode imports -23.8% y/y in September. The form of copper imports is shifting, so cathode overstates the weakness.
- As creditor, China is a net collector from developing countries. Restructuring is moving to yuan conversion with longer tenors and lower margins (Kenya, Ethiopia talks), not principal write-downs.
- China's sovereign ratings are stable at all three agencies and are not an EM stress source.
- Biggest risk: a trade-measure response to the export surge, or a property-driven import slump hitting metals exporters (Chile, Peru, Zambia, Indonesia).

## Analysis

### Growth
- 3Q26 GDP was +4.5% y/y and January–September +4.6%, against 4.4% consensus (NBS via Mysteel, 19-Oct-26, Medium: tier 2 data via vendor relay). H1-26 was +4.7%, so 3Q is below the H1 average (NBS via SMM, 24-Jul-26, Medium).
- The October official manufacturing PMI was 49.3 against 50.1 in September and 50.0 expected, the lowest since July (Trading Economics, 31-Oct-26, Medium: tier 2 data via tier 3 relay; Mysteel carries a headline on the same release). The September release is on the NBS site (NBS, 8-Oct-26, High for the September release only).
- September retail sales were +1.1% y/y and industrial output +5.5% (Seoul Economic Daily, Outlook India, SBS, 19-Oct-26, Medium).
- FAI was -7.2% January–September and CPI about +0.8% (press summaries of NBS data via FreePressJournal, 19-Oct-26, Medium: wording on CPI is loose).
- The IMF projects 4.5% for 2026 and 4.0% for 2027, citing energy prices linked to the Iran war and weak domestic demand (IMF WEO via The Star, 14-Oct-26, Medium).
- Policy support is described as incremental. The Politburo made domestic demand the top 2026 priority with a measured approach (Business Standard and Caixin, 8-Dec-25, Medium, dated). The 15th Five-Year Plan ranks property last among eight 2026 priorities (Caixin, 16-Mar-26, Medium, dated). No fresh stimulus announcement was found.

### Trade and competition
- September exports were US$447.18bn (+36.3% y/y), imports US$313.66bn (+31%) and the surplus US$133.53bn. The January–September surplus was US$938.11bn (GAC via Trading Economics and political.org, 13-Oct-26, Medium).
- The 2025 surplus was a record of about US$1.19tn (GAC via Asia Pacific Foundation, 2026, Medium).
- The import jump may be partly price-driven, and no GAC volume index was read. Growth of this size should be checked against GAC directly (Low-Medium).
- Search results referred to an EU-China arrangement on hybrid-vehicle exports and EU measures on chemicals and machine tools. The dates could not be confirmed and these items are not relied on (Low).
- The view would change on new tariff or quota action, a sharp reversal of the monthly surplus, or material RMB appreciation.

### Commodity demand
- Iron ore imports were 109.8mt in September (-5.6% y/y), and January–September was +4.1% (SteelOrbis, Oct-26, Medium). Another source shows 110.54mt, so the level is not fully reconciled but the direction is the same.
- Copper cathode imports were 254.6kt in September (-23.84% y/y) and 2.1383mt January–September (-16.34% y/y). Cathode exports were up 81% and anode imports up 58%, which suggests a shift in the form of copper imports (SMM, Oct-26, Medium).
- Other press shows total copper imports of 722,450t in September against 668,486t in August (Low-Medium). The series definition and the y/y base are unconfirmed, so it cannot be reconciled with the cathode series.
- Earlier 2026 data are soft: August copper about -10% y/y and crude +6.2% m/m (Trading Economics, 08-Sep-26, Medium). January–May crude imports were -4.8% y/y (GAC via SMM, Medium).
- Commodity prices were not retrieved, so import value cannot be separated from price.

### China as a creditor
- The poorest 75 countries faced record repayments of about US$22bn to China in 2025 (Lowy Institute, May-25, Medium).
- Net transfers from developing countries to China turned negative in 2022–23, at about US$3.9bn a year (Boston University GDP Center via Reuters, 2025, Medium). No 2026 update was found.
- Kenya converted three Chinese dollar loans into renminbi in October 2025. The saving of about US$215m a year in debt service came mainly from longer tenors, grace periods and lower margins, so AidData calls it a restructuring in disguise (Kenyans.co.ke, Nation Africa and AidData, 2025–26, Medium: dates from search summaries).
- Ethiopia is negotiating conversion of part of its US$5.38bn Chinese debt into yuan, including US$2.6bn of China Exim railway loans. Ethiopia, Mozambique, Zambia, Pakistan and Indonesia were named as possible followers (Africa.com, Kenyans.co.ke and Pakistan Today, Jun–Aug-26, Low-Medium: talks, not agreements). The IMF has warned Kenya and Ethiopia on yuan swaps (People's Daily Digital, 2026, Low).
- Africa BRI announcements were US$33.5bn in 1H26 (+254% y/y), with Ethiopia (US$14.8bn) and Egypt (US$12.2bn) over 80% of the total (Green Finance & Development Center via Africa.com/Stratfor, 28-Jul-26, Low-Medium). About US$10bn of RMB credit lines were pledged to Latin America at the China-CELAC meeting (ProActivo, 2026, Low-Medium). Neither is a disbursement.
- Zambia signed deals with Chinese lenders including China Exim in October 2025 (Bloomberg, 13-Oct-25, headline only, Medium). The creditor committee deal covers US$6.3bn over 20 years (Cleary, Atlantic Council, undated, Low-Medium).
- Inference: China is a net collector from the poorest borrowers. New engagement is investment- and RMB-led, and relief comes as tenor and currency terms rather than principal cuts. Disbursement data would confirm or reverse this.

### China sovereign credit
- Moody's A1, outlook moved from Negative to Stable on 27-Apr-26. S&P A+ stable, affirmed Aug-26. Fitch A stable (Hellenic Shipping News and iTiger, 2026, Medium: secondary press, agency pages not read).

### Exporter exposure
- Zambia, Chile, Peru, Indonesia and Angola are most exposed to a slowdown in Chinese bulk and copper demand (inference, Low-Medium). Weak cathode and iron ore volumes and the soft PMI support this, but price confirmation is missing.
- Manufacturing exporters (Mexico, Turkey, Brazil, Eastern Europe, ASEAN) face the competition channel (inference, Low-Medium).
- Ethiopia and Egypt are the main recipients of announced Chinese investment (Low-Medium).
