## View
- Neutral. External liquidity is the strength. Debt affordability and the missing IMF anchor are the weakness. Market levels are absent, so there is no relative-value call (Medium).
- Ratings are low single-B and stable: S&P B, Fitch B-, Moody's B3 (upgraded from Caa1 on 27-Jan-26). Levels agree across tier 3 press. Action dates are not established and no agency text was seen (Medium on levels, Low on dates).
- Gross reserves are US$14.702B (CBK, 07-Oct-26), about 4.6 months of imports. The KES is flat near 129.4/USD. Net usable reserves are unknown (High on gross, Low on net).
- The CBR was held at 8.75% on 07-Oct-26, the fourth consecutive hold. CPI rose from 5.59% in Apr-26 to 6.76% in Sep-26, which limits scope to ease (CBK, KNBS/IMF, High).
- Debt is 68.5% of GDP against the 55% anchor. Interest takes about 33% of ordinary revenue, the zone where the framework says defaults cluster. The FY2025/26 deficit of 6.4% of GDP is still a projection (National Treasury, Medium).
- No IMF programme since the Apr-25 expiry. An Article IV mission is expected in early Dec-26, and press reports IMF reluctance to agree a programme before the 09-Aug-27 election (Medium).
- Repeated Eurobond-funded buybacks have reduced the 2028 bullet. Whether the 2032 bullet is also cleared is not established. They support access but add to the gross stack at higher cost (Medium).
- Biggest risk: fiscal slippage into the 2027 election with no Fund anchor, forcing costlier financing.
- Confidence: High on reserve, rate and CPI data. Medium on solvency ratios, ratings, the IMF outlook and liability management. Low on market pricing, which is absent.

## Analysis

### Ratings
- Levels: S&P B, Fitch B-, Moody's B3, all stable (CNBC Africa, 27-Jan-26; BusinessDay Nigeria, Jan-26; Ecofin, Aug-25; repo summary, Aug-26, Medium).
- Moody's upgraded to B3/Stable from Caa1 on 27-Jan-26, citing reserves and external liquidity. Weak debt affordability and slow consolidation cap the rating (CNBC Africa, 27-Jan-26; AllAfrica, 28-Jan-26, Medium).
- Fitch affirmed B-/Stable on 23-Jan-26 (BusinessDay Nigeria; Kenyan Wall Street, Jan-26, Medium).
- S&P upgraded to B on 22-Aug-25 on the debt buyback (Ecofin, Aug-25, Medium). A later retention at B/Stable warns of rising debt costs and a possible 7.1% deficit in FY2026/27 (Kenyans.co.ke, undated, Low on date).
- The repo dates (Fitch 17-Jul-26, S&P 21-Aug-26, Moody's Jul-26) are uncorroborated. Judgement: upgrade momentum looks largely exhausted and near-term rating risk is balanced (Low).

### External position
- CBK gross reserves are US$14.702B at 07-Oct-26 (CBK, High). IMF International Liquidity shows US$13.074B (May-26), US$13.930B (Jun-26), US$15.361B (Jul-26), up 34.7% over twelve months. Gold is negligible (IMF, High).
- The two series differ in date and possibly definition. Net reserves after swaps, forwards and short-term liabilities are unknown (open item 5).
- Import cover is 4.63 months (World Bank WDI, 2025, High). The current account is -4.1% of GDP and net FDI 0.8% of GDP (IMF WEO 2026E; WDI 2025, Medium). Nominal GDP is US$147.3B (IMF WEO, Medium).
- The KES traded between 129.35 and 129.48 per USD from Mar-26 to Aug-26 (BIS, High).
- The commodity terms-of-trade index is down 2.9% over twelve months to May-26 (IMF CTOT, Medium).
- Position-stated triggers to watch: reserves below about US$12bn or cover below 4 months without official inflows.

### Fiscal and financing
- Public debt is Ksh13.01tn, 68.5% of GDP, at Jun-26, against the 55% anchor (National Treasury, High).
- The FY2025/26 deficit is projected at Ksh1.199tn (6.4% of GDP). The year closed in Jun-26 but no audited outturn is in hand (National Treasury, Medium).
- Interest is about 33% of ordinary revenue, approximate (National Treasury / World Bank, Medium).
- FY2026/27 budget: Ksh4.82tn spending, Ksh1.16tn deficit (Kenyans.co.ke; the-star.co.ke, Jun-Jul-26, Medium).
- Eurobond and liability management, all tier 3:
  - Oct-25: US$1.5bn raised. Ksh86.4bn funded a buyback of US$1bn of the 2028s and Ksh103.1bn funded budget support (People Daily, Medium).
  - Feb-26: a Ksh64.5bn buyback began on 19-Feb-26 (HapaKenya, Medium), followed by a US$2.25bn dual-tranche issue (BusinessDay Nigeria; CNBC Africa, Feb-26, Medium).
  - A further buyback of up to US$500m is planned (The Star, 15-Jul-26, Medium). Overlap with the Feb-26 operation is unresolved (open item 9).
  - A US$815m Eurobond is planned by end-Dec-26 (Bloomberg headline, 14-Aug-26, paywalled, content not read, Low).
  - An audit report flags losses on the buyback deal (People Daily, undated, Low).
- The domestic/external split, average maturity, gross financing need, the ladder after the buybacks and contingent liabilities are not sourced.

### Monetary
- The CBR was held at 8.75% on 07-Oct-26, the fourth consecutive hold, citing energy prices linked to the Middle East conflict (CBK MPC press release, 07-Oct-26, High).
- CPI y/y: Apr-26 5.59%, May 6.68%, Jun 6.41%, Jul 6.49%, Aug 6.59%, Sep 6.76% (IMF data portal; KNBS 6.8%, High).
- The headline real rate is about +2.0pp on trailing CPI (arithmetic, High). A true ex-ante rate needs expectations data (open item 6).

### IMF and politics
- The previous EFF/ECF/RSF expired uncompleted in Apr-25 (IMF, Sep-26, Medium).
- Kenya requested a new programme in Mar-25. Missions in Sep-Oct-25 and Feb-Mar-26 ended without staff-level agreement (Times Live, 26-Feb-26; Kenyan Wall Street, Oct-26, Medium).
- An Article IV mission is expected in early Dec-26. The IMF is reportedly reluctant to agree a programme before the 09-Aug-27 election (Kenyan Wall Street, Oct-26; Kenya Times, Oct-26, Medium).
- Judgement: the election limits consolidation in H2 FY2026/27 (Low-Medium). Protest and security risk was not researched.

### Banking system
- IMF FSI, deposit takers, Q3-25 (about a year old, Medium):
  - CAR 19.97%, Tier 1 17.65%.
  - NPL 13.54%, down from 14.2% in Q2-25.
  - Provisions/NPL 51.97%.
  - ROE 27.2%.
  - FX open position -0.82% of capital.
- Sovereign exposure as a share of bank assets is not sourced. Judgement: the sector is a contained contingent liability, but the doom-loop channel cannot be assessed without that figure.

### Market
- No Bloomberg levels are available. No relative-value conclusion is possible beyond the neutral stance.
