## View
- Kenya's sovereign credit trajectory has achieved near-term stabilization in the low single-B tier following successful Eurobond liability management and external reserve accumulation, but medium-term solvency remains constrained by heavy domestic debt service (S&P / Fitch, Aug-26, High).
- Sovereign ratings are aligned across the low single-B bracket: S&P at B (Stable, 21-Aug-26), Fitch at B- (Stable, 17-Jul-26), and Moody's at B3 (Stable, Jul-26) (Rating Agencies, Aug-26, High).
- External liquidity risk has materially receded after the February 2026 US$2.25B dual-tranche amortising Eurobond placement lifted CBK gross FX reserves to US$14.702B in October 2026 (Central Bank of Kenya, 07-Oct-26, High).
- Monetary policy remains orthodox, with the CBK holding the Central Bank Rate at 8.75% on 07-Oct-26, maintaining a positive ex-ante real policy rate of +195 bps over September 2026 inflation of 6.8% YoY (CBK / KNBS, Oct-26, High).
- However, public debt stands at Ksh 13.01 trillion (68.5% of GDP) as of June 2026, well above the statutory 55% anchor, with rigid interest payments absorbing ~33% of ordinary revenue (National Treasury, Jun-26, High).
- Following the expiration of the previous IMF EFF/ECF arrangement in April 2025, Kenya operates without a formal Fund financing umbrella, leaving fiscal consolidation vulnerable to pre-election political friction (IMF, Sep-26, High).
- The FY2025/26 fiscal deficit is projected at Ksh 1.199 trillion (6.4% of GDP), underscoring the urgency of finalizing a successor IMF programme to anchor structural tax reform (National Treasury, Jul-26, High).
- Primary risks: domestic political resistance to revenue measures ahead of the 2027 general elections, fiscal slippage widening gross financing needs, and vulnerability to global risk-off refinancing shocks.
- Overall confidence: High on macroeconomic stabilization, reserve accumulation, and CBK monetary discipline; Medium on true usable net reserves ex-swaps; Low on secondary Eurobond yields until verified via Bloomberg.

## Analysis

### Sovereign credit ratings and external liability management
- Kenya's sovereign credit ratings have stabilized across the low single-B bracket following successful external commercial liability management operations that eliminated near-term default risk (S&P Global Ratings, 21-Aug-26, High).
- S&P Global Ratings affirmed Kenya at B/B with a Stable outlook on 21-Aug-26, citing improved external liquidity and multilateral support despite high interest burdens and political constraints on revenue generation (S&P Global Ratings, 21-Aug-26, High).
- Fitch Ratings affirmed Kenya at B- with a Stable outlook on 17-Jul-26, pointing to reduced near-term refinancing pressures following the February 2026 Eurobond issuance while highlighting structurally weak revenue collection (Fitch Ratings, 17-Jul-26, High).
- Moody's Investors Service maintains a B3 rating with a Stable outlook (affirmed July 2026, upgraded from Caa1 in January 2026), reflecting receding near-term liquidity stress balanced against severe fiscal rigidities (Moody's Investors Service, Jul-26, High).

### External liquidity, reserve cover, and balance of payments
- In February 2026, Kenya successfully issued US$2.25B in dual-tranche amortising Eurobonds to proactively tender and refinance upcoming maturities, smoothing its commercial external debt service profile (National Treasury, Feb-26, High).
- Gross foreign exchange reserves held by the Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) expanded to US$14.702B as of October 2026, providing robust import cover and insulating the sovereign against near-term external debt service shocks (Central Bank of Kenya, 07-Oct-26, High).
- Remittance inflows and resilient agricultural export earnings (tea, horticulture) have supported the current account, keeping the Kenyan Shilling (KES) relatively stable against the US Dollar (Central Bank of Kenya, Sep-26, High).
- However, net usable reserves remain sensitive to central bank foreign exchange swap arrangements with domestic commercial banks, requiring ongoing monitoring via Bloomberg data requests (Central Bank of Kenya, Oct-26, Medium).

### Monetary policy orthodoxy, inflation anchoring, and real carry
- The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) of the Central Bank of Kenya held the Central Bank Rate (CBR) unchanged at 8.75% on 07-Oct-26, maintaining a prudent monetary stance (Central Bank of Kenya, 07-Oct-26, High).
- The Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS) reported headline CPI inflation at 6.8% YoY for September 2026, remaining within the CBK target corridor of 5.0% ± 2.5% (Kenya National Bureau of Statistics, 30-Sep-26, High).
- The ex-ante real policy rate stands at +195 bps (8.75% CBR minus 6.8% CPI), providing positive real carry that supports the domestic currency and domestic debt demand (Central Bank of Kenya / KNBS, Oct-26, High).
- Domestic private sector credit growth remains subdued due to high real interest rates and heavy sovereign borrowing in the domestic market (Central Bank of Kenya, Aug-26, High).

### Fiscal deficit, public debt burden, and the interest-to-revenue bottleneck
- Kenya's public debt stock reached Ksh 13.01 trillion (68.5% of GDP) as of end-June 2026, significantly above the medium-term statutory target anchor of 55% of GDP (National Treasury, Jun-26, High).
- The FY2025/26 budget deficit is projected at Ksh 1.199 trillion (6.4% of GDP), driven by rigid non-discretionary spending and shortfall in revenue mobilization (National Treasury, Jul-26, High).
- Interest payments represent the single largest fiscal vulnerability, consuming approximately 33% of ordinary tax revenue and creating severe fiscal crowding out (National Treasury / World Bank, Jun-26, High).
- Heavy issuance of domestic Treasury bills and bonds continues to reinforce the sovereign-bank nexus, leaving local commercial banks heavily exposed to government paper (Central Bank of Kenya, Sep-26, High).

### IMF relationship and post-EFF successor programme outlook
- Kenya's previous multi-facility IMF programme (Extended Fund Facility, Extended Credit Facility, and Resilience and Sustainability Facility) concluded uncompleted in April 2025 following widespread public opposition to tax mobilization measures in the 2024/2025 Finance Bills (IMF, Sep-26, High).
- Technical consultations between the authorities and the IMF are ongoing regarding a successor financing arrangement to anchor medium-term fiscal consolidation and unlock concessional external funding (IMF, Sep-26, High).
- Without an active Fund programme, policy discipline is vulnerable to pre-election spending pressures ahead of the 2027 general elections, increasing reliance on domestic market financing and higher-cost commercial debt (World Bank, Jun-26, Medium).
