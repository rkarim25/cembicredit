## View
- Neutral. External liquidity is the strength; debt affordability, early fiscal slippage and the missing IMF anchor are the weakness. No market levels are held, so there is no relative-value call (Medium).
- Ratings are low single-B and stable: S&P B, Fitch B-, Moody's B3 (upgraded from Caa1 on 27-Jan-26). Levels are consistent across tier 3 sources. Action dates are not verified from agency text (Medium on levels, Low on dates).
- Gross reserves are US$14.702bn (CBK, 07-Oct-26), about 4.6 months of imports. The KES is flat near 129.4/USD. Net usable reserves are unknown (High on gross, Low on net).
- The CBR was held at 8.75% on 07-Oct-26, the fourth consecutive hold. CPI rose from 5.59% in Apr-26 to 6.76% in Sep-26, which limits scope to ease (CBK, KNBS/IMF, High).
- Debt is 68.5% of GDP against the 55% anchor. Interest takes about 33% of ordinary revenue, in the range where the framework says defaults cluster (National Treasury, Medium).
- New this run: Q1 FY2026/27 revenue missed target by Ksh58.2bn and a supplementary budget is due in November (press, Medium).
- No IMF programme since Apr-25. The Article IV mission is expected in early Dec-26 and the governor called a loan "not that crucial". Press reports IMF reluctance to agree a programme before the 09-Aug-27 election (Medium).
- Biggest risk: revenue shortfall plus pre-election spending with no Fund anchor, forcing costlier Eurobond or domestic financing.
- Confidence: High on reserve, rate and CPI data. Medium on solvency ratios, ratings, the IMF outlook and liability management. Low on Eurobond sizing and on market pricing, which is absent.

## Analysis

### Ratings
- Levels: S&P B, Fitch B-, Moody's B3, all stable (CNBC Africa, 27-Jan-26; FXEmpire aggregator, 03-Nov-26; Ecofin, Aug-25, Medium).
- Moody's upgraded to B3 from Caa1 on 27-Jan-26, citing higher reserves, a narrower current account deficit and a stable shilling. Weak debt affordability and slow consolidation cap the rating (CNBC Africa, 27-Jan-26; AllAfrica, 28-Jan-26, Medium).
- Fitch affirmed B-/Stable on 23-Jan-26 (Kenyan Wall Street, Jan-26; FXEmpire, 03-Nov-26, Medium).
- S&P upgraded to B on 22-Aug-25 on the debt buyback (Ecofin, Aug-25, Medium). A later retention at B/Stable warned of rising debt costs and a possible 7.1% deficit in FY2026/27 (Kenyans.co.ke, undated, Low on date).
- The PDMO ratings page (https://pdmo.treasury.go.ke/credit-ratings) was not read. Judgement: upgrade momentum looks exhausted, and the Q1 slippage tilts near-term rating risk to the downside, though no agency action is reported (Low).

### External position
- CBK gross reserves are US$14.702bn at 07-Oct-26 (CBK, High). IMF International Liquidity shows US$13.074bn (May-26), US$13.930bn (Jun-26), US$15.361bn (Jul-26) and US$14.729bn (Aug-26), up 28.3% over twelve months. Gold is negligible (IMF, High).
- The CBK and IMF series differ in date and possibly definition. Net reserves after swaps, forwards and short-term liabilities are unknown (open item 5).
- Import cover is 4.63 months (World Bank WDI, 2025, High). The current account is -4.1% of GDP (IMF WEO 2026E, Medium). Net FDI is 0.8% of GDP (WDI, 2025, Medium). Nominal GDP is US$147.3bn (IMF WEO, Medium).
- The KES traded between 129.35 and 129.48 per USD from Mar-26 to Aug-26 (BIS, High).
- The commodity terms-of-trade index is down 2.9% over twelve months to May-26 (IMF CTOT, Medium). Kenya is an oil importer, and Brent was about 112 on 30-Oct-26 (knowledge base, Trading Economics, Medium).
- Judgement: reserves below about US$13bn or a break of the KES range would change the external view (Low).

### Fiscal and financing
- Public debt is Ksh13.01tn, 68.5% of GDP, at Jun-26, against the 55% anchor (National Treasury, High).
- The FY2025/26 deficit is projected at Ksh1.199tn (6.4% of GDP). The year closed in Jun-26 but no audited outturn is in hand (National Treasury, Medium).
- Interest is about 33% of ordinary revenue, approximate (National Treasury / World Bank, Medium).
- FY2026/27 budget:
  - Earlier press: spending Ksh4.82tn, deficit Ksh1.16tn (Kenyans.co.ke; the-star.co.ke, Jun-Jul-26, Medium).
  - Latest press: spending Ksh4.81tn, revenue Ksh3.63tn, deficit 5.5% of GDP (Ksh1.14tn), rising to 5.9% in FY2027/28 (TechTrendsKE, 31-Oct-26, Medium).
- Q1 FY2026/27 revenue was Ksh655.8bn against a Ksh714bn target, a Ksh58.2bn miss. A supplementary budget is expected in Nov-26, including Ksh45.5bn for Kenya Airways (TechTrendsKE, 31-Oct-26; Business Daily, Medium).
- Eurobond and liability management, all tier 3:
  - Oct-25: US$1.5bn raised. Ksh86.4bn funded a buyback of US$1bn of the 2028s and Ksh103.1bn funded budget support (People Daily, undated).
  - Feb-26: a Ksh64.5bn buyback began on 19-Feb-26 (HapaKenya), followed by a US$2.25bn dual-tranche issue (BusinessDay Nigeria; CNBC Africa, Feb-26).
  - Planned buyback of up to US$500m (The Star, 15-Jul-26). Overlap with the Feb-26 operation is unresolved (open item 9).
  - Planned Eurobond: Treasury is considering about Ksh145.6bn (US$1.12bn) to refinance the Ksh194bn 9.75% note due 2031 (Kenyans.co.ke; Business Daily/Zawya via search summary, Low-Medium). A Bloomberg headline of 14-Aug-26 reported US$815m by end-Dec-26 (paywalled, content not read, Low). The sizes are inconsistent.
  - An audit report flags losses on the buyback deal (People Daily, undated, Low).
- The domestic/external split, average maturity, gross financing need, the post-buyback ladder, arrears and contingent liabilities (Kenya Airways, KPLC) are not sourced.

### Monetary
- The CBR was held at 8.75% on 07-Oct-26, the fourth consecutive hold, citing energy prices linked to the Middle East conflict (CBK MPC press release, 07-Oct-26, High).
- CPI y/y: Apr-26 5.59%, May 6.68%, Jun 6.41%, Jul 6.49%, Aug 6.59%, Sep 6.76% (IMF data portal; KNBS 6.8%, High).
- The headline real rate is about +2.0pp on trailing CPI (arithmetic, High). A true ex-ante rate needs expectations data (open item 6).
- Next MPC: 02-Dec-26 (CBK, High).

### IMF and politics
- The previous programme (about US$3.6bn per the position, not otherwise sourced) expired uncompleted in Apr-25 (IMF, Sep-26, Medium).
- Kenya requested a new programme in Mar-25. Missions in Sep-Oct-25 and Feb-Mar-26 ended without staff-level agreement (Times Live, 26-Feb-26; Kenyan Wall Street, Oct-26, Medium).
- Treasury (12-Oct-26) expects an IMF mission in early Dec-26. Governor Thugge (15-Oct-26) said the start depends on agreeing the programme's structure and priorities, and called a loan "not that crucial" for the balance of payments. The IMF has awaited Kenya's response to a draft governance diagnostic since Apr-26 (Rio Times, secondary summary; Kenya Times, Oct-26, Medium).
- Judgement: no agreement is likely before the 09-Aug-27 election, and the election limits consolidation in H2 FY2026/27 (Low-Medium). Protest and security risk was not researched.

### Banking system
- IMF FSI, deposit takers, Q3-25 (about a year old, Medium):
  - CAR 19.97%, Tier 1 17.65%.
  - NPL 13.54%, down from 14.2% in Q2-25.
  - Provisions/NPL 51.97%.
  - ROE 27.2%.
  - FX open position -0.82% of capital.
- The position states that banks are the main buyer of government paper but gives no figure. Sovereign exposure as a share of bank assets is not sourced, so the doom-loop channel cannot be assessed.

### Market
- No Bloomberg levels are available. Supply is the main technical event: a Eurobond and possible buyback before or around year-end (Low-Medium). No relative-value conclusion is possible beyond the neutral stance.
