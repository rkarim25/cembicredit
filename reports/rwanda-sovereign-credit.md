## View
- Draft. Neither position produced a retrieved, linked source, so no figure below is above Medium confidence.
- Working view: a single-B, programme-anchored sovereign. External debt is mostly multilateral and concessional, and the only commercial bond is a US$620m 5.50% issue due Aug-31 (Medium).
- Reported but unverified (Gemini; no URLs): Fitch B+ Stable (11-Sep-26), Moody's B2 outlook to Stable (06-Oct-26), S&P B+ Stable (May-26), a 38-month ECF with first-review staff-level agreement on 06-Oct-26, and H1 2026 growth of 9.7%.
- The same reported data show a weaker macro picture: Aug-26 CPI of 15.7% against a 2–8% target band, a policy rate of 8.75% and a current account deficit near 15% of GDP.
- The reported trailing real policy rate is about –7 pp (8.75% less 15.7%), which is not an ex-ante measure. If the data are right, the external position is the lead risk and the stable-outlook narrative needs reconciling.
- Biggest risk: loss or conditioning of donor and bilateral financing through the eastern DRC conflict. It would hit the external and fiscal accounts together.
- No commercial refinancing pressure before Aug-31 is the main mitigant (Medium).
- Confidence in the overall view: Medium on structure (concessional debt, one bond, programme anchor). Low on every 2026 data point and rating action until the open items close.

## Analysis

### Debt structure and refinancing
- A single commercial Eurobond: US$620m 5.50% due Aug-31, issued Aug-21. Both models recall the same terms; the prospectus was not retrieved (Medium).
- The ISIN XS2373051320 was given by one model only and is unverified (Low).
- 87–89% of external debt is reported as multilateral or concessional, attributed to Fitch (11-Sep-26). No link was given (Medium).
- The bond is a bullet. Refinancing risk is nil until Aug-31, then concentrated in one maturity (Medium).

### IMF and official sector
- An IMF anchor probably exists (Medium). The specific programme chronology is not verified (Low).
  - Gemini reports a 38-month ECF approved in Jun-26, a first-review staff-level agreement on 06-Oct-26 for SDR 26.43m (~US$35.7m) and a Board date in Dec-26.
  - It also reports the prior PCI completing in Dec-25 and the RSF in Dec-24.
  - Claude recalls the PCI and RSF both being approved in Dec-22.
  - Four months from approval to a first-review staff-level agreement is faster than the usual semi-annual cadence.
- The disbursement implies about US$1.35 per SDR, which is arithmetically plausible.
- The debt-distress rating and latest DSA are not retrieved. Claude recalls "moderate" (Low).

### Ratings
- Gemini reports Fitch B+ Stable (affirmed 11-Sep-26), Moody's B2 Stable (outlook raised from Negative, 06-Oct-26) and S&P B+ Stable (affirmed May-26) (Medium for levels, Low for outlooks and dates).
- The reported Moody's rationale is easing DRC tail risk and regional diplomacy. It is an unquoted paraphrase (Low).
- Single-B levels are consistent with Claude's independent recall.

### Macro and external
- Reported by Gemini only, from BNR, NISR and Fitch and without links (Low–Medium):
  - H1 2026 real GDP growth of 9.7%; Fitch 2026 forecast of 7.8%.
  - Aug-26 CPI of 15.7%; the BNR hiked 50 bps to 8.75% in Aug-26, the third hike of 2026.
  - A 2026 current account deficit near 15% of GDP, driven by Bugesera Airport and commodity imports.
  - Gross reserves of ~US$2.2bn, or ~4.0 months of imports.
- Gemini cites both a 5% target and a 2–8% band. The target definition needs checking.
- Gemini attributes the inflation to cost-push factors (fuel, fertilizer, logistics). No source was given for that (Low).
- If these figures are confirmed, reserve cover of ~4 months against a ~15% deficit leaves little buffer.

### Fiscal
- Reported fiscal deficits: 4.8% of GDP for FY2025/26 (attributed to the IMF release) and about 4.5% of GDP for FY2026/27 (attributed to Fitch). The fiscal-year convention is not stated, and growth figures are on a calendar basis (Low).
- Not obtained: debt-to-GDP and interest-to-revenue. Gemini's 75–80% debt-to-GDP and 63% net external debt-to-GDP are attributed to Fitch without a link (Low).
- Contingent liabilities are the fiscal gap: RwandAir and Bugesera Airport. Qatar Airways is reported to hold a 60% stake in the ~US$1.3bn airport PPP, which limits direct sovereign debt creation. Neither model verified this; guarantees and lease commitments are unknown (Low).

### Politics and willingness
- The eastern DRC/M23 conflict is the shared main tail risk to donor budget support and diplomatic standing, through possible sanctions or aid conditionality (Medium for the risk, Low for current status).
- Both models agree on the risk. Neither retrieved current news.
