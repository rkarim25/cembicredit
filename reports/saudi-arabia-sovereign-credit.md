## View
- Stable, low-risk hard-currency credit on the official data. Ability to pay is strong; valuation cannot be assessed because no market levels were provided.
- External: gross reserves USD 488.7bn (Jul-26), USD 482.1bn net of one-year drains. USD/SAR is unchanged at 3.75 from Apr to Sep-26. Import cover is 15.6 months.
- Fiscal: debt 32.1% of GDP and deficit 3.5% of GDP (2026E, IMF WEO). Debt stays low but is rising on a persistent deficit.
- Current account: -USD 25.8bn over the last four quarters, but +USD 4.1bn in 2026-Q1. The swing coincides with a terms-of-trade jump, so persistence is unproven.
- Banks: CAR 20.46%, NPL 1.04% (2025-Q4). They are not a contingent-liability concern on these numbers.
- Biggest risk: an oil price reversal combined with PIF and giga-project commitments. The debt perimeter in WEO excludes these.
- Ratings, the curve, the financing plan and the Sep-26 rate trigger are unverified. No independent news or filings search was done.
- Confidence: Medium-High on fundamentals (tier 2 data). Low on anything involving ratings, pricing or contingent liabilities.

## Analysis
### External position
- Gross reserves were USD 488.684bn in Jul-26 (IMF International Liquidity), up 10.0% over 12 months (derived, High). Gold is only USD 0.433bn, so reserves are almost entirely FX. Net of predetermined one-year drains (-USD 6.484bn) they were USD 482.147bn (IMF reserve template, Jul-26, High).
- Reserves moved between USD 476bn and USD 497bn from Feb to Jul-26. Mar-26 was the peak at USD 496.6bn, and Jul-26 is about USD 8bn below it (IMF, High).
- Import cover was 15.6 months in 2025 (World Bank WDI, High). USD/SAR was 3.75 in every month from Apr to Sep-26 (BIS, High).
- The current account was -USD 13.4bn, -8.2bn, -8.2bn and +4.1bn over 2025-Q2 to 2026-Q1, summing to -USD 25.8bn (IMF BOP, High). The IMF WEO 2026E is -1.6% of GDP (about USD 22bn on nominal GDP of USD 1,389bn, derived).
- The goods surplus rose to USD 30.1bn in 2026-Q1 and the services deficit narrowed to -USD 10.7bn. Secondary income, mostly remittances, widened to -USD 19.4bn (IMF BOP, High).
- The IMF commodity terms-of-trade index (net exports to GDP weights) rose from 82.5 in Feb-26 to 95.8 in Mar-26 and was 94.2 in May-26. It is up 15.9% over 12 months (IMF CTOT, High on data). One quarter of surplus does not establish a trend (Medium).
- The REER z-score is -0.31 standard deviations against the 10-year mean (BIS broad real EER, Aug-26). This is no overvaluation signal, but under a peg it is a weak indicator (Medium).

### Fiscal position
- Government debt is 32.1% of GDP and the fiscal balance is -3.5% of GDP (2026E, IMF WEO). On nominal GDP of USD 1,389bn that implies debt of about USD 446bn and a deficit of about USD 49bn (derived, Medium). WEO figures are estimates.
- Real growth is 3.1% in 2026E (IMF WEO, Medium).
- Interest-to-revenue, gross financing need, the debt-stabilising primary balance and the 2026 financing plan are not in the brief and were not researched. The framework tests (interest to revenue above 20%, gross financing need above 15% of GDP) cannot be applied (data gap).
- Contingent liabilities, mainly PIF, SOE and giga-project debt, sit outside the central government debt figure. They are the main unquantified fiscal risk (Low, framework judgement with no sourced item).

### Monetary and financial
- The policy rate was 4.25% from Apr to Aug-26 and 4.50% in Sep-26 (BIS, High). CPI was 1.82% y/y in Aug-26 (IMF CPI database, High). The derived ex-ante real policy rate is 2.68%, using latest CPI as a proxy for expectations.
- The rate rise is consistent with the peg and USD rate following. The trigger is unverified and is an open item.
- Banking system, 2025-Q4 (IMF FSI, High): CAR 20.46%, Tier 1 18.82%, NPL 1.04%, provisions to NPL 70.25%, ROA 2.26%, ROE 14.98%. FX open position is -12.61% of capital, wider than -7.79% in 2025-Q3.
- No loan-to-deposit, interbank or offshore wholesale funding data is in the brief.

### Institutions, willingness and official sector
- No sourced items. The view that willingness to pay is not a constraint rests on framework judgement and the absence of a default history (Low). No IMF programme is shown in the brief.

### Issuer data in the repo
- The repo holds quasi-sovereign and corporate files for SEC, SNB and STC. They are stored with ratings, quotes and 2021-2024 financials dated Sep-26 (tier 2, not re-verified).
- No Cognitive Credit model exists for SEC. A model must be built from SEC's own filings (annual and interim reports from the investor relations site or Tadawul) before it is used in this analysis.
- These files were not used for the sovereign view.

### Data gaps
- Ratings and outlooks; Eurobond and sukuk list and EMBI GD weight.
- MoF 2026 budget statement, NDMC borrowing plan and 2026 outturn.
- IMF Article IV and DSA.
- PIF and SOE debt, and SAMA deposit placements.
- Oil data behind the terms-of-trade jump.
- All market levels.
- A sovereign model must be built from official sources (MoF, NDMC, SAMA, GASTAT, IMF) to extend the data record.
- Last 90 days of news and filings: not searched, so no negative result can be reported.
