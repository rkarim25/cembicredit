## View
- Côte d'Ivoire stands as sub-Saharan Africa's premier sovereign credit story, anchored by harmonized BB/Ba2 ratings, robust 6.0% real growth, and completion of its US$4.8B IMF arrangements (IMF / Rating Agencies, Jun-26, High).
- In June 2026, the IMF Executive Board completed the final reviews of the EFF/ECF/RSF programmes, elevating Côte d'Ivoire to the sole SSA sovereign with 'low risk of debt distress' (IMF, Jun-26, High).
- Ratings are unified across the double-B tier: Fitch at BB (Stable, 12-Jun-26), S&P at BB (Stable, 14-Nov-25), and Moody's at Ba2 (Stable, Mar-26), putting the sovereign on the threshold of crossover status (Rating Agencies, Jun-26, High).
- Monetary stability is anchored by the BCEAO's CFA peg to the euro; policy rate was held at 3.0% on 09-Sep-26 as domestic inflation cooled to 1.2% YoY in August 2026 (BCEAO / INS, Sep-26, High).
- Public debt peaked at 56.3% of GDP in 2025 and is tracking down toward 55.1% in 2026, while the fiscal deficit will temporarily widen to 3.8% before converging to 3.0% by 2028 (IMF / Ministry of Finance, Jun-26, High).
- International market access is demonstrated by the February 2026 €1.1B 15-year Eurobond placement at 4.30% (5.39% hedged yield), confirming deep institutional demand (Ministry of Finance, Feb-26, High).
- Offshore hydrocarbon expansion provides a structural balance-of-payments tailwind: Eni reached US$4B Phase 3 FID on the Baleine field in May 2026 (150k bpd oil, 200 mmcf/d domestic gas) alongside the Calao discovery (Eni, May-26, High).
- Political continuity is locked in following President Alassane Ouattara's re-election for a fourth term on 25-Oct-25 with 89.8% of the vote, securing technocratic policy stability through 2030 (CEI, Oct-25, High).
- Key downside risks: security spillover from the Sahel/AES border buffer, regional WAEMU bank liquidity constraints, and global risk-off refinancing headwinds (Fitch, Jun-26, Medium).
- Overall confidence: High on macroeconomic growth, rating stability, and IMF debt distress classification; Medium on BCEAO pooled reserve allocation; Low on live secondary Eurobond valuations until confirmed via Bloomberg.

## Analysis

### Sovereign credit ratings and crossover credit profile
- Côte d'Ivoire has consolidated its position in the upper tier of emerging African credits, securing uniform BB/Ba2 credit ratings across all three major international agencies (Rating Agencies, Jun-26, High).
- Fitch Ratings affirmed Côte d'Ivoire's Long-Term Foreign-Currency IDR at BB with a Stable outlook on 12-Jun-26, following its upgrade from BB- on 12-Dec-25, citing institutional political stability, strong economic expansion, and sustained reform momentum (Fitch Ratings, 12-Jun-26, High).
- S&P Global Ratings affirmed its BB/B sovereign ratings with a Stable outlook on 14-Nov-25, emphasizing favorable economic growth prospects and multilateral anchoring (S&P Global Ratings, 14-Nov-25, High).
- Moody's Investors Service affirmed Ba2 with a Stable outlook in March 2026, highlighting manageable government debt service and resilient access to international capital markets (Moody's Investors Service, Mar-26, High).

### IMF programme graduation and debt sustainability milestone
- In June 2026, the IMF Executive Board completed the sixth and final reviews under the 40-month Extended Fund Facility (EFF) and Extended Credit Facility (ECF) arrangements (totaling US$3.5B), as well as the fifth review under the Resilience and Sustainability Facility (RSF) (US$1.3B), unlocking approximately US$832.8M in final disbursements (IMF, Jun-26, High).
- In a rare milestone for sub-Saharan Africa, the IMF reclassified Côte d'Ivoire's debt sustainability rating from 'moderate' to 'low risk of debt distress', making it the sole sovereign in sub-Saharan Africa to achieve this status (IMF, Jun-26, High).
- The authorities met all end-December 2025 quantitative performance criteria and structural benchmarks, successfully reducing the fiscal deficit to the WAEMU convergence ceiling of 3.0% of GDP in 2025 (IMF, Jun-26, High).

### BCEAO monetary union mechanics, currency peg, and inflation containment
- Côte d'Ivoire benefits from its membership in the West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU) and the BCEAO central bank framework, which pegs the CFA franc (XOF) to the euro at 655.957 XOF per EUR with an unlimited convertibility guarantee backed by the French Treasury (BCEAO, Sep-26, High).
- The BCEAO Monetary Policy Committee maintained its main policy rate at 3.0% and marginal lending facility at 5.0% on 09-Sep-26, holding policy steady to maintain real interest rate support across the zone (BCEAO, 09-Sep-26, High).
- Domestic inflation has cooled rapidly: national statistics reported headline CPI inflation easing to 1.2% YoY in August 2026 (down from 1.9% in July), well below the regional 3.0% ceiling, preserving real purchasing power and providing positive real carry in regional assets (Institut National de la Statistique, Aug-26, High).

### Fiscal consolidation path and public debt dynamics
- Central government debt-to-GDP peaked at 56.3% in 2025 and is projected to decline to 55.1% in 2026, remaining well within sustainable bounds and well below the WAEMU 70% debt limit (IMF / Ministry of Finance, Jun-26, High).
- While the fiscal deficit is budgeted to temporarily expand to 3.8% of GDP in 2026 to accommodate security outlays and social buffers amid external volatility, the authorities remain formally committed to returning to the 3.0% WAEMU ceiling by 2028 (IMF / Ministry of Finance, Jun-26, High).
- Tax revenue mobilization remains the primary medium-term fiscal challenge: tax-to-GDP stands at approximately 13.5%, prompting ongoing digitalisation of tax administration and broadening of the VAT net (IMF, Jun-26, Medium).

### External debt structure, Eurobond market access, and liability management
- Côte d'Ivoire maintains proven, recurring international capital market access, contrasting sharply with peers locked out of commercial issuance (Ministry of Finance, Feb-26, High).
- On 18-Feb-26, the Republic successfully placed €1.1B in 15-year amortising Eurobonds due 2040 at a 4.30% coupon (5.39% yield equivalent after FX hedging), attracting strong European institutional book demand (Ministry of Finance, Feb-26, High).
- This follows the March 2025 US$1.75B Eurobond and CFAF 220B (~€335M) international placement, as well as the January 2024 US$2.6B dual-tranche issue (Ministry of Finance, Mar-25, High).
- The sovereign actively amortises its commercial external debt profile to avoid single-year maturity cliffs, but relies on UMOA-Titres domestic/regional bond issuances to meet local financing needs (Ministry of Finance / UMOA-Titres, Jun-26, High).

### Structural growth drivers: Offshore hydrocarbons (Baleine & Calao) and agribusiness
- Real GDP growth is projected at 6.0% in 2026 following 6.5% in 2025, maintaining Côte d'Ivoire's position as one of the fastest-growing economies in Africa (IMF, Jun-26, High).
- In May 2026, Eni and state oil company Petroci finalized the US$4B Final Investment Decision (FID) for Phase 3 of the giant Baleine offshore field, which will scale output to 150,000 bpd of crude oil and 200 mmcf/d of natural gas, with all gas earmarked for domestic power generation (Eni, May-26, High).
- The 2024 Calao deepwater discovery (estimated at 5 Tcf of gas and 450M barrels of condensate) reinforces the long-term energy transition, positioning the country as a regional power exporter and industrial gas feedstock provider (Ministry of Mines and Petroleum, May-26, High).
- Cocoa and agricultural processing continue to generate steady export receipts, though crop diseases and weather volatility require active sector monitoring (Ministry of Agriculture, Sep-26, Medium).

### Political environment and regional Sahel security transmission
- Political risk has significantly abated following the 25-Oct-25 presidential election, where incumbent President Alassane Ouattara secured re-election for a fourth term with 89.8% of the vote (50.1% turnout), ensuring policy continuity and technocratic governance through 2030 (Commission Électorale Indépendante, Oct-25, High).
- The primary geopolitical headwind stems from instability in neighboring Sahel nations (Mali and Burkina Faso within the AES confederation), which requires sustained border security deployments in northern districts (Fitch Ratings, 12-Jun-26, Medium).
- Regional WAEMU banking system liquidity has tightened as commercial banks absorb rising volumes of sovereign paper across member states, elevating yields in the domestic Treasury bill and bond markets (UMOA-Titres, Jun-26, Medium).
