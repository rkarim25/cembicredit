## View
Armenia is an improving BB- credit supported by orthodox macroeconomic management, robust growth, and record external liquidity buffers.
Gross international reserves exceeding USD 6.5bn provide over 5 months of import cover, substantially cushioning the sovereign against external volatility.
Government debt discipline remains intact with debt-to-GDP held below 50% (5.12trn AMD) and a 2026 fiscal deficit tracking at 3.4% of GDP.
Near-term commercial refinancing risk is negligible following the Mar-25 issuance of the USD 6.75% 2035 Eurobond, leaving no Eurobond maturities until Sep-29.
Economic activity remains strong with the EAI expanding 7.3% YoY Jan–Aug 2026, transitioning from re-export windfalls toward domestic investment.
The Central Bank of Armenia maintains credible policy orthodoxy, raising the refinancing rate to 6.75% in Sep-26 to stabilize inflation at 4.3%.
Rating momentum is uniformly positive with Fitch (BB-), Moody's (Ba3), and S&P (BB-) all holding Positive outlooks.
The primary structural vulnerability is an elevated current account deficit (9.8% of GDP in Q2 2026) as Russian transit flows normalize.
The key tail risk remains diplomatic friction and unfinished border demarcation regarding the initialed peace treaty with Azerbaijan.
Overall credit conviction: Medium-High, underpinned by strong liquidity, precautionary IMF backing, and absent Eurobond redemptions through 2029.

## Analysis

### 1. External position and liquidity buffers
Gross international reserves held by the Central Bank of Armenia (CBA) surpassed USD 6.5bn in September 2026 (CBA, Sep-26, High). This represents an increase from USD 3.6bn in late 2023, driven by sustained net foreign currency purchases and capital inflows, and provides more than 5 months of import cover (CBA, Sep-26, High).
The external current account deficit stood at USD 749.7m in Q2 2026, or 9.8% of nominal GDP (Armstat, Jun-26, High). While this marks an improvement from 12.5% in Q1 2026, it highlights an underlying structural deficit as transitory re-export flows and bilateral intermediation trade cool down (Armstat, Jun-26, High).
A 3-year IMF Stand-By Arrangement (SBA) approved in December 2025 provides policy continuity and is treated as fully precautionary by Armenian authorities (IMF, Jun-26, High). The completion of the first review in June 2026 brought total available access to US$50.2m without drawings (IMF, Jun-26, High).

### 2. Fiscal trajectory and debt structure
Armenia's total public debt stood at 5.12trn AMD (approximately USD 13.9bn) as of 30 June 2026, maintaining public debt below 50% of GDP and well beneath the statutory 60% debt ceiling (Ministry of Finance, 30-Jun-26, High).
The 2026 state budget deficit is currently tracking at 3.4% of GDP, outperforming the initial target of 4.5% (Ministry of Finance, Oct-26, High).
The approved draft budget for 2027 projects revenues of 3.8trn AMD, expenditures of 4.2trn AMD, and an overall deficit of 468.2bn AMD (Ministry of Finance, Oct-26, High). Deficit funding is diversified across domestic debt and official bilateral and multilateral lines, including a newly secured €250m facility from the French Development Agency alongside ADB and World Bank loan disbursements (Ministry of Finance, Oct-26, High).
Commercial external debt refinancing is clear through late 2029 following the issuance of the USD 10-year 6.75% 2035 Eurobond (XS3020792696) in March 2025, which pre-financed upcoming liabilities and eliminated Eurobond maturities until the USD 3.95% notes come due in September 2029 (Ministry of Finance, 12-Mar-25, High).

### 3. Monetary policy and inflation
The Central Bank of Armenia raised the benchmark refinancing rate by 25 bps to 6.75% on 15 September 2026, marking the first policy rate hike since December 2022 (CBA, 15-Sep-26, High).
Headline CPI inflation registered at 4.3% YoY in September 2026 (Armstat, Sep-26, High), slightly above the CBA's 3.0% medium-term target (±1.0% tolerance band), with core inflation running at 4.8% YoY (Armstat, Sep-26, High).
The policy hike delivers an ex-post real policy rate of +2.45%, preserving positive real rates, anchoring inflation expectations, and defending dram stability without reserve drain (CBA, 15-Sep-26, High).

### 4. Economic growth and re-export normalization
The Economic Activity Index (EAI) expanded 7.3% YoY in January–August 2026 (Armstat, Sep-26, High). Growth was led by domestic construction (+24.7%) and services (+12.8%), while trade turnover growth moderated sharply to +0.7% YoY as re-export windfalls tapered (Armstat, Sep-26, High).
Multilateral growth forecasts for full-year 2026 project real GDP expansion between 5.2% and 5.5% (IMF 5.25%, World Bank 5.3%, EBRD 5.5%, Fitch 5.2%), reflecting resilient domestic demand (World Bank / IMF / EBRD, Jun-26, High).

### 5. Geopolitical dynamics and rating momentum
Rating agencies maintain a uniformly positive trajectory: Fitch affirmed Armenia at BB- with a Positive outlook on 10 July 2026; Moody's revised its Ba3 outlook to Positive on 28 July 2026; and S&P affirmed BB- with a Positive outlook on 21 August 2026 (Fitch / Moody's / S&P, Jul-Aug-26, High).
Key rating drivers cited include structural reserve accumulation, diversification of foreign security partnerships toward the EU and US, and progress in regional diplomacy (Fitch / Moody's / S&P, Jul-Aug-26, High).
The interstate peace treaty between Armenia and Azerbaijan was initialed in Washington, D.C. on 8 August 2025 (Government of Armenia, 08-Aug-25, Medium). While border delimitation and regional transport communication protocols ("Trump Route" transit) remain subject to ongoing negotiations, the border has remained stable, reducing acute conflict escalation risk (Government of Armenia, Oct-26, Medium).
