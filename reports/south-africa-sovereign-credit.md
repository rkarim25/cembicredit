## View
- Constructive, with upgrade risk skewed to the upside. Moody's (Ba2) and S&P (BB FC / BB+ LC) are on Positive; Fitch upgraded to BB (Stable) on 05-Jun-26.
- Moody's and S&P are scheduled to review on 27-Nov-26 and Fitch on 11-Dec-26 (Business Day, 27-Oct-26; BusinessTech, Oct-26, Medium).
- Sell-side is split on the first mover. BofA sees Moody's (to Ba1) as most likely and an S&P move to BB+ as a closer call. Morgan Stanley expects S&P in November and Moody's in early 2027. I do not call a winner.
- Fitch is unlikely to move on 11-Dec-26 after a Stable outlook set in June (inference, Medium).
- The fiscal path is on track but not improving. Per press, the MTBPS (21-Oct-26) shows a FY26/27 deficit of 3.9%, debt of 78.2% and a primary surplus of about 1.7% of GDP. The revenue overrun was largely spent.
- Biggest risk: growth and oil. 2026 growth is cut to 1.2%, Q2-26 GDP was -0.2% q/q, and Brent is above USD 110 (knowledge base, 30-Oct-26). SARB hiked to 7.25% in Sep-26.
- The external position is not a rating constraint: gross reserves $75.5B (Sep-26) and a current account of about -1.6% of GDP.
- Not assessable: market pricing. No bond, CDS or EMBI levels are sourced.
- Confidence: Medium. Fiscal figures rest on press summaries, not Treasury tables.

## Analysis

### Ratings and calendar
- Fitch: upgraded to BB from BB-, Stable, on 05-Jun-26, the first upgrade since 2005 (Africanews, 06-Jun-26; Daily Maverick, 07-Jun-26; National Treasury statement; Medium-High).
- S&P: upgraded to BB in Nov-25 with a Positive outlook, and affirmed BB FC / BB+ LC Positive on 29-May-26 (IOL, 30-May-26; Treasury statement; Medium-High).
- Moody's: Ba2, outlook raised to Positive in May-26 (Business Day, 23-May-26, Medium-High). On 14-Oct-26 it said an upgrade was possible, and noted it upgrades about 60% of positive-outlook sovereigns (Engineering News, 14-Oct-26, Medium).
- Reviews: Moody's and S&P on 27-Nov-26, Fitch on 11-Dec-26 (Business Day, 27-Oct-26; BusinessTech, Oct-26, Medium).
- Sell-side: BofA says Moody's is most likely to act, with S&P a closer call. Morgan Stanley expects S&P in Nov-26 and Moody's in early 2027 (Engineering News, 20-Oct-26, Medium). Four of 11 surveyed economists expect an upgrade by year-end (Times Live, 17-Oct-26, Medium).
- All three agencies rate South Africa two notches below investment grade. A one-notch move by Moody's or S&P would leave it one notch below.

### Fiscal
- MTBPS (21-Oct-26, press summaries): debt 78.4% of GDP in FY25/26, 78.2% in FY26/27 and 76.6% in FY29/30. Deficit 3.9% in FY26/27, narrowing to 2.5% in FY29/30. Primary surplus 1.7% in FY26/27 (IOL, EWN, 21-Oct-26, Medium).
- The revenue overrun of R50–70bn (Times Live, 17-Oct-26) was largely offset by higher spending. Non-interest spending was raised by R45bn against R38.1bn of planned savings, and debt service by R9bn (IOL, 21-Oct-26, Medium).
- A fuel levy relief costs about R10bn (IOL, 21-Oct-26, Medium). The overrun is mining-driven and cyclical (Medium).
- Debt service was R420.6bn in FY25/26, 21% of revenue, above the framework's 20% warning level (Times Live, 17-Oct-26, Medium).
- Moody's puts the debt-stabilising primary surplus at about 1.5% of GDP, against its 1.8% forecast for 2027 (Bloomberg via press, Oct-26, Medium). I did not compute pb* independently.
- A principles-based fiscal anchor is to be legislated in 2027. No numerical ceiling has been seen (EWN, 21-Oct-26, Medium).
- The IMF Article IV (10-Feb-26) put debt at 77% of GDP at end-Mar-25 and found the expenditure ceiling insufficient to stop debt rising (IMF, High).
- Contingent liabilities (Eskom, Transnet, SANRAL) and the maturity profile were not researched.

### Monetary
- SARB raised the repo rate 25bp to 7.25% unanimously in Sep-26, after a 4–2 hold on 23-Jul-26 in which two members voted for a hike (EWN, 23-Jul-26; Briefly, 24-Sep-26; Medium-High).
- CPI was 4.5% y/y in Sep-26 against a 3.0% target adopted 12-Nov-25. SARB projects CPI above 5% in late 2026 and early 2027 (SAnews, Oct-26; press on the SARB statement, Medium).
- The ex-ante real rate is about 2.9% (derived using a BER expectation that is not independently sourced). The next MPC date is unconfirmed.

### Growth
- The MTBPS cut 2026 growth to 1.2% from 1.6%, and forecasts 1.6% for 2027 (IOL, 21-Oct-26, Medium). The IMF WEO in the repo has 1.0% for 2026E.
- Q2-26 GDP was -0.2% q/q after +0.4% in Q1, with mining -3.0% q/q (CareEdge, 28-Sep-26, Medium).

### External
- Gross reserves incl. gold were $75.48B in Sep-26 (+8.2% over 12 months). Net of one-year drains they were $72.8B, and ex-gold $58.6B (IMF, High).
- The SARB headline of $63.5B with 5.8 months of import cover is not reconciled to the IMF series.
- The current account was -$1.32B over the last four quarters (IMF BOP, High), about -1.6% of GDP (SARB, Medium).
- The BIS real broad REER was 113.91 in Sep-26, a z-score of +1.04, so the rand is not cheap (BIS, High).

### Banks, politics, market
- Banks 2025-Q2: CAR 16.06%, NPL 4.51% (IMF FSI, 15 months old, Medium).
- The GNU (ANC, DA, IFP) has held since the May 2024 elections. The 2027 anchor legislation will test its cohesion (Medium). No IMF programme exists.
- Eurobond ISINs, sizes, CAC terms, index weights and all market levels are unsourced.

## Where the models disagreed
1. **First mover among the agencies:** BofA says Moody's. Morgan Stanley says S&P in November. Both are tier 3 and conflict, so no winner is picked (rule 2).
2. **Fitch outlook:** one search summary said Fitch BB- and Moody's Stable. The position discounted it against Treasury and press reports of the 05-Jun-26 upgrade. Resolved by tier 2 primary evidence. The claim that all three are on Positive is wrong for Fitch, which is Stable.
3. **FY26/27 deficit and debt peak:** the position noted a 4.3% snippet and 78.9%. These are older or unsourced and are superseded by the post-MTBPS 3.9% and 78.4%. They remain flagged as press-sourced (recency rule).
4. **Reserves:** the $63.5B headline against $75.5B gross is unresolved and kept as an open item.
