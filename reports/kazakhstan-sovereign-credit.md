## View
Kazakhstan is a low-leverage, investment-grade sovereign. Government debt is 24.9% of GDP and the fiscal deficit is 1.3% of GDP (2026E, IMF WEO, High). Ratings are reported at BBB/Baa1 with stable outlooks and positive momentum, but this rests on secondary press (Medium).
Gross reserves were USD 70.6bn in Aug-26, up 29.9% in 12 months. The rise is mostly gold: USD 55.8bn, against USD 14.8bn of reserves ex gold, which have been flat since Mar-26.
Disinflation is under way. CPI fell from 10.64% in Apr-26 to 9.24% in Sep-26, while the base rate is reported at 16.75%.
The biggest risk is external. Primary income outflows of USD 5.0–6.6bn a quarter (oil-sector FDI income) keep the current account in deficit. A fall in oil prices or a disruption to CPC export routes would hit it. The 12-month terms-of-trade gain of 11.5% to May-26 currently masks this.
The data do not show a credit problem. Valuation cannot be judged without spreads (open items 2–3). Confidence in the credit-quality view is High on fundamentals and Medium on ratings.

## Analysis
### Ratings and index
- S&P BBB stable (reported upgrade, Aug-26), Fitch BBB stable (Jun-26), Moody's Baa1 stable (Sep-26), per secondary reporting (dknews.kz; fxempire; Medium). The agency releases were not opened, so dates and outlooks are unverified (open item 1).
- EMBI GD weight and the Eurobond list are not sourced (open items 3–4).
- No IMF programme is known. No Article IV 2026 finding was sourced (Low).

### Fiscal and debt
- Government debt is 24.9% of GDP and the fiscal balance is -1.3% of GDP for 2026E (IMF WEO, 09-Oct-26, High). Nominal GDP is USD 360.5bn (2026E).
- Interest to revenue, the non-oil deficit, NFRK transfers and the 2026 budget are not sourced. Debt composition and contingent liabilities of state-owned enterprises (Samruk-Kazyna, KMG) are also not sourced.
- Fiscal strength is a valid reason for the ratings. The missing NFRK data would show how large the buffer is.

### External position
- **Current account:** USD -2.5bn (2025-Q2), -3.7bn (Q3), -5.2bn (Q4) and -2.0bn (2026-Q1), USD -13.4bn in total (IMF BOP, High). The goods balance is positive each quarter (USD 0.3–4.1bn). Primary income is USD -5.0bn to -6.6bn a quarter, which points to a structural income outflow from foreign-owned oil projects (the attribution to Tengiz and Kashagan is inference, Low).
- **WEO inconsistency:** the 4Q total is about 3.7% of 2026E GDP (arithmetic from the figures above), against -1% in the WEO. The gap is unresolved (open item 7).
- **FDI:** net FDI liabilities were negative in 2025-Q2 (USD -1.3bn) and 2025-Q4 (USD -1.8bn), and positive in 2025-Q3 (USD 1.3bn) and 2026-Q1 (USD 2.6bn) (IMF BOP, High). Flows are volatile.
- **Reserves:** gross reserves incl. gold USD 70.57bn in Aug-26, up from USD 61.83bn in Jun-26 (IMF International Liquidity, High). Gold rose from USD 47.6bn to USD 55.8bn over the same period; ex-gold reserves stayed at USD 14.3–15.2bn from Mar to Aug-26. Reserves net of 1-year drains were USD 59.8bn in Jul-26 (IMF reserve template, High).
- **Interpretation:** the reserve rise is mostly a gold effect. The split between purchases and price is unknown (open item 6), so the improvement in usable FX liquidity is not demonstrated (Medium).
- **Import cover:** 7.4 months in 2025 (World Bank WDI, High).
- **Terms of trade:** the IMF net-export CTOT index rose 11.5% in the 12 months to May-26 (High).
- **FX:** USD/KZT ranged 464.6–489.3 from Mar to Aug-26 (BIS, High). It was 464.55 in Aug-26.

### Monetary and inflation
- CPI was 9.24% y/y in Sep-26, down from 10.64% in Apr-26 (IMF CPI database, High).
- The base rate was held at 18% in Mar-26, cut to 17.00% in Jun-26 and to 16.75% in Jul-26 (ING; Interfax 24-Jul-26; Medium). The NBK target is single-digit inflation in 2026 (Medium).
- Trailing real rate is about 7.5pp (16.75% less 9.24%, arithmetic). The ex-ante measure needs forward CPI, which is not sourced.
- Risk to disinflation: administered tariff and utility price increases. The VAT/tax code changes are unverified (Low).

### Banking system
- 2025-Q4 (IMF FSI, High): CAR 20.77%, Tier 1 20.05%, NPL 3.63% (3.52% in Q3), provisions to NPL 60.6% (63.6% in Q3), ROA 5.13%, ROE 28.41%, FX open position to capital 1.29%.
- Capital and FX exposure are strong. Watch the NPL uptick and falling provision cover in retail unsecured lending (the link to rapid consumer credit growth is inference, Low).

### Growth
- Real GDP growth 4.6% for 2026E (IMF WEO, High). Oil production, OPEC+ compliance and the Tengiz expansion are unsourced and should be added next run.

### Market and relative value
- No Bloomberg levels were supplied. Relative value cannot be assessed (open items 2–3).
