## View
Azerbaijan is a premier CEEMEA net sovereign creditor rated BBB- (Stable) by Fitch (05-Jun-26) and Baa3 (Stable) by Moody's (18-Jun-26), alongside an unsolicited BB+ (Stable) from S&P after Azerbaijan terminated its paid rating contract in late 2025.
External liquidity buffers are exceptionally strong: strategic foreign currency assets totaled $87.96bn in mid-2026, comprising SOFAZ assets of $72.60bn (30-Jun-26) and CBAR foreign exchange reserves of $15.36bn (23-Sep-26).
Sovereign asset buffers exceed 400% of total public debt and provide more than 50 months of merchandise import cover, placing Azerbaijan in an unassailable net external creditor position.
Public debt is negligible at 18.4% of GDP ($14.18bn / 24.1bn AZN as of 01-Jun-26), with external government debt at just $6.4bn (8.3% of GDP), among the lowest across global emerging markets.
The external current account remains structurally in surplus ($4.74bn in H1 2026, ~10.5% of annualized GDP), firmly backing the de facto currency peg of 1.7000 AZN/USD.
Commercial debt service is minimal following the full maturity and redemption of the $1.0bn Southern Gas Corridor (SGC) 2026 Eurobond in March 2026, leaving only $1.39bn in direct sovereign Eurobonds outstanding.
Economic growth is subdued at 1.2% y/y (Jan–Aug 2026), held back by mature oil field depletion (-0.8% in hydrocarbons) despite solid non-oil sector growth (+2.1%).
The primary structural vulnerability is long-term hydrocarbon depletion and heavy non-oil fiscal deficit dependence, alongside lingering regional geopolitical friction over the Zangezur transit corridor.
Overall credit conviction is High: sovereign solvency is fortress-like, default probability is negligible, and Eurobonds trade as quasi-investment-grade quality paper.

## Analysis

### Sovereign credit rating profile and institutional dynamics
Azerbaijan maintains an institutional investment-grade credit profile supported by massive net asset positions, though agency coverage reflects differing assessment methodologies (Fitch Ratings, 05-Jun-26, High). Fitch affirmed its BBB- rating with a Stable outlook on 05-Jun-26, underscoring the sovereign's massive sovereign wealth fund buffers, negligible public debt, and solid current account surplus (Fitch Ratings, 05-Jun-26, High). Moody's maintains a Baa3 rating with a Stable outlook (reaffirmed 18-Jun-26 following an upgrade in July 2025), citing robust external resilience and low government financing requirements against structural institutional constraints (Moody's Ratings, 18-Jun-26, High). S&P Global Ratings maintains an unsolicited BB+ rating with a Stable outlook following the Azerbaijani government's decision in December 2025 to discontinue its commercial relationship with S&P (S&P Global Ratings, 22-May-26, High). Governance indicators remain constrained by autocratic executive concentration, weak judicial independence, and limited transparency outside the hydrocarbon sector, which cap rating convergence toward the BBB/Baa2 bucket (World Bank WGI, 01-Jul-26, High).

### Fortress external balance sheet and sovereign wealth fund buffers
Azerbaijan's external balance sheet functions as one of the strongest sovereign shock absorbers in emerging markets (State Oil Fund of Azerbaijan, 30-Jun-26, High). Strategic foreign currency assets expanded to $87.96bn in mid-2026, consisting of $72.60bn managed by the State Oil Fund of the Republic of Azerbaijan (SOFAZ) and $15.36bn in official gross foreign exchange reserves held by the Central Bank of Azerbaijan (CBAR) (SOFAZ / CBAR, Sep-26, High). SOFAZ assets alone represent approximately 94% of projected 2026 GDP and cover the entire stock of total government debt more than five times over (SOFAZ, 30-Jun-26, High). CBAR gross foreign exchange reserves expanded by 33.4% year-to-date from $11.51bn at end-2025 to $15.36bn as of 23-Sep-26, bolstered by persistent trade surpluses and mandatory foreign currency conversions by energy exporters (Central Bank of Azerbaijan, 23-Sep-26, High). The external current account recorded a surplus of $4.74bn in H1 2026 (~10.5% of GDP), driven by steady natural gas export volumes via the Southern Gas Corridor to Southern Europe and elevated regional oil export realizations (Central Bank of Azerbaijan, 15-Sep-26, High).

### Public debt structure and commercial Eurobond maturities
Azerbaijan possesses one of the lowest public debt ratios in the sovereign emerging markets universe (Ministry of Finance, 01-Jun-26, High). Total government debt stood at 24.1bn AZN ($14.18bn) as of 01-Jun-26, representing 18.4% of GDP (Ministry of Finance, 01-Jun-26, High). Direct external public debt accounts for ~$6.4bn (8.3% of GDP), with the remainder comprising domestic government securities held primarily by domestic commercial banks and the central bank (Ministry of Finance, 01-Jun-26, High). Commercial external refinancing risk was significantly derisked in March 2026 when the state-guaranteed Southern Gas Corridor (SGC) $1.0bn 6.875% Eurobond (XS1319820897) matured and was fully repaid without refinancing issuance (Luxembourg Stock Exchange, 08-Oct-26, High). The sovereign's remaining marketable Eurobond debt profile is exceptionally compact, consisting of only two liquid benchmark lines: the USD 5.125% due 01-Sep-2029 ($310.7m outstanding, ISIN XS1678623064) and the USD 3.500% due 01-Sep-2032 ($1,076.0m outstanding, ISIN XS1678623734) (Luxembourg Stock Exchange, 08-Oct-26, High). Neither line presents refinancing distress given that SOFAZ liquid assets could redeem the entire Eurobond stock simultaneously with negligible reserve impact (Fitch Ratings, 05-Jun-26, High).

### Fiscal framework, non-oil balance, and SOFAZ transfer mechanisms
Fiscal policy remains heavily dependent on hydrocarbon revenues and direct annual statutory transfers from SOFAZ to the state budget (Ministry of Finance, 01-Jun-26, High). The consolidated government budget operates near balance, recording a slight overall surplus in H1 2026 supported by average oil price realizations above the budget benchmark of $75/bbl (Ministry of Finance, 01-Jun-26, High). However, the underlying non-oil primary fiscal deficit remains structurally elevated at ~23% of non-oil GDP, reflecting substantial capital expenditure allocations for infrastructure reconstruction and demining across liberated Karabakh and East Zangezur territories (IMF Article IV, 01-Jul-26, Medium). SOFAZ transfers directly finance approximately 45–50% of annual central government budgetary expenditures, insulating the domestic economy from revenue shortfalls but embedding persistent vulnerability to long-term hydrocarbon demand contraction (Fitch Ratings, 05-Jun-26, High).

### Monetary orthodoxy and the unassailable currency peg
The Central Bank of Azerbaijan operates a de facto fixed exchange rate peg anchored at 1.7000 AZN per US dollar, supported by bi-weekly foreign exchange auctions (Central Bank of Azerbaijan, 23-Sep-26, High). At its monetary policy meeting on 23-Sep-26, the CBAR maintained its benchmark refinancing rate at 6.50%, with the interest rate corridor floor at 6.00% and ceiling at 7.50% (Central Bank of Azerbaijan, 23-Sep-26, High). Annual headline inflation remained subdued at 3.5% y/y in August 2026, well within the central bank's target band of 4.0% ± 2.0% (State Statistical Committee, Sep-26, High). The de facto peg exhibits zero parallel market premium and is fully credible over a multi-year horizon: CBAR foreign exchange reserves ($15.36bn) exceed the domestic currency monetary base (M0) by over 130%, ensuring complete convertibility backstop under adverse balance-of-payments scenarios (Central Bank of Azerbaijan, 23-Sep-26, High).

### Structural growth constraints and hydrocarbon transition
Economic growth is structurally constrained by the secular depletion of mature oil fields, partially offset by natural gas expansion and non-hydrocarbon service sector development (State Statistical Committee, Sep-26, High). Total real GDP expanded by 1.2% y/y in Jan–Aug 2026, with the oil and gas sector contracting by 0.8% y/y due to output decline at the mature Azeri-Chirag-Gunashli (ACG) oil field complex (State Statistical Committee, Sep-26, High). In contrast, the non-oil/gas economy expanded by 2.1% y/y over the same period, led by transportation, construction, and agriculture (State Statistical Committee, Sep-26, High). Natural gas production from the Shah Deniz field and expanding European export commitments via the Trans-Adriatic Pipeline (TAP) continue to provide medium-term volume support, though long-term growth will require sustained structural economic diversification away from extractives (IMF Article IV, 01-Jul-26, Medium).

### Geopolitical risk and regional corridor diplomacy
Geopolitical risk has stabilized following the full restoration of sovereign territorial control over Karabakh in late 2023, though regional peace negotiations with Armenia remain pending formal treaty signature (Fitch Ratings, 05-Jun-26, Medium). Ongoing diplomatic discussions focus on border demarcation and the establishment of transport connectivity, particularly the Zangezur corridor linking mainland Azerbaijan with the Nakhchivan exclave (Reuters, 15-Sep-26, Medium). Azerbaijan's strategic position as a critical node in the Trans-Caspian International Transport Route (Middle Corridor) linking Central Asia and China to Europe enhances its geopolitical relevance and generates sustained non-oil transit revenues, reinforcing strategic bilateral relationships with both the European Union and regional powers (Ministry of Economy, 01-Jul-26, High).
