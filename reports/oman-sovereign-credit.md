## View
- Oman operates as a consolidated triple-investment-grade sovereign credit (S&P BBB- Stable; Moody's Baa3 Stable; Fitch BBB- Stable) following multi-year fiscal de-leveraging (Rating Agencies, Dec-25, High).
- Public debt has declined to OMR 14.6bn (~$37.9bn or 35.0% of GDP) at end-2024 and ~34.7% at end-2025, down from 68% in 2020 via surplus-driven buybacks (Ministry of Finance, Dec-25, High).
- Fiscal discipline remains anchored with consecutive budget surpluses (3.3% of GDP in 2024, ~0.6% in 2025, ~4.5% projected 2026) and a breakeven oil price reduced to $65–$70/bbl (Ministry of Finance / IMF, Jun-26, High).
- Structural fiscal diversification is advancing via 5% VAT and legislated 5% Personal Income Tax on high earners effective 2028 under Royal Decree 56/2025 (Official Gazette, Jul-25, High).
- External buffers are fortified by CBO foreign reserves of $18.38bn (OMR 7.07bn, ~5 months import cover) and Oman Investment Authority assets of $59.7bn (OMR 23bn) (CBO / OIA, Dec-25, High).
- The currency peg (0.3845 OMR/USD since 1986) remains unchallenged, underpinned by net external creditor status and persistent current account surpluses (~2.5%–3.3% of GDP) (CBO / IMF, Jun-26, High).
- Benchmark Eurobond spreads trade compressed inside 80 bps on 5Y–7Y paper, limiting capital appreciation and leaving the credit as a low-beta defensive anchor (Secondary Quotes, Sep-26, Medium).
- Primary downside risk stems from sustained oil prices below $65/bbl combined with regional geopolitical escalation near the Strait of Hormuz (Market Sources, Sep-26, Medium).
- Overall confidence: High on official fiscal de-leveraging, rating status, and reserve adequacy; Low on secondary market pricing until verified via [BBG].

## Analysis

### Sovereign rating trajectory and triple-IG restoration (BBB- / Baa3 / BBB-)
- Oman achieved full investment-grade reinstatement across all three major rating agencies between late 2024 and late 2025, reversing pandemic-era downgrades into high-yield territory (Rating Agencies, Dec-25, High).
- S&P Global Ratings upgraded Oman to BBB- with a Stable outlook on 27-Sep-2024 (affirmed Sep-2026), citing rapid debt de-leveraging and governance improvements under the Medium-Term Fiscal Plan (S&P Global Ratings, Sep-26, High).
- Moody's Ratings upgraded Oman to Baa3 with a Stable outlook on 10-Jul-2025, emphasizing enhanced fiscal resilience, robust state-owned enterprise (SOE) oversight, and reduced vulnerability to oil price shocks (Moody's Ratings, 10-Jul-25, High).
- Fitch Ratings finalized triple-IG status by upgrading Oman to BBB- with a Stable outlook on 08-Dec-2025, highlighting structural declines in debt-to-GDP and external debt service burdens (Fitch Ratings, 08-Dec-25, High).
- Investment-grade status broadens the institutional investor base for Omani Eurobonds, lowering sovereign borrowing costs and dampening spread beta relative to high-yield CEEMEA peers (Market Sources, Sep-26, Medium).

### Fiscal consolidation, debt trajectory, and breakeven oil price dynamics
- Public debt sustainability has structurally improved: central government debt fell to OMR 14.6bn ($37.9bn or 35.0% of GDP) at end-2024 and approximately 34.7% of GDP at end-2025, down from a peak of ~68% in 2020 (Ministry of Finance, Dec-25, High).
- The fiscal balance posted consecutive surpluses of 3.3% of GDP in 2024 (OMR 1.38bn) and ~0.6%–0.7% in 2025, with the IMF projecting a surplus rebound to ~4.5% of GDP in 2026 as hydrocarbon and gas export revenues remain supportive (Ministry of Finance / IMF, Jun-26, High).
- The sovereign has systematically applied surplus revenue to active liability management, pre-paying expensive commercial bank loans and buying back high-coupon Eurobonds prior to maturity (Ministry of Finance, Dec-25, High).
- Structural fiscal adjustments—including spending rationalization, civil service hiring discipline, and subsidy targeting—have lowered Oman's fiscal breakeven oil price to ~$65–$70/bbl from over $80/bbl in 2019 (IMF Staff Report, Jun-26, High).
- Interest expenditure to revenue has retreated below 10%, eliminating fiscal crowding out and preserving substantial budgetary headroom for domestic infrastructure allocation (Ministry of Finance, Dec-25, High).

### External balance sheet, central bank reserves, and currency peg stability
- The external position is anchored by Central Bank of Oman (CBO) gross foreign assets and gold of OMR 7.068bn ($18.38bn) at end-2024 (up 4.9% YoY from OMR 6.734bn in 2023), providing over 5 months of merchandise import cover (Central Bank of Oman, Dec-24, High).
- Oman Investment Authority (OIA), the sovereign wealth fund managing the Future Generations Fund and domestic development assets, held total assets under management of OMR 23bn ($59.7bn) in 2025, reinforcing net sovereign creditor standing (Oman Investment Authority, Jun-25, High).
- The current account recorded consecutive surpluses of ~3.3% of GDP in 2024 and ~2.5% in 2025, supported by steady LNG shipments from Oman LNG and non-oil manufacturing exports (Central Bank of Oman / IMF, Jun-26, High).
- The currency peg of OMR 0.3845 per USD, established in 1986, faces zero devaluation pressure; interbank forward markets trade flat to par and the banking sector maintains net foreign asset buffers (Central Bank of Oman, Sep-26, High).
- External debt service for 2026–2028 is well-covered by projected sovereign cash flows and OIA liquid offshore assets, containing gross external financing requirements (IMF, Jun-26, High).

### Structural non-hydrocarbon revenue diversification and Vision 2040 reforms
- Non-hydrocarbon revenues are expanding steadily following the April 2021 launch of 5% Value Added Tax (VAT), which yields ~OMR 500m–OMR 600m annually in recurring non-oil tax income (Tax Authority, Jun-25, High).
- In July 2025, Royal Decree No. 56/2025 promulgated a 5% Personal Income Tax (PIT) on net global income of Omani tax residents exceeding OMR 42,000 (~$109,000) annually, effective 01-Jan-2028 (Official Gazette, Jul-25, High).
- Oman is the first GCC sovereign to legislate a direct personal income tax, demonstrating institutional willingness to institutionalize progressive non-oil taxation ahead of regional peers (Tax Authority / Press Releases, Jul-25, High).
- Non-oil GDP growth expanded by ~3.5% in 2024–2025, driven by logistics (Duqm Port and special economic zone), tourism, and green hydrogen concessions, buffering aggregate growth against OPEC+ crude curtailments (National Centre for Statistics and Information, Jun-25, High).

### Eurobond curve structure, liability management, and relative value
- The sovereign Eurobond curve trades as an investment-grade defensive anchor, with 5Y–7Y benchmark spreads compressed to 65–75 bps over US Treasuries and long-end tenors trading at 115–125 bps (Secondary Quotes, Sep-26, Medium).
- Indicative secondary trading benchmarks reflect: OMAN 5.625% Jan-28 at ~4.86% yield (G-spread ~67 bps); OMAN 6.250% Jan-31 at ~5.21% yield (G-spread ~73 bps); OMAN 6.500% Mar-47 at ~6.43% yield (G-spread ~118 bps); OMAN 7.000% Jan-51 at ~6.43% yield (G-spread ~118 bps) (Secondary Quotes, Sep-26, Medium).
- With spreads compressed inside 80 bps, further capital gain via spread tightening is capped; the curve primarily offers low-volatility yield carry and capital preservation for high-grade portfolios (Market Sources, Sep-26, Medium).
- Domestic financing is developing via regular Government Development Bond (GDB) auctions and Sukuk issuances in OMR, though secondary local currency liquidity remains concentrated among domestic commercial banks (Central Bank of Oman, Jun-25, High).

### Institutional governance, contingent liabilities, and geopolitical exposure
- Sovereign governance and SOE oversight are centralized under the Ministry of Finance and OIA, which enforces strict borrowing ceilings and dividend contribution targets for state holding companies (Oman Investment Authority, Jun-25, High).
- Energy Development Oman (EDO), which holds the government's 60% participating interest in Block 6 oil and 100% in Block 6 gas, manages its own debt financing independently ($2.5bn–$3.0bn outstanding) without direct budget call (EDO Financial Statements, Dec-24, High).
- OQ Group (national energy integration entity) continues selective asset privatizations (e.g. OQ Gas Networks, OQ Base Industries) via the Muscat Stock Exchange, using IPO proceeds to deleverage internal holding debt (OQ Group / OIA, Dec-25, High).
- Geopolitical risk remains the primary exogenous vulnerability, specifically potential military escalations in the Persian Gulf or disruptions to tanker navigation through the Strait of Hormuz; however, Oman's diplomatic neutrality and ports outside the Gulf (Duqm, Salalah) provide operational hedge (Regional Press, Sep-26, Medium).
