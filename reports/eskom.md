## View
Eskom is a state-backed credit with a weak standalone profile. Ratings are B (Fitch, S&P) and B2 (Moody's), with the standalone profile at ccc+ (Fitch) and caa1 (Moody's). Guaranteed debt is rated BB- and Ba2.
Operations have improved: no load-shedding since 16-May-25, availability of 67.55% for April–August 2026, and FY26 profit after tax of R30.3bn against a restated R14.0bn.
The tariff step-ups (8.76% in 2026/27, 8.83% in 2027/28) and the R54.7bn error-correction recovery support cash flow to FY28.
The main risks are municipal arrears, falling sales volumes, and the funding path once the three-year R254bn relief programme ends.
Debt fell from about R356bn to about R320bn by June 2026, still above the R300bn target (reached in the year to 31-Mar-30).
View: stable to improving, with spread set by sovereign and guarantee status.
Confidence: Medium. Fitch and Moody's ratings are primary, but reported debt, liquidity and the post-relief plan rest on secondary summaries. The repo's financial data is unreliable and was not used.

## Analysis

### Ratings and support
- Fitch affirmed the long-term local-currency IDR at B, Stable. Unsecured debt is B (RR4), guaranteed debt BB-, standalone profile ccc+ (Eskom SENS, 21-May-26, https://www.eskom.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/20260521-Fitch-Ratings-affirms-Eskom-credit-rating-SENS.pdf; summary https://www.sanews.gov.za/index.php/node/81600, High).
- Moody's affirmed B2 CFR, B2-PD, caa1 BCA, Ba2 backed senior unsecured, Stable. It cites rising municipal arrears, capex needs and unbundling execution (SAnews, 01-Jun-26, https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/81816, High).
- S&P is reported at B unsecured and BB- guaranteed. This was not read at source (Medium).
- Eskom reports its first upgrade in over a decade from all three agencies, following the sovereign upgrade (SAnews, 31-Aug-26, https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/83938, Medium).
- Relief of R254bn over three years is in its final year, R80bn in the latest year (Medium, secondary summaries). The February 2027 Budget is the next test of what follows.

### Operations
- 455 consecutive days without load-shedding since 16-May-25 (Eskom via Engineering News, Oct-26, Medium).
- Availability of 67.55% for April–August 2026, the highest year-to-date since 25-Oct-20 (same source, Medium).
- Four load-shedding days (26 hours) in FY26 (SAnews, 31-Aug-26, High).
- EBITDA margin of 30.63% against 28.75% restated (SAnews, 31-Aug-26, Medium).

### Earnings quality
- FY26 profit after tax was R30.3bn against a restated R14.0bn (Sunday Tribune, 31-Aug-26, https://sundaytribune.co.za/business/2026-08-31-eskom-doubles-profit-to-r303bn-despite-falling-electricity-demand/, Medium).
- Sales were a record-low 178,032 GWh, about 21% below 2008 (Engineering News, 03-Sep-26, Medium). Profit is tariff-driven and may not repeat if volumes keep falling.

### Tariffs
- NERSA corrected its errors (a R14.5bn depreciation shortfall plus a rollforward error). Direct-customer tariffs rise 8.76% on 01-Apr-26 and 8.83% on 01-Apr-27, against 5.36% and 6.19% before.
- R54.7bn is recovered as R12bn in 2026/27, R23bn in 2027/28 and R19.7bn in the following period. Eskom's original claim was R107bn (Engineering News, 10-Mar-26, https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/nersa-approves-higher-electricity-hikes-for-202627-after-r547bn-error-2026-03-10, High).

### Debt and liquidity
- The R38bn ES26 bond was redeemed in April 2026 (Eskom, Apr-26, https://www.eskom.co.za/eskom-redeems-landmark-r38-billion-es26-bond-a-turning-point-for-financial-sustainability-and-investor-confidence/, High).
- Debt securities and borrowings were about R356bn at year-end and about R320bn by June 2026. Eskom targets borrowings below R300bn by the year ending 31-Mar-30 and has said it will resume borrowing (Business Day via search summary, Medium).
- Cash, the maturity ladder and committed lines were not obtained (see Data gaps).

### Municipal arrears
- Arrears rose 17.9% to R111.6bn at 31-Mar-26 and were about R119bn by June 2026 (Sunday Tribune and Engineering News, Aug/Sep-26, Medium on levels, High on direction). This is the main cash-flow risk.

### Repo data
- The stored benchmark (ESKOM 7.125% 2025) matured on 11-Feb-25.
- The 2021A–2024A USD figures and their narrative text are generic template content (e.g. "hard-currency tariff indexation", "corporate dividend policy") that does not describe Eskom.
- The "B / B-" rating is outdated. None of this is used above (High).

### What would change the view
- Negative: a sovereign downgrade or outlook change, return of Stage 2+ load-shedding, a Budget that cuts the remaining relief, or arrears rising further.
- Positive: a funded municipal debt solution, or stable volumes with profit holding without a tariff step-up.

### Data gaps
- FY26 integrated results in rand: revenue, EBITDA, cash, capex, maturity ladder. The Engineering News article is paywalled.
- Fitch drivers, sensitivities and leverage metrics (the PDF was unreadable).
- S&P action details.
- The outstanding USD bond stack, call features and covenants.
- Current prices, yields and spreads.
- Treasury relief conditions and FY27 amounts.
- No Cognitive Credit model exists. A rand model must be built from Eskom's own annual financial statements and integrated reports.
- Searched without a useful result: bond maturities beyond ES26, unbundling milestones (NTCSA and the generation entity), IMF Article IV commentary.
