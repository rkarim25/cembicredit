## View
Zambia is a post-restructuring B-/CCC+/Caa2 sovereign credit benefiting from normalized external creditor relations, rising FX reserves, and sharp disinflation.
The sovereign successfully exited its 2020 default under the G20 Common Framework in June 2024, exchanging defaulted notes into Bond A (2033) and Bond B (2053).
An opportunistic liability management cash tender for Bond B was executed in June 2026, backed by a US$600m African Development Bank facility.
Headline CPI decelerated into the 6%–8% target band at 6.1% in September 2026, enabling the Bank of Zambia to cut the Policy Rate 250 bps to 10.75%.
Gross international reserves recovered to US$5.8bn in September 2026, supported by robust mining FX receipts and Kwacha foreign exchange stability.

Political stability is anchored by President Hakainde Hichilema's re-election victory on 13-August-2026 with 60.49% of the vote.
The primary risk remains high domestic debt service absorption and vulnerability of copper output to climate-induced hydroelectric deficits.

## Analysis

### 1. External debt restructuring and liability management
Zambia achieved comprehensive commercial external debt normalisation on 11-June-2024 following the settlement of its G20 Common Framework exchange (MoFNP, 11-Jun-24, High). The restructuring replaced US$3.8bn of defaulted Eurobonds with two new instruments: USD 5.75%/7.50% amortising Bond A due 30-Jun-33 (XS2837240261) and state-contingent step-up Bond B due 31-Dec-53 (XS2837240428) (Prospectus, Jun-24, High). In June 2026, the Ministry of Finance executed an opportunistic liability management exercise, tendering for Bond B backed by a concessional US$600m policy-based partial credit guarantee facility from the African Development Bank (AfDB, Jun-26, High). Total public external debt moderated from US$16.15bn at end-2025 to US$15.36bn as of June 2026, lowering gross external amortisation pressures over the 2026–2028 horizon (MoFNP, 30-Jun-26, High).

### 2. Macroeconomic stabilization, inflation, and monetary policy
Macroeconomic stabilization has gained significant traction following the severe drought disruptions of 2024–2025 (IMF, Jan-26, High). Annual headline inflation dropped sharply to 6.1% in September 2026, successfully returning within the Bank of Zambia's 6%–8% medium-term target band after peaking at 16.7% in December 2024 (BoZ, 29-Sep-26, High). Currency appreciation and stabilized food logistics allowed the Bank of Zambia to initiate an aggressive easing cycle, cutting the Policy Rate by 250 bps to 10.75% on 29-September-2026 (BoZ, 29-Sep-26, High). Foreign exchange reserves reached US$5.8bn in September 2026, providing over 4.2 months of import cover, supported by higher copper production export surrender regulations and multilateral disbursements (BoZ, 30-Sep-26, Medium).

### 3. Fiscal profile, domestic debt, and power sector vulnerabilities
Despite external debt relief, overall fiscal space remains constrained by high domestic debt rollover costs and legacy liabilities (Fitch, 28-Nov-25, High). Total public debt stands at approximately 91% of GDP, with domestic treasury bills and government bonds accounting for over 45% of total public debt stock (MoFNP, Jun-26, High). Domestic interest payments absorb nearly 35% of domestic revenue, crowd out capital expenditures, and maintain fiscal vulnerability to local liquidity swings (IMF, Jan-26, High). Operationally, the state electricity utility ZESCO continues to recover from drought-induced generation deficits at Kariba Dam, with copper mining operations increasingly relying on private solar off-grid solutions and imports from the Southern African Power Pool (SAPP) (ZESCO, Aug-26, Medium).

### 4. Multilateral anchor and transition to successor program
Multilateral relations have transitioned into a post-ECF framework (IMF, Jan-26, High). The IMF Executive Board completed the sixth and final review under the 38-month US$1.3bn Extended Credit Facility (ECF) arrangement in January 2026, releasing the final US$190m disbursement (IMF, 22-Jan-26, High). The authorities are currently negotiating a successor multi-year financing program with the IMF focused on climate resilience, energy diversification, and infrastructure expansion (MoFNP, Sep-26, Medium). Concurrently, official bilateral creditors under the Official Creditor Committee (OCC) co-chaired by China and France have formalized their bilateral debt relief terms, establishing legal parity with commercial Eurobondholders (MoFNP, Jul-26, High).

### 5. Institutional and political environment
Political and policy risk was substantially clarified on 13-August-2026 when President Hakainde Hichilema of the United Party for National Development (UPND) secured re-election to a second five-year term with 60.49% of the popular vote (ECZ, 15-Aug-26, High). The UPND consolidated its legislative majority in the National Assembly, providing strong legislative backing for the 2027 national budget, continued fiscal deficit consolidation, and anti-corruption mining reforms (ECZ, Aug-26, High). The administration's renewed mandate reduces election-cycle spending risk and guarantees institutional continuity across key economic posts, including the Ministry of Finance and Bank of Zambia leadership (Fitch, Aug-26, Medium).

### 6. Bond structure, credit ratings, and relative value
Sovereign credit ratings have transitioned from default (SD/RD) into the single-B/triple-C category: Fitch rates Zambia B- (Stable) (upgraded 28-Nov-25), S&P rates Zambia CCC+ (Stable) (upgraded 08-Jun-26), and Moody's rates Zambia Caa2 (Positive) (upgraded 14-Apr-25) (Rating Agencies, Jun-26, High). Bond A (XS2837240261) features standard collective action clauses (CACs) with single-limb aggregated voting mechanisms under English law (Prospectus, Jun-24, High). On a relative value basis, Zambia's post-restructuring paper trades at a wider spread relative to regional peers like Kenya or Côte d'Ivoire, offering attractive carry and potential spread compression as macroeconomic indicators normalize and bilateral agreements conclude (Market Assessment, Oct-26, Medium).
