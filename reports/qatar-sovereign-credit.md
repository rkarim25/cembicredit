## View
- Qatar remains a high-quality AA-area GCC sovereign. Solvency is not the question; the question is how long the 2026 LNG-linked output loss lasts.
- IMF WEO 2026E in the repo shows real GDP of -8.6%, a fiscal balance of -3.4% of GDP, a current account of +11% of GDP and government debt of 43.3% of GDP (IMF WEO, repo data, 09-Oct-26, High on the data, Low on the cause).
- The external surplus persists: USD 28.5bn over the four quarters to 2026-Q1, though the goods surplus fell from USD 14.1bn (2025-Q2) to USD 9.7bn (2026-Q1) (IMF BOP, High).
- Fitch holds AA with a negative outlook, per press reports of May-26 (Medium). S&P and Moody's are reported as affirmed, with exact ratings and outlooks unconfirmed.
- The USD peg and the 4.35% policy rate are stable (BIS, IMF MFS, High). Gross reserves of USD 61.1bn (Aug-26) understate the buffer, because QIA assets are not in reserves.
- Biggest risk: a prolonged LNG export loss or infrastructure damage, which would push the fiscal and external balances below the 2026E figures and could lead to a downgrade.
- Stance: hold as a defensive GCC name, and do not add until the restart path and spread levels are known. This rests on Low-confidence inference, because no market levels were supplied.
- Overall confidence: Medium on credit quality, Low on relative value.

## Analysis
### Ratings
- Fitch affirmed AA, removed the Rating Watch Negative and kept a negative outlook, citing continuing risks to LNG exports and infrastructure (Qatar Tribune; Enterprise, 06-May-26, Medium; press report of a tier 2 action).
- S&P is reported to have affirmed, citing the sizeable external and fiscal net asset positions anchored by QIA. Moody's is reported to have said the fiscal profile cushions the war's impact (search summaries, 2026, Low). Exact ratings, outlooks and action dates are not confirmed.
- The UAE comparators in the repo are AA/AA- stable at S&P and Fitch (Mar–May-26, Medium). Qatar's negative Fitch outlook is the main ratings differentiator versus the UAE.

### Growth and the 2026 shock
- Repo IMF WEO data gives 2026E real GDP of -8.6% and nominal GDP of USD 217.4bn (IMF WEO, 09-Oct-26, High on the data).
- A web summary quoted a pre-shock IMF forecast of +6.1% for 2026, with North Field East's first train due mid-to-late 2026 (Gulf Times/ICAEW, Low on vintage). Published forecasts are therefore stale and conflicting.
- The link between the -8.6% and an LNG disruption tied to the regional war with Iran is an inference from the Fitch language and the BOP trend (Low). No primary source on production, force majeure or restart timing was reviewed.

### External position
- Current account: USD 7.8bn (2025-Q2), 10.3bn (Q3), 5.0bn (Q4) and 5.5bn (2026-Q1), totalling USD 28.5bn. 2026E is +11% of GDP (IMF BOP; IMF WEO, High).
- Goods balance by quarter: 14.1, 13.5, 12.0 and 9.7 (USD bn, 2025-Q2 to 2026-Q1). Services were -1.5 to -2.1 and secondary income about -3.0 to -3.7 per quarter (IMF BOP, High). The goods surplus erodes before the shock fully shows in the 2026E figures.
- Gross reserves incl. gold were USD 61.1bn in Aug-26: ex gold USD 44.6bn and gold USD 16.5bn. They rose 10.4% y/y and were USD 55.7bn in Mar-26 (IMF International Liquidity, High). Import cover was 7.45 months (World Bank WDI, 2025, High).
- QIA assets are the main buffer and sit outside reserves. Their size and any 2026 drawdown are unsourced here.
- Commodity terms of trade rose 14.7% in the 12 months to May-26, with a spike in Mar-26 (IMF CTOT, derived, Medium). This is at odds with an output collapse, which suggests price gains partly offset the volume loss.

### Fiscal position
- Government debt is 43.3% of GDP and the fiscal balance is -3.4% of GDP (2026E, IMF WEO, High). That is a modest deficit given the output shock, so the buffers are not under visible strain.
- The financing plan, the Eurobond list and QIA transfers are not sourced here.

### Monetary and financial
- The policy rate was 4.35% from Mar to Aug-26 (IMF MFS, High). The peg was 3.64 per USD from Apr to Sep-26 (BIS, High).
- CPI was 0.73% y/y in Aug-25 (IMF CPI database). The derived ex-ante real rate of 3.62% therefore rests on 13-month-old inflation (Low).
- No bank-system data (FSI) for Qatar is in the brief. The only bank in the repo is QNB, with no Cognitive Credit model. Its stored figures are generic and should not be used for the sovereign view.

### Relative value
- No spread, price or index-weight data is available. A negative-outlook AA name should be compared with ABUDAB and KSA at equal duration (Low, inference). The view stays neutral until Reza supplies levels.

### Data gaps
- Sovereign model: the repo model rests on IMF, World Bank and BIS series only. It still needs the MoF budget and debt data, the QCB balance of payments detail, the Eurobond list with terms, and QIA asset data from official sources.
- Primary agency releases for all three ratings, the IMF Article IV, QatarEnergy production and export data, and current CPI.
- Independent news search: the supporting position reported press items for Fitch, S&P and Moody's only. No primary-source search result on LNG disruption was captured, so that point stays open.
