## View
Cameroon is a single-B/Caa credit (Fitch B Negative, S&P B- Stable, Moody's Caa1 Stable) supported by CEMAC monetary union backing and moderate headline public debt (~43% of GDP).
External refinancing risk was successfully extended to 2029–2031 through the Jul-24 placement of a USD 550m 9.5% 2031 amortising bond, cutting the Nov-25 maturity to a manageable ~$68.7m.
Liquidity remains structurally strained with debt service absorbing over 20% of fiscal revenue, driving an IMF classification of "high risk of debt distress".
Domestic financing capacity is constrained as regional CEMAC commercial banks approach regulatory single-obligor exposure limits amidst persistent domestic supplier arrears.
Multilateral programme financing lapsed following completion of the ECF/EFF/RSF arrangement in Jul-25, transitioning IMF surveillance to a Post-Financing Assessment (PFA).
Executive succession around 93-year-old President Paul Biya remains the dominant institutional risk, notwithstanding the Apr-26 constitutional amendment re-establishing an appointed Vice President.
The principal risk is an unmanaged political transition precipitating administrative paralysis, civil unrest in Anglophone regions, and delayed external debt payments.
Overall view confidence: Medium.

## Analysis

### 1. External position and market access
Cameroon actively mitigated near-term commercial refinancing risk via its liability management transaction in July 2024, placing USD 550m in 9.50% amortising Eurobonds due July 2031 (XS2869469333) (CAA, Jul-24, High). The proceeds repurchased the bulk of the USD 750m 9.50% November 2025 notes (XS1313779081), leaving a residual principal of ~$68.7m that presents negligible default risk for external debt service (CAA, Jul-24, High). The new 2031 amortising structure disburses repayments in three equal annual tranches across 2029, 2030, and 2031, smoothing external debt service alongside the EUR 685m 5.95% 2032 Eurobond (XS2360598630) (Prospectus, Jul-24, High). The external current account deficit remains contained at approximately 3.5%–4.0% of GDP, supported by crude oil, liquefied natural gas (LNG), cocoa, and timber export earnings (IMF, Jul-25, Medium). External reserve backing is pooled at the regional central bank (BEAC) under the French Treasury convertibility guarantee, insulating the sovereign from unilateral balance-of-payments exchange-rate panics (BEAC, Jun-26, High).

### 2. Fiscal trajectory and debt sustainability
Headline public debt remains moderate by Sub-Saharan African standards, standing at XAF 14.105 trillion or 43.0% of GDP as of end-June 2025, with external debt accounting for approximately 30% of GDP and domestic debt comprising 13% of GDP (CAA, 30-Jun-25, High). However, debt sustainability is severely compromised by weak domestic non-oil revenue mobilisation (~12% of GDP) and rigid recurrent spending, which drives debt service to exceed 20% of budgetary revenues (IMF, Jul-25, High). The IMF Debt Sustainability Analysis (DSA) classifies Cameroon at "high risk of debt distress" due to breaches in the debt-service-to-revenue and debt-service-to-export thresholds (IMF, Jul-25, High). Contingent liabilities from state-owned enterprises remain material, specifically the uncompleted financial restructuring of the state oil refinery SONARA following its 2019 fire, alongside persistent arrears to domestic suppliers and pending VAT refund claims that drain budget execution (Fitch, 24-Apr-26, Medium).

### 3. Monetary, banking sector and CEMAC constraints
Monetary policy and macro-financial stability are anchored by the Bank of Central African States (BEAC) within the Central African Economic and Monetary Community (CEMAC), maintaining the fixed peg at 655.957 XAF per EUR (BEAC, Jun-26, High). This institutional arrangement eliminates sovereign FX transfer risks on euro-denominated debt and keeps domestic inflation moderate (2.6% y/y through Aug-26) (INS Cameroun, Aug-26, High). Conversely, domestic treasury financing is heavily reliant on commercial banks within the CEMAC syndicated bond market (BVMAC and Treasury bill auctions), where commercial banks are nearing regulatory exposure limits for sovereign paper (Moody's, 27-Feb-24, High). Strained banking sector liquidity and domestic payments processing bottlenecks have historically induced technical delays in non-Eurobond commercial debt servicing, driving rating agencies to constrain the credit rating to the single-B/Caa tier (Fitch, 24-Apr-26, High).

### 4. Political transition and governance
Governance and institutional continuity represent the paramount risk factor for Cameroonian sovereign risk (Fitch, 24-Apr-26, High). President Paul Biya, aged 93 and in power since 1982, secured an eighth presidential term in the 12-October-2025 election (Elections Cameroon, Oct-25, High). To address succession fragility, the National Assembly passed constitutional amendments in April 2026 re-establishing the post of an appointed Vice President designated to take over executive authority in the event of vacancy (Cameroon Tribune, Apr-26, Medium). Nevertheless, factional rivalries within the ruling Cameroon People's Democratic Movement (RDPC) and between security factions create acute uncertainty regarding an orderly succession (Fitch, 24-Apr-26, Medium). Concurrently, the protracted conflict in the Anglophone North-West and South-West regions and Boko Haram incursions in the Far North maintain security spending pressure and present persistent social risk (IMF, Jul-25, Medium).

### 5. Multilateral engagement and IMF relationship
Cameroon successfully concluded its three-year Extended Credit Facility (ECF) and Extended Fund Facility (EFF) arrangements alongside the Resilience and Sustainability Facility (RSF) in July 2025, which unlocked over USD 1.0bn in total financing over the 2021–2025 period (IMF, 15-Jul-25, High). Surveillance has since transitioned to an annual Post-Financing Assessment (PFA), as outstanding Fund credit exceeds 200% of quota (IMF, 15-Jul-25, High). While official bilateral support from France and multilateral disbursements from the World Bank and African Development Bank continue on a project basis, Cameroon currently lacks an active concessional balance-of-payments financing anchor (IMF, Jul-25, High). Negotiation of a successor programme will require politically sensitive structural reforms, including further rationalisation of untargeted fuel subsidies and civil service payroll audits (IMF, Jul-25, Medium).

### 6. Eurobond curve structure and recovery valuation
Cameroon's hard-currency bond curve comprises the residual USD 2025s (XS1313779081), the benchmark EUR 685m 5.95% 2032 notes (XS2360598630), and the amortising USD 550m 9.50% 2031 notes (XS2869469333) (Prospectus, Jul-24, High). The bonds incorporate standard collective action clauses (CACs) with two-limb aggregation mechanisms under English law (Prospectus, Jul-24, High). Pricing reflects a substantial sovereign spread premium over African peer benchmarks, driven by political transition uncertainty rather than immediate solvency failure (CBonds, Sep-26, Medium). In a downside scenario involving protracted political paralysis or Common Framework debt treatment, high multilateral seniority (~50% of external debt) would require significant private commercial creditor participation, with recovery valuations estimated around 55c–65c based on historical Sub-Saharan African restructuring precedents (Precedent Analysis, Oct-26, Low).
