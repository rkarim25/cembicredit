## View
- Regime: an oil-supply shock (Hormuz disruption) feeding US inflation and a Fed hiking cycle. The Fed hiked 25bp to 3.75–4.00% on 16-Sep-26. September CPI was 3.7% y/y against 3.6% expected, with core at 2.6% (Medium-High on the hike, Medium on CPI).
- Brent is about USD 109.5, the highest since May and up from about USD 100 on 03-Oct-26 and USD 90.1 at end-Aug-26. This is the main change since the last version (Medium).
- The 10y UST is reported at 5.07%, below the early-October peak of about 5.30% but still restrictive. A 28-Oct-26 hike is a live call, with press odds of about 47% (Medium-Low).
- The dollar gives limited help. DXY is about 101 (unverified), and the CPI print lifted it (Low-Medium).
- EM credit has held up on carry and Q3 inflows. HY sovereign spreads are described as tight, which leaves little cushion (Medium).
- Dispersion remains the main implication. Importers with negative real rates or thin net reserves (Romania, Philippines, Egypt, Pakistan, Kenya) sit against exporters with terms-of-trade gains and high real rates (High on the dispersion, Medium on persistence).
- Biggest risk: a hawkish 28-Oct-26 FOMC combined with a Hormuz or Saudi escalation. That would hit importers and HY hard-currency duration together.
- Confidence: Medium on the regime, High on the cross-sectional dispersion, Low on current market levels pending Bloomberg.

## What changed

- September CPI (14-Oct-26) came in hot at 3.7%. This revives hike risk for 28-Oct-26. The earlier version only had hike odds of about 70% for December.
- The 10y level is partly resolved. It is 5.07% on 19-Oct-26, consistent with a fall from the 5.30% touch. The undated 4.74% aggregator figure carries no weight. Still single-source, so a Bloomberg check remains open.

- The macro monitor was refreshed on 19-Oct-26. Philippines real rate is now −2.2 (CPI 7.2%), Brazil 9.2, Kazakhstan 7.5. Turkey net reserves are down 8% over 12 months.

## Analysis
### US rates and the Fed
- The FOMC raised the range to 3.75–4.00% on 16-Sep-26, the first hike since 2023 (Schwab and press, 16-Sep-26, Medium-High). The September SEP median implies about 4.1% by end-2026 (aggregator summary, Sep-26, Medium).
- September CPI was 3.7% y/y against 3.6% expected, core 2.6% (investinglive.com, 14-Oct-26, Medium). Press puts the 28-Oct-26 hike probability at 47% and 36bp priced for the year (gvwire.com, 14-Oct-26, Medium-Low). A prediction-market page showed about 78.5% hold, but it dates from about 05-Oct-26, before the CPI, so it does not conflict (Robinhood, Low).
- 10y UST fell about 2.5bp to 5.07% on 19-Oct-26 (FinanceFeeds, Oct-26, Medium-Low). Gramercy had the 10y at 5.30% (highest since 2007) and the 30y above 5.60% in the week to 03-Oct-26 (Gramercy, 03-Oct-26, Medium). Treasuries led the September sell-off while EM debt held up (VanEck, Oct-26, Medium).
- Early-October data: September payrolls +29k against about 90k expected, unemployment 4.2%, August core PCE 3.0% y/y (Gramercy, 03-Oct-26, Medium). Growth data has softened while headline inflation has re-accelerated.

### Dollar and EM FX
- DXY was above 102 (an 18-month high) on about 03-Oct-26 (Gramercy, Medium). One summary puts it at 101.45 on 30-Sep-26 (unverified, Low). DXY fell 0.49% in August (Aberdeen, Aug-26, Medium). The dollar advanced after the 14-Oct-26 CPI (gvwire.com, Medium-Low).
- An undated source says EM FX and Treasuries diverged the most in over four years (cryptobriefing.com, undated, Low). It is not used for any conclusion.

### Commodities
- Brent closed at USD 109.55 on 19-Oct-26 after a USD 109.86 settle on 16-Oct-26, the highest since May. Hormuz flows averaged 11.62m b/d in the week to 13-Oct-26 against a 17.15m b/d baseline (FinanceFeeds, Oct-26; Mitrade, 19-Oct-26, Medium). Houthi claims against Saudi infrastructure are unconfirmed (Low). Brent was USD 90.1 at end-Aug-26 (Aberdeen, Aug-26, Medium).
- Exporters gain and importers lose, per the structural map (knowledge/themes/commodities-and-em.md, Medium). Terms of trade, 12m to May-26 (macro monitor, 19-Oct-26, High on the data but pre-dating the latest oil move): Kuwait +28, Angola +18, Iraq +17, Saudi Arabia +16, Qatar +15, Azerbaijan +14, Kazakhstan +12. Jordan −6, Côte d'Ivoire −7, Thailand −4.
- Gold, copper and agricultural prices were not sourced in this run.

### Cross-section (macro monitor, 19-Oct-26, tier 2)
- High real policy rates: Turkey 12.5 (CPI 29.7%, policy 37.00%), Brazil 9.2, Ghana 8.8, Uzbekistan 7.8, Ukraine 7.9, Nigeria 7.6, Kazakhstan 7.5, Angola 6.0, Colombia 5.7, Hungary 4.0, Serbia 3.5, Mexico 3.0.
- Negative real rates: Rwanda −7.0, Philippines −2.2 (CPI 7.2%, policy 5.00%), Romania −1.7 (CPI 8.2%, Jul-26), Thailand −1.5. CPI dates lag policy dates, so signs may change.
- Thin buffers:
  - Egypt: net-of-drains reserves USD 17.2bn against USD 53.6bn gross, fiscal −12.1%, CA/GDP −4.2%.
  - Pakistan: reserves USD 28.0bn, CPI 10.3%.
  - Kenya: reserves USD 14.7bn, fiscal −6.4%.
  - Turkey: net reserves USD 137.1bn, gross down 8% over 12 months, CA 4Q −USD 38.9bn.
  - Indonesia: net USD 75.5bn against USD 146.5bn gross.
- Deep buffers: India net USD 715.7bn, Poland USD 277.2bn, Czech Republic USD 172.0bn, Saudi Arabia USD 480.8bn.
- Central banks are diverging. SARB hiked 25bp to 7.25% on 24-Sep-26. Banxico held 6.50%. Copom cut to 13.75% on 16-Sep-26. CBRT held 37% (knowledge base, Sep-26, Medium). CBRT meets 22-Oct-26.

### Risk appetite and EM credit
- HY spreads tightened 13bp and IG 6bp in Aug-26. EMBI GD returned +0.89% (Aberdeen, Aug-26, Medium). Q3 flows were about +USD 1.4bn hard currency and +USD 4.1bn local (search summary, Oct-26, Low-Medium, not confirmed on the primary page). Commentary describes sovereign spreads as uncomfortably tight, with upgrades concentrated in low-rated names (Medium).
- Gramercy (03-Oct-26): hard-currency sovereigns −1.59%, local debt −1.11%, corporates −0.83% on the week (Medium). Spread levels after August are unsourced.

### Growth backdrop
- IMF WEO, Oct-26 (reported 13-Oct-26): emerging and developing economies 3.7% in 2026 against 4.5% in 2025. Asia 5.1%, SSA 4.5%, LatAm 2.3%, emerging Europe 1.7%. China 4.5% and India 7.0% (FY27). Higher oil prices are named as a headwind (The Week, 13-Oct-26, Medium; secondary report of a tier 2 source).

### Idiosyncratic events
- Romania: S&P affirmed BBB-/A-3 with a negative outlook on 02-Oct-26, citing consolidation risks, large external financing needs and high non-resident holdings (Agerpres, 03-Oct-26, High on the action).
- Brazil: runoff expected 25-Oct-26 (Gramercy, 03-Oct-26). Bloomberg (06-Oct-26) reported EM assets rallying on the first-round result; the body is paywalled.

### Not found
- EMBI GD and CEMBI BD spread levels after August, DXY and UST curve primary prints, EM fund flows after 30-Sep-26 and a systematic scan of rating actions in the last 90 days were not found.
