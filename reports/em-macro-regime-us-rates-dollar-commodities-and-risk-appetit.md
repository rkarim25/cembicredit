## View
- Regime: an oil-supply shock (Hormuz disruption) feeding US inflation and a Fed hiking cycle. The Fed hiked 25bp to 3.75–4.00% on 16-Sep-26, 12–0. September CPI was 3.7% y/y, core 2.6% (Medium-High).
- The 28-Oct-26 FOMC decision was not published at the time of the searches. It is the key unknown and every rates call below is pre-decision.
- Long-end US yields look to have peaked, but this is unconfirmed. The 10y was reported at about 5.30% in early and mid-October, the highest since 2002–2007, then 5.07% on 19-Oct-26 and 4.96% on 22-Oct-26 (Medium-Low, single-source prints).
- Brent was USD 109.55 on 19-Oct-26. A later snippet points to about USD 100 after a weekly loss on ceasefire headlines. The level is unverified (Medium-Low).
- The dollar rose from Aug–Sep lows and broke just below 102 on 01-Oct-26. Its correlation with US yields tightens EM FX carry (Medium).
- EM hard-currency debt held up while Treasuries led the September sell-off. Spreads are described as tight, which leaves little cushion. No spread level after August is sourced (Low-Medium).
- Dispersion is the main implication: importers with negative real rates or thin net reserves (Philippines, Egypt, Pakistan, Kenya, Romania) against exporters with terms-of-trade gains and high real rates (High on dispersion, Medium on persistence).
- Biggest risk: a hawkish FOMC outcome combined with Hormuz or Saudi escalation, which would hit importer FX and HY duration together.
- Confidence: Medium on the regime, High on the cross-section, Low on current market levels and on the FOMC outcome.

## Analysis
### US rates and the Fed
- The FOMC raised the range to 3.75–4.00% on 16-Sep-26 by 12–0, the first hike since 2023 (Federal Reserve release via Schwab, 16-Sep-26, Medium-High).
- The September minutes (07-Oct-26) reportedly say most participants judged another increase would likely be appropriate by year end (search summary, primary text not read, Medium-Low).
- The October meeting runs 27–28 Oct, with no projections or dot plot (Fed calendar aggregators, Medium). The decision was not available when searched.
- September CPI was 3.7% y/y against 3.6% expected, core 2.6% (investinglive.com, 14-Oct-26, Medium). Press put the October hike probability at 47% after the print, against about 80% hold in futures on 05-Oct-26 (gvwire.com, 14-Oct-26; fedratecalc, 05-Oct-26, Medium-Low). The two are not comparable, so the pre-decision pricing is open.
- Long end: the 10y touched about 5.30% in early October, the highest since 2002–2007 (Gramercy, 03-Oct-26; BNN Bloomberg, 01-Oct-26, Medium). It was 5.07% on 19-Oct-26 (FinanceFeeds, Oct-26, Medium-Low) and 4.96% on 22-Oct-26 (search summary, Low-Medium).
- Early-October data: September payrolls +29k against about 90k expected, unemployment 4.2%, August core PCE 3.0% y/y (Gramercy, 03-Oct-26, Medium). Growth has softened while headline inflation has re-accelerated.

### Dollar and EM FX
- DXY rose from a double bottom near 98.50 in Aug–Sep and broke just below 102.00 on 01-Oct-26 (FXStreet, 07-Oct-26, Medium). It rose after the 14-Oct-26 CPI (gvwire.com, Medium-Low). No current print was found.
- The dollar has risen on days when Fed expectations lifted yields (FXStreet, Medium), which ties EM FX to the US rates path.

### Commodities
- Brent closed at USD 109.55 on 19-Oct-26 and settled at USD 109.86 on 16-Oct-26, the highest since May. It was USD 90.1 at end-Aug-26 (Mitrade, 19-Oct-26; Aberdeen, Aug-26, Medium).
- Hormuz flows averaged 11.62m b/d in the week to 13-Oct-26 against a 17.15m b/d baseline (FinanceFeeds, Oct-26, Medium). Later snippets cite Brent near USD 100 on a ceasefire claim despite new Hormuz clashes and a Fujairah strike (Gulf News, Hellenic Shipping News, undated, Low). A USD 74.52 snippet contradicts every other source and is disregarded.
- Houthi claims against Saudi infrastructure are unconfirmed (Low).
- Exporters gain and importers lose, per the structural map (knowledge/themes/commodities-and-em.md, Medium). Terms of trade, 12m to May-26 (macro monitor, 28-Oct-26, High on data, pre-dating the latest oil move): Kuwait +28, Angola +18, Iraq +17, Saudi Arabia +16, Qatar +15, Azerbaijan +14, Kazakhstan +12. Côte d'Ivoire −7, Jordan −6, Thailand −4.
- Gold, copper and agricultural prices were not sourced.

### Cross-section (macro monitor, 28-Oct-26, tier 2)
- High real policy rates: Turkey 12.1, Brazil 9.2, Ghana 8.8, Nigeria 8.4, Ukraine 7.9, Uzbekistan 7.8, Kazakhstan 7.5, Angola 6.0, Colombia 5.7, Hungary 4.0, Serbia 3.5, Mexico 3.0.
- Negative real rates: Rwanda −7.0, Philippines −2.2 (CPI 7.2%, policy 5.00%), Thailand −1.5. CPI dates lag policy dates, so signs may change.
- Romania: CPI 6.2% (Aug-26) against policy 6.50%, real rate 0.3, current account −USD 32.2bn over four quarters, fiscal −6.2% of GDP, growth 0.7% in 2026.
- Thin buffers:
  - Egypt: net-of-drains reserves USD 17.2bn against USD 53.6bn gross, fiscal −12.1%, CA/GDP −4.2%.
  - Pakistan: reserves USD 31.6bn, CPI 10.3%.
  - Kenya: reserves USD 14.7bn, fiscal −6.4%.
  - Turkey: net reserves USD 137.1bn against USD 171.6bn gross, down 8% over 12 months, CA 4Q −USD 38.9bn.
  - Indonesia: net USD 75.5bn against USD 146.5bn gross.
- Deep buffers: India net USD 715.7bn, Saudi Arabia USD 480.8bn, Poland USD 267.4bn, Czech Republic USD 172.0bn.
- Central banks are diverging. The CBRT cut 100bp to 36% on 22-Oct-26, in line with consensus (search summary, Medium). Copom cut 25bp to 13.75% on 16-Sep-26, the fifth consecutive cut. SARB hiked 25bp to 7.25% on 24-Sep-26. Banxico held 6.50% on 24-Sep-26 (knowledge base, Sep-26, Medium-High).

### Risk appetite and EM credit
- HY spreads tightened 13bp and IG 6bp in Aug-26. EMBI GD returned +0.89% (Aberdeen, Aug-26, Medium).
- VanEck's headline says Treasuries led the September sell-off and EM debt held its ground (VanEck, Oct-26, headline only, Low-Medium). Gramercy had hard-currency sovereigns −1.59%, local debt −1.11% and corporates −0.83% on the week to 03-Oct-26 (Medium).
- Q3 flows were about +USD 1.4bn hard currency and +USD 4.1bn local (search summary, Oct-26, Low-Medium, unconfirmed).
- Spread levels after August are unsourced.

### Growth backdrop
- IMF WEO Oct-26 (reported 13-Oct-26): emerging and developing economies 3.7% in 2026 against 4.5% in 2025. China 4.5%. Higher oil is named as a headwind (The Week, 13-Oct-26, Medium; secondary report of a tier 2 source).
