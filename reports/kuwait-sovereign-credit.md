## View
- Kuwait remains a strong AA-/A1 sovereign. Solvency rests on the KIA/FGF asset base, which Fitch says gives the highest net foreign assets to GDP among Fitch-rated sovereigns. This view comes from press and agency summaries, not primary documents (Medium).
- Liquidity and flow metrics have deteriorated in 2026. The FY26/27 draft budget deficit is KD 9.8bn ($31.9bn, about 18% of the repo nominal GDP of USD 172.9bn, our calculation), against a stated breakeven of $90.5/bbl.
- The debt law (KD 30bn, 50 years) ends the no-borrowing stance. Press puts debt rising from about 3% of GDP (FY24/25) to about 12% (FY27/28), still low.
- CBK gross reserves fell 23.4% in 12 months to USD 32.7bn in Aug-26. This is the central bank's liquid pool, not KIA assets.
- The main risk is a drawn rather than rebuilt buffer. Oil disruption plus FGF borrowing, with no fiscal rule and weak governance, would show that assets are funding recurrent deficits.
- The repo WEO fiscal and current account fields cannot be reconciled with the budget or the quarterly balance of payments. They are excluded from the view until verified.
- Confidence: Medium on the credit view, Low on the size of the 2026 deficit and the debt path. No primary documents were opened and no market levels are available.

## Analysis
### Ratings and anchor
- Fitch affirmed AA- with a stable outlook (Aug-26, published by CBK; Kuwait Times summary). It cites the highest sovereign net foreign assets to GDP among rated peers, offset by weak governance and oil dependence (Fitch via CBK/Kuwait Times, Aug-26, Medium; primary PDF not opened).
- S&P is reported at AA-, upgraded from A+ on 21-Nov-25, and Moody's at A1 stable. Both come from search summaries and are unverified against agency releases (Low-Medium).

### Fiscal
- Draft FY26/27 budget: deficit KD 9.8bn ($31.9bn), up 54.7%. Revenues are KD 16.3bn, down 10.5%. Oil revenue is KD 12.8bn, down 16.3%. The oil price assumption is $57/bbl against a breakeven of $90.5/bbl (Arab News, Kuwait Times, TradeArabia, Feb-26, Medium).
- AGBI (Sep-26) puts the FY26/27 deficit at about 20% of GDP against about 5% a year earlier, and links it to Gulf conflict disrupting exports (Medium; no primary source on the disruption).
- Our check: KD 9.8bn ($31.9bn) over repo nominal GDP of USD 172.9bn is 18.5%. This is consistent with press in order of magnitude (derived, Medium).
- The repo WEO 2026E shows a fiscal balance of +27.6% of GDP and a government debt of 22.3% of GDP (IMF WEO via repo, 09-Oct-26). Both conflict with the draft budget and the press debt path. This may be a vintage, sign or fiscal-year-basis issue, and it is unresolved (open item 2).

### Debt
- The public debt law allows up to KD 30bn over 50 years. Gross debt is forecast to rise from 2.9% of GDP (FY24/25) to about 12% (FY27/28) (search summary, Oct-26, Medium).
- AGBI (Sep-26) reports that the government now permits borrowing from the FGF. If used, this would be a policy shift in how reserve assets fund the budget (Medium).
- Eurobond stock, maturities, governing law and CAC terms were not established.

### External
- The quarterly current account was USD 7.9bn, 8.8bn, 7.2bn and 8.6bn over 2025-Q2 to 2026-Q1, a sum of USD 32.5bn (IMF BOP, High). That is about 18.8% of the repo nominal GDP (derived).
- This is consistent in sign with a large surplus but not with the WEO +26% figure. It predates much of the 2026 shock window, so the 2026 current account is unknown.
- Gross reserves incl. gold fell from USD 39.9bn (Mar-26) to USD 32.7bn (Aug-26), down 23.4% over 12 months. Gold is only USD 0.10bn (IMF International Liquidity, High).
- Import cover is 8.4 months (WDI, 2025, High).
- The KWD is stable within a basket peg, at 0.3065-0.3077 per USD from Apr-26 to Sep-26 (BIS, High). The policy rate is unchanged at 3.5% (BIS, Sep-26, High).
- The cause of the reserve fall is not established. It may reflect FX or liquidity management, budget funding or KWD defence. The data cannot distinguish these.

### Monetary and banks
- Real policy rate: the repo shows 3.5%, based on a 0.0% CPI print for Jun-26 that looks like a missing value. Earlier prints were 1.9-2.6% y/y in Jan-May-26 (IMF CPI database, Low for the real rate).
- Banking system, 2026-Q1: CAR 18.46%, tier 1 16.18%, NPL 1.57% (up from 1.40% in Q4-25), provisions to NPL 34.19%, ROE 13.64% (IMF FSI, High). Capital and profitability are strong. Provision coverage is low.

### Politics and governance
- Fitch flags weaker governance than peers. The long gap before the debt law shows slow legislative decision-making (Kuwait Times/Fitch summary, Medium).
- No fiscal rule, subsidy reform or consolidation plan was found.

### Data gaps
- No Cognitive Credit coverage applies to sovereigns. A sovereign model must be built from MoF, CBK, CSB, IMF and prospectus sources.
- The only issuer file in the repo (KFH) is a placeholder bank file with templated financials and was not used.
- Primary documents were not opened: Fitch PDF, S&P and Moody's releases, IMF Article IV, MoF budget.
- Missing items: KIA asset figures, FGF mechanics, total public debt stock and maturity profile, Eurobond terms, index weight, and market levels.

## Where the models disagreed
No material disagreement was recorded in the positions supplied. The one conflict is internal: repo WEO fields against press and balance of payments data. It is not resolvable on the evidence hierarchy (WEO is tier 2 but unverified as to vintage and basis; press is tier 3), so it is held as open item 2.
