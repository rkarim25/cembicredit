## View
- Montenegro is a small, unilaterally euroised, tourism-dependent sovereign. The EU accession path is the main credit anchor.
- Euroisation removes FX depreciation risk and also removes a lender of last resort. Refinancing access and bank liquidity are the binding constraints.
- The 2027-29 window carries the key risk. It contains a reported €750m Dec-27 Eurobond and the reported Jul-28 expiry of the hedge on the USD-denominated China Exim highway loan.
- Direction on fiscal policy is reasonably clear. After "Europe Now" wage and pension measures, a 2023 surplus reportedly turned to deficits of about 3.5-3.7% of GDP. Levels are unverified.
- Reported ratings momentum (S&P B+ Positive, Moody's Ba2 Positive) would support market access if confirmed.
- This run had no web access and no tier 1-2 sources in the brief. No ratings, debt ratios, bond terms or spreads are established.
- Overall confidence is Low to Medium. Structure is reliable, and all numbers need checking against the items above.

## Analysis

### Regime and liquidity
- Montenegro has used the euro unilaterally since 2002. The CBCG cannot create euros, so there is no lender of last resort and no monetary backstop for the sovereign or banks (structural fact, both models, Medium).
- A central bank "financial plan" is a budget document, so the reported €1.24bn of projected reserves is not credible without a source (unsourced, Low). In a euroised economy, much of CBCG "reserves" are bank liquidity deposits. They are not a measure of sovereign liquidity.

### Ratings (unverified)
- One model reported the following, with no URL or filing reference. The other model could not confirm either action, which falls after its knowledge.
  - S&P B+ with a Positive outlook on 27-Feb-26.
  - Moody's upgrade from Ba3 to Ba2 with a Positive outlook on 25-Sep-26.
- Treat both as claims to verify (Low to Medium).
- The attribution of the actions to EU chapter closures is the model's inference, not agency text (Low).
- The Fitch rating is not stated by either model. The ratings fields on the knowledge page remain unfilled.

### Fiscal
- Both models agree on the direction: a surplus in 2023, then deficits after "Europe Now" (Medium).
- Reported levels are a deficit of 3.5-3.7% of GDP, debt of about 61.3% in 2024 and 63.5% in 2025, and up to about 69.4% over the medium term without consolidation. They are attributed to IMF Article IV reports but carry no report reference (Low to Medium).
- The 69.4% figure is a no-consolidation scenario and not a base case.
- The attributed year range for the deficit is inconsistent across the position (2024-25 versus 2025/26).
- The driver is disputed. One model attributes the deterioration to mandated wage and pension increases ("Europe Now 2"), the other to the 2022 tax cuts. Both are plausible and could be partly right (Low).

### External
- The current account deficit is structurally large and mostly FDI-financed, with tourism a large share of GDP (both models, Medium).
- Reported figures are a deficit of 11.0-13.5% of GDP and tourism receipts above 25% of GDP (IMF Article IV 2025, unreferenced, Low to Medium).
- A reported H1-26 CBCG bulletin shows tourism revenue up 6.2% y/y and FDI covering 74.8% of the deficit. It is unsourced and unverifiable here (Low).

### Debt and refinancing
- The reported Eurobond inventory comes from one model's list. The other model recognised the 2027 and 2029 lines and the Apr-25 redemption from memory (Medium for those; Low for the rest):
  - XS2270576700, €750m 2.875%, due 16-Dec-27.
  - XS2050982755, €500m 2.550%, due 03-Oct-29.
  - XS2779850630, $750m 7.25%, issued Mar-24, due 03-Dec-31.
  - XS3037625319, €850m 4.875%, due 01-Apr-32.
  - XS1807201899, €500m, redeemed at maturity on 21-Apr-25.
- The 2031 and 2032 lines are unconfirmed.
- The Bar-Boljare highway loan from China Exim is reported as USD-denominated (original principal reported as $944m). It is reportedly hedged into EUR through Jul-28 at an effective 1.46% (CBCG bulletin and SeeNews Apr-25, Medium).
- The 1.46% is a hedge outcome and not the cost of the underlying loan.
- The risk is hedge-roll and refinancing after Jul-28, which sits inside the 2027-29 window. It is not a resolved item.
- The reported share of 12.98% of external public debt, at about €550-600m, implies external public debt of about €4.2-4.6bn. This has not been reconciled (Low).

### Politics and accession
- EU accession is the main anchor, supporting IPA funding, financing access and policy discipline (Medium).
- Coalition fragmentation is an execution risk for fiscal measures and chapter closures. There is no evidence of willingness-to-pay risk (Low).

### Coverage limits
- No 90-day news search was run by either model, so no statement can be made on what was searched and not found.
- Status of the IMF Article IV, MoF debt strategy, Western Balkans Growth Plan funding and EMBI GD weight is unknown.
- A sovereign model needs to be built from MoF, CBCG, Monstat, IMF and World Bank sources, with market levels from Reza via [BBG].

## Where the models disagreed
1. **Ratings and bond inventory.** Gemini stated ratings and four ISINs as High confidence. Claude had search denied and gave none. Neither supplied a source above tier 4. Resolved as unverified claims at Low to Medium, with BBG verification requested, under rule 2 (no source above tier 4, ask instead of picking a winner).
2. **Exim loan currency.** Claude first said euro-denominated, and later withdrew that. Gemini said USD, hedged to EUR. Resolved as USD with a hedge. The remaining dispute is whether the risk is "managed". Retained as hedge-roll risk after Jul-28, because the expiry falls inside the 2027-29 window.
3. **Fiscal driver.** Gemini said expenditure indexation and Claude said revenue loss from tax cuts. Unresolved, no tier 1-2 source. Direction is agreed; the Article IV text is requested.
4. **Reserves.** Gemini cited €1.24bn from the CBCG financial plan, which Claude disputed as an implausible source. Excluded from the analysis pending a breakdown.
5. **Confidence labels.** Gemini labelled everything High. Claude held most at Medium or Low. Resolved at Low to Medium. The evidence hierarchy requires a linked tier 1-2 source for High, and none was given.
