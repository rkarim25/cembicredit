## View
- Serbia operates as a rising crossover sovereign credit fundamentally anchoring at the threshold of Investment Grade (S&P BBB- Stable; Fitch BB+ Positive; Moody's Ba2 Stable) (Rating Agencies, Jul-26, High).
- Public debt sustainability is sound: general government debt stood at €41.93bn or 43.3% of GDP at end-Aug-2026, well below the 60% statutory threshold (Public Debt Administration, Aug-26, High).
- External liquidity is strongly buffered by record NBS gross foreign exchange reserves of €30.8bn covering over 6 months of imports (National Bank of Serbia, Aug-26, High).
- The current account deficit (~4.1% of GDP in 2026) is fully over-financed by net FDI inflows of €3.5bn–€4.2bn directed into export manufacturing (National Bank of Serbia / IMF, Jun-26, High).
- Fiscal performance remains disciplined under an active IMF Policy Coordination Instrument, with 8M 2026 deficit outperforming targets at RSD 98.4bn (Ministry of Finance, Aug-26, High).
- Monetary policy maintains positive real rates: NBS key policy rate held at 5.75% while headline CPI decelerated to 2.2% YoY in Aug-2026 (National Bank of Serbia, Sep-26, High).
- Primary risk centres on fiscal execution risk and potential borrowing cost escalation from the €17.8bn Expo 2027 infrastructure investment envelope (Ministry of Finance, Aug-26, High).
- Secondary risks comprise domestic political friction surrounding the Jadar lithium project and external trade vulnerability to the broader European manufacturing cycle (Government of Serbia, Sep-26, Medium).
- Overall confidence: High on official fiscal accounts, FX reserve buffers, and debt amortization profile; Low on secondary Eurobond spread curves until terminal levels are verified [BBG].

## Analysis

### Sovereign rating profile and investment-grade trajectory (BBB- / BB+ Positive / Ba2)
- S&P Global Ratings upgraded Serbia to BBB- with a Stable outlook on 04-Oct-2024, assigning the sovereign full investment-grade status on the back of resilient external buffers and disciplined fiscal execution (S&P Global Ratings, 04-Oct-24, High).
- Fitch Ratings affirmed Serbia at BB+ with a Positive outlook on 10-Jul-2026, explicitly noting that a track record of continued fiscal outperformance and FDI-backed export growth could trigger a full upgrade to BBB- within a 12-month window (Fitch Ratings, 10-Jul-26, High).
- Moody's Ratings lags peers at Ba2 with a Stable outlook (affirmed 27-Feb-2026), reflecting institutional governance constraints, geopolitical balancing between the EU, China, and Russia, and unresolved regional disputes over Kosovo (Moody's Ratings, 27-Feb-26, High).
- Achieving a second investment-grade rating from Fitch would formally qualify Serbia for inclusion in major investment-grade aggregate benchmark indices, driving institutional passive demand for its Eurobond curve (Fitch Ratings / Market Sources, Jul-26, Medium).

### Fiscal dynamics, debt trajectory, and the Expo 2027 investment perimeter
- Public debt sustainability is sound: general government debt was reported at €41.93bn (43.3% of GDP) as of end-Aug-2026 by the Public Debt Administration, decreasing from 47.7% at end-2024 and significantly below the 60% Maastricht ceiling (Public Debt Administration, javnidug.gov.rs, Aug-26, High).
- Fiscal execution for the first eight months of 2026 outperformed budget targets: the cumulative general government deficit printed at RSD 98.4bn against a planned deficit of RSD 176.4bn (Ministry of Finance, mfin.gov.rs, Aug-26, High).
- For FY 2026, the general government deficit is targeted at 3.0%–3.5% of GDP, accommodating elevated capital expenditures linked to the specialized international exhibition "Expo 2027" and the associated national infrastructure perimeter ("Leap into the Future - Serbia 2027") (Ministry of Finance, Aug-26, High).
- The total Expo 2027 investment perimeter is budgeted at €17.8bn across 2024–2027; while boosting economic capacity, large non-concessional borrowing envelopes could elevate contingent liabilities and reverse recent debt-to-GDP declines if cost overruns occur (Ministry of Finance / IMF, Jun-26, High).

### External position, central bank FX reserves (€30.8bn), and FDI coverage
- The National Bank of Serbia (NBS) reported record gross foreign exchange reserves of €30.8bn at end-Aug-2026 (up €303.5m month-on-month), providing over 6.2 months of goods and services import cover and significantly exceeding the gross external financing requirement (National Bank of Serbia, nbs.rs, Aug-26, High).
- Net foreign exchange reserves (gross reserves minus commercial bank required FX reserves and other NBS obligations) stood robust at €26.2bn at end-Aug-2026 (National Bank of Serbia, Aug-26, High).
- The current account deficit is projected at 4.1% of GDP in 2026, widened moderately by imports of capital equipment for energy and transport infrastructure (National Bank of Serbia / IMF, Jun-26, High).
- External deficit financing remains fully covered by non-debt-creating flows: net FDI inflows are projected to reach €3.8bn–€4.2bn in 2026 (following €4.5bn in 2024), driven by automotive manufacturing, electric vehicle battery supply chains, and industrial manufacturing (National Bank of Serbia, Aug-26, High).

### Monetary policy stance, inflation deceleration, and dinar stability
- The NBS Executive Board maintained its key policy rate at 5.75% in Sep-2026 and Oct-2026, following cumulative rate cuts totaling 75 bps implemented across mid-2024 (National Bank of Serbia, 10-Sep-26, High).
- Headline consumer price inflation decelerated to 2.2% YoY in August 2026, dropping firmly into the lower half of the NBS target band (3.0% ± 1.5%), driven by lower food and global energy netbacks (Statistical Office of the Republic of Serbia, stat.gov.rs, Sep-26, High).
- Ex-ante real policy rates remain firmly positive at approximately 2.0%–2.5%, providing a solid yield cushion to protect dinar-denominated domestic securities from sudden capital outflows (National Bank of Serbia, Sep-26, High).
- The NBS maintains a de facto stabilized exchange rate regime for the Serbian Dinar against the Euro (EUR/RSD around 117.0–117.2), intervening symmetrically in the interbank market (net purchaser of FX in 2025–2026) to anchor inflation expectations (National Bank of Serbia, Aug-26, High).

### Public debt management, Eurobond curve, and liability management
- In May 2026, the Republic of Serbia executed an active liability management transaction, tendering and buying back €870.8m of its €2.0bn 3.125% Eurobond due May 2027 (XS2170186923), reducing the outstanding maturity wall to €1.13bn (Public Debt Administration, May-26, High).
- The buyback was financed via a successful triple-tranche international offering: €1.0bn 5Y Eurobond at 4.25% due May 2031 (XS3363425383), €750m 12Y Green Eurobond at 4.875% due May 2038 (XS3363425466), and $1.25bn 10Y Eurobond at 5.75% due May 2036 (XS3363387351) (Public Debt Administration, May-26, High).
- To eliminate foreign exchange risk on the dollar tranche, Serbia simultaneously entered into cross-currency swap arrangements converting the $1.25bn liability into Euros at an effective fixed coupon rate of 4.66% (Ministry of Finance, May-26, High).
- External debt represents approximately 55% of total general government debt, with EUR-denominated debt making up ~58% of the total, RSD debt ~23%, and USD debt ~13% (all non-EUR foreign currency exposures subject to active hedging) (Public Debt Administration, Aug-26, High).

### Institutional risks, geopolitical balancing, and SOE contingent liabilities
- Structural credit risk stems from geopolitical friction, notably ongoing tensions surrounding Kosovo normalization talks and EU alignment mandates concerning sanctions policy (European Commission / Oxford Economics, Sep-26, Medium).
- Domestic political opposition and widespread civic protests surrounding the proposed Jadar lithium mining concession (operated by Rio Tinto) present legislative and execution friction for foreign investment policy (Press Releases / Balkan Insight, Sep-26, Medium).
- State-owned enterprises (Elektroprivreda Srbije - EPS and Srbijagas) have seen operational restructuring under IMF guidance, transitioning EPS to a joint-stock company; however, state guarantees on SOE commercial facilities represent ~€1.8bn in contingent liabilities (Ministry of Finance / IMF, Jun-26, High).
- Relations with the IMF remain strongly anchored under a 36-month non-financing Policy Coordination Instrument (PCI) running through late 2027, providing policy continuity and fiscal target surveillance (IMF, Press Release No. 26/208, 15-Jun-26, High).
