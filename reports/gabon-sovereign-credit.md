## View
Gabon is a distressed CCC-/Caa2 credit where near-term external default risk was pushed out to 2031 by repaying 2025 maturities and issuing a USD 920m 2033 Eurobond in Jul-26.
Political transition risks normalized after the Nov-24 referendum and the Apr-25 election of President Brice Oligui Nguema, restoring formal constitutional governance.
Liquidity remains acute with gross financing needs of 15%–20% of GDP amidst regional CEMAC commercial banking saturation and high domestic treasury yields.
Concessional financing is absent: IMF engagement remains limited to technical surveillance, with the Sep-26 staff mission concluding without a financing agreement.
The primary risk is oil price downside below USD 65/bbl cutting ~60% of fiscal revenue and precipitating payment stops on regional debt or arrears accumulation.
Domestic supplier arrears remain an unquantified fiscal drag and contingent liability pending final audit verification.
Overall view confidence: Medium.

## Analysis

### 1. External position and market access
Gabon secured temporary external debt relief through commercial market access in mid-2026, successfully refinancing the USD 6.95% Eurobond due Jun-25 (XS1245960684) with a USD 920m 9.375% Eurobond due Aug-33 (XS3460790887) (CBonds, Jul-26, High). This liability management extended the sovereign's commercial maturity profile, leaving no external commercial bond maturities until the Feb-31 notes (XS2113615228) (CBonds, Jul-26, High). However, the elevated 9.375% coupon and secondary 2031 trading levels (~920 bps Z-spread) reflect prohibitive marginal borrowing costs (CBonds, Jul-26, Medium). External reserve pooling via the BEAC regional central bank provides minimal dedicated foreign currency cushion, leaving external debt service dependent on sustained hydrocarbon export revenues (IMF, 24-May-24, Medium).

### 2. Fiscal trajectory and debt sustainability
Public finances are constrained by gross financing requirements of 15%–20% of GDP annually, driven by heavy domestic debt roll requirements and rigid recurrent spending (Fitch, 22-May-26, High). Total public debt hovers near 70% of GDP, with debt service absorbing substantial budgetary resources (IMF, 24-May-24, Medium). Revenue remains structurally vulnerable to commodity terms of trade, with crude oil generating ~60% of fiscal receipts and ~75% of merchandise exports (IMF, 24-May-24, High). Domestic expenditure arrears to local suppliers and contractors remain a persistent contingent liability that continues to weigh on private sector liquidity and fiscal transparency (Moody's, 18-Jun-26, Medium).

### 3. Monetary, banking sector and CEMAC constraints
Monetary policy is governed by the BEAC within the CFA franc peg to the euro, shielding Gabon from nominal currency depreciation risk but removing monetary financing options (BEAC, Jun-26, High). Domestic borrowing is constrained by regional CEMAC debt market saturation, where Gabonese commercial banks face regulatory single-name sovereign exposure limits and elevated local yields (Moody's, 18-Jun-26, High). Fitch downgraded Gabon's local-currency debt rating to CC in Dec-25 and affirmed LTFC at CCC- in May-26, reflecting recurrent delays in domestic debt service and tight banking liquidity (Fitch, 22-May-26, High).

### 4. Political transition and governance
Institutional and political governance stabilized with the conclusion of the post-2023 transitional period under President Brice Oligui Nguema (MoI Gabon, Apr-25, High). The transitional authority enacted a new constitution via referendum on 16-Nov-24 with 91.6% approval, followed by the presidential election on 12-Apr-25 won by Oligui Nguema with 94.85% of the vote (MoI Gabon, Apr-25, High). The transition to the Fifth Republic has normalized international bilateral relations, removing immediate risks of regional sanctions from ECCAS or the African Union (MoI Gabon, Apr-25, Medium).

### 5. Multilateral engagement and IMF relationship
Gabon lacks a disbursing IMF programme to anchor macro adjustment following the expiration of the 2021 Extended Fund Facility (IMF, 25-Sep-26, High). An IMF mission led by Aliona Cebotari conducted technical surveillance in Libreville from 14 to 25 Sep-26 to examine the mid-2026 public debt audit, but discussions concluded without a Staff-Level Agreement (SLA) or formal board timeline (IMF, 25-Sep-26, High). Concessional multilateral inflows remain minimal pending structural commitments on fuel subsidy rationalisation and governance benchmarks (IMF, 25-Sep-26, Medium).

### 6. Eurobond curve structure and recovery valuation
The sovereign capital structure consists of commercial Eurobonds (2031s, 2033s), multilateral debt, Paris Club and bilateral loans, and the innovative USD 500m 2038 Blue Bond (USU35914AA51) backed by a US DFC political risk insurance policy (CBonds, Jul-26, High). The DFC-guaranteed 2038 notes trade at a distinct yield premium to unsecured Eurobonds due to credit enhancement. Unsecured bonds trade in distressed high-single-digit yield territory, where pricing reflects long-term debt sustainability risks rather than imminent default (CBonds, Jul-26, Medium). In a downside restructuring scenario under the G20 Common Framework or comparable treatment, recovery values would likely center on the 55c–65c range based on historical Sub-Saharan African oil-exporter precedents (Precedent Analysis, Oct-26, Low).
