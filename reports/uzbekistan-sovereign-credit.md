## View
Uzbekistan is a reforming CIS sovereign rated BB (Stable) by S&P (22-May-26), BB (Positive) by Fitch (03-Jun-26), and Ba2 (Stable) by Moody's (25-Jun-26, upgraded from Ba3).
External liquidity is exceptionally robust, with CBU reserves reaching $72.13bn as of 01-Sep-26 (>12 months of import cover), heavily exceeding total public debt.
Public debt is modest at 27.0% of GDP ($48.31bn total; $40.71bn external) as of 01-Jul-26, well below regional peers and IMF debt sustainability thresholds.
Over 55% of external public debt is owed to concessional multilateral institutions (World Bank, ADB), mitigating commercial refinancing and interest rate risks.
Economic expansion is among the strongest in CEEMEA, with real GDP growing 7.7% in 2025 and 8.7% y/y in Q1 2026, supported by investment and gold export receipts.
Fiscal consolidation is progressing as the consolidated deficit narrowed to 2.1% of GDP in 2025, driven by landmark phased domestic energy tariff deregulation.
Monetary policy is orthodox: the CBU maintains its policy rate at 14.0% against 6.2% y/y inflation (August 2026), delivering a high positive real rate of ~7.8%.
The primary vulnerability is extreme gold concentration (~90% of CBU reserves), exposing external asset valuations to sharp global bullion price reversals.
Overall credit conviction is High: sovereign default risk is minimal, reform momentum is institutionalized, and rating trajectory is firmly tilted toward BB+/Ba1.

## Analysis

### Sovereign rating trajectory and institutional reform momentum
Uzbekistan's credit rating trajectory is on an active upward path, reflecting sustained structural reforms, governance modernization, and prudent debt management (Moody's Ratings, 25-Jun-26, High). Moody's upgraded the long-term issuer rating from Ba3 to Ba2 with a Stable outlook on 25-Jun-26, citing institutional capacity building, enhanced fiscal frameworks, and economic diversification (Moody's Ratings, 25-Jun-26, High). Fitch affirmed its BB rating on 03-Jun-26 while revising the outlook to Positive, highlighting strong macroeconomic execution, robust external buffers, and progress in price deregulation (Fitch Ratings, 03-Jun-26, High). S&P Global Ratings affirmed its BB/B sovereign ratings on 22-May-26 with a Stable outlook, balancing low net debt and ample liquidity against relatively low GDP per capita and commodity exposure (S&P Global Ratings, 22-May-26, High).

### External balance sheet and reserve asset quality
The sovereign's external balance sheet functions as a fortress credit anchor (Central Bank of Uzbekistan, 01-Sep-26, High). Official gross international reserves held by the Central Bank of Uzbekistan (CBU) expanded to $72.13bn as of 01-Sep-26, providing over 12 months of merchandise import cover and positioning Uzbekistan in an unambiguous net external creditor posture (Central Bank of Uzbekistan, 01-Sep-26, High). Gold holdings constitute ~90% of total reserves (~439 tonnes), reflecting aggressive domestic production monetization where the CBU purchased nearly 50 tonnes of gold year-to-date through August 2026, ranking third globally among central bank buyers (Central Bank of Uzbekistan, 01-Sep-26, High). While elevated gold prices have dramatically boosted reserve values, this extreme asset concentration represents the principal vulnerability in the external account, as a sustained gold market correction would compress headline reserves (IMF Article IV, 01-Jun-26, Medium). To address this, the CBU has initiated portfolio diversification into US Treasuries and liquid sovereign paper (Central Bank of Uzbekistan, 01-Sep-26, High).

### Public debt structure and concessional financing profile
Uzbekistan exhibits one of the cleanest debt profiles across CEEMEA (Ministry of Economy and Finance, 01-Jul-26, High). Total public debt stood at $48.31bn as of 01-Jul-26, corresponding to 27.0% of GDP, down from 31.9% at end-2025 due to rapid nominal GDP expansion (Ministry of Economy and Finance, 01-Jul-26, High). External debt represents $40.71bn (84.3% of total), while domestic debt stands at $7.60bn (15.7%) (Ministry of Economy and Finance, 01-Jul-26, High). Crucially, commercial market risk is heavily diluted: over 55% of external debt is held by multilateral development institutions (World Bank, Asian Development Bank, Islamic Development Bank) on highly concessional terms featuring 20–30 year amortisation horizons and low weighted-average coupons (Ministry of Economy and Finance, 01-Jul-26, High). Domestic borrowing in national currency (UZS) is steadily scaling via regular Treasury bond auctions on the CBU platform, deepening local financial markets (Ministry of Economy and Finance, 01-Jul-26, Medium).

### Fiscal consolidation and domestic energy tariff reform
Fiscal consolidation is progressing in line with statutory targets (Ministry of Economy and Finance, 31-Dec-25, High). The consolidated budget deficit narrowed to 2.1% of GDP in 2025 from 3.0% in 2024, supported by buoyant mining tax receipts and landmark retail energy tariff adjustments that phased out blanket subsidies for natural gas and electricity (IMF Article IV, 01-Jun-26, High). The government is targeting a consolidated deficit of ~2.5% of GDP in 2026, anchored by a statutory 60% of GDP public debt ceiling enacted in the State Debt Law (Ministry of Economy and Finance, 01-Jul-26, High). The primary fiscal risk is off-budget contingent liabilities across state-owned enterprises (SOEs) in the energy, metallurgy, and aviation sectors (Uzbekneftegaz, Uztransgaz, Navoi MMC, Almalyk MMC), where state guarantees require ongoing monitoring (IMF Article IV, 01-Jun-26, Medium).

### Monetary orthodoxy and inflation dynamics
The Central Bank of Uzbekistan maintains a credible orthodox monetary framework (Central Bank of Uzbekistan, 12-Sep-26, High). The CBU held its key policy rate unchanged at 14.0% per annum in September 2026, preserving a high positive ex-ante real policy rate of ~7.8% against annual headline CPI inflation of 6.2% in August 2026 (Central Bank of Uzbekistan, 12-Sep-26, High). Inflation has decelerated following the initial pass-through of utility tariff hikes, aligning monetary conditions with the medium-term 5.0% inflation target (Central Bank of Uzbekistan, 12-Sep-26, High). The Uzbek Som operates under a managed crawling peg against the US dollar without a parallel market spread, supporting systematic dedollarization of banking sector deposits (Central Bank of Uzbekistan, 01-Sep-26, High).

### Commercial Eurobond curve and market positioning
Uzbekistan's marketable Eurobond curve comprises well-spaced USD and EUR benchmark issuances (Luxembourg Stock Exchange, 08-Oct-26, High). Key outstanding liquid lines include: EUR 5.10% due 25-Feb-2029 (ISIN XS3008639810), USD 7.85% due 12-Oct-2028 (ISIN XS2701166717), USD 3.70% due 25-Nov-2030 (ISIN XS2263765856), USD 3.90% due 19-Oct-2031 (ISIN XS2365195978), and USD 6.90% due 28-Feb-2032 (ISIN XS2827783437) (Luxembourg Stock Exchange, 08-Oct-26, High). The sovereign faces no commercial Eurobond redemptions before October 2028, eliminating near-term refinancing cliffs and providing insulation from global capital market volatility (Ministry of Economy and Finance, 01-Jul-26, High).

### Multilateral surveillance and IMF relationship
Uzbekistan does not maintain an active borrowing arrangement with the International Monetary Fund, as its balance-of-payments position requires no exceptional balance sheet support (IMF Country Portal, 08-Oct-26, High). Bilateral engagement takes place through regular annual Article IV surveillance consultations and technical assistance missions covering public financial management, tax administration, and monetary transmission (IMF Country Portal, 08-Oct-26, High).
