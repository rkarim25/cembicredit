## View
Underweight / neutral duration on hard-currency sovereign debt; external default risk is low through 2027 following the full repayment of the €700m Eurobond in July 2026, but medium-term solvency remains constrained by fiscal dominance and absence of structural reforms.
Key drivers: Absence of major international bond maturities until the $150m 8.25% note in September 2027 provides near-term cashflow breathing space.
FX reserves: BCT net reserves stand at TND 23.7bn (~$7.6bn), covering 92 days of imports as of 06-Oct-26, down from 104 days in 2025 due to an expanding energy import deficit (TND 11.3bn in 8M26).
Domestic financing: Shut out of international debt markets; sovereign funds a ~5.2% of GDP deficit via domestic bank liquidity and direct Central Bank of Tunisia (BCT) exceptional advances.
IMF status: Programme remains frozen; President Kais Saied rejects IMF conditionality on public wage bill and subsidy cuts under his economic self-reliance doctrine.
Monetary stance: BCT policy rate held at 7.00% on 07-Oct-26, preserving positive ex-post real rates against September headline inflation of 5.6% y/y.
Ratings: Fitch B- (Stable) affirmed 08-Sep-26; Moody's Caa1 (Stable) affirmed 16-Jul-26.
Biggest risk: Rapid erosion of FX reserves below the critical 90-day threshold if energy and food import bills widen further.
Overall confidence: Medium-High.

## Analysis

### External position and FX reserves
Tunisia's external liquidity position has stabilized temporarily following the successful redemption of its heavy 2025–2026 Eurobond amortisation cluster (Ministry of Finance, 15-Jul-26, High). Banque Centrale de Tunisie (BCT) daily indicators report net foreign exchange reserves at TND 23.7bn (approximately $7.6bn) as of 06-Oct-26, corresponding to 92 days of import cover (BCT, 06-Oct-26, High). This represents a decline from 104 days of import cover at end-2025, driven primarily by an expanding energy trade deficit, which widened to TND 11.3bn in the first eight months of 2026 compared to TND 8.8bn in 8M25 (BCT, 06-Oct-26, High). While tourism receipts and worker remittances have demonstrated structural resilience, external reserves remain precarious and vulnerable to terms-of-trade shocks, hovering just above the conventional 90-day adequacy threshold (BCT, 06-Oct-26, Medium).

### Debt profile and maturity schedule
The sovereign's hard-currency debt maturity schedule is exceptionally light through mid-2027 following the settlement of the €700m 6.375% Eurobond (XS2023698553) on 15-Jul-26 and the prior $1.0bn repayment in January 2025 (Ministry of Finance, 15-Jul-26, High). The only remaining benchmark international debt security in the near term is the $150m 8.25% bond due 19-Sep-27 (US066716AB78) (Ministry of Finance, 15-Jul-26, High). Consequently, market-driven default risk over the next 12 months is minimal. However, external debt service to multilateral and bilateral creditors (notably AFREXIMBANK, Algeria, and Saudi Arabia) remains steady, requiring continuous foreign exchange allocations in the absence of market access (Fitch, 08-Sep-26, Medium).

### Fiscal position and domestic financing
Public finances remain the sovereign's fundamental structural vulnerability. Public debt is estimated at approximately 82% of GDP, with the 2026 fiscal deficit projected at ~5.2% of GDP (Ministry of Finance, 08-Sep-26, Medium). Shut out from external commercial bond markets, the Treasury has financed the fiscal gap through aggressive domestic commercial bank borrowing and extraordinary direct central bank monetization (BCT, 07-Oct-26, High). Following legislation enacted in February 2024 granting the BCT authority to extend direct exceptional financing to the Treasury, domestic credit expansion has increasingly crowded out private sector lending and elevated sovereign-bank nexus contagion risks (Moody's, 16-Jul-26, High).

### Monetary policy and inflation
The Central Bank of Tunisia maintains a prudent and orthodox monetary stance to counter monetization pressures. At its Executive Board meeting on 07-Oct-26, the BCT held its key policy interest rate unchanged at 7.00% (BCT, 07-Oct-26, High). Headline inflation eased to 5.6% year-on-year in September 2026, down from 6.7% earlier in the year, although food price inflation remains stubborn at 13.0% y/y (INS, 05-Oct-26, High). With policy rates at 7.00% against headline inflation of 5.6%, the ex-post real policy rate stands at +140 bps, providing critical support to the Tunisian dinar (TND) and preventing disorderly currency depreciation (BCT / INS, 07-Oct-26, High).

### IMF and multilateral relations
Institutional support from the International Monetary Fund remains entirely stalled. The $1.9bn Extended Fund Facility (EFF) agreed at staff level in October 2022 was never submitted to or approved by the IMF Executive Board due to political resistance to structural reform benchmarks (IMF, 08-Sep-26, High). President Kais Saied has consistently rejected external "diktats", specifically opposing required cuts to the public sector wage bill (which exceeds 13% of GDP) and the elimination of consumer energy and food subsidies (Official Presidential Statements, Oct-24, High). Bilateral funding from regional partners (Algeria and Gulf allies) has filled selective funding gaps on an ad-hoc basis, but cannot substitute for multilateral program scale (Fitch, 08-Sep-26, Medium).

### Political economy and institutional governance
Following the October 2024 presidential election, President Kais Saied consolidated absolute executive authority under the 2022 constitution (Official Gazette, 08-Oct-24, High). Economic policymaking is characterized by a doctrine of national sovereignty and self-reliance, prioritizing domestic social stability over orthodox fiscal consolidation. While political stability has suppressed social unrest in the near term, the lack of structural economic transformation caps medium-term GDP growth at 1.0%–1.5% and leaves long-term debt sustainability dependent on perpetual domestic financial repression (Moody's, 16-Jul-26, High).
