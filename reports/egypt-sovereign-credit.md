## View
- Egypt is a stable-to-improving B credit with a heavy interest burden. Hold as a carry credit and do not add duration before the Eighth Review (about mid-Dec-26). Medium confidence.
- S&P affirmed B/Stable on 11-Oct-26 and cites strengthened external buffers, exchange-rate flexibility and energy price adjustments (Arab News, 11-Oct-26, Medium). Moody's Caa1/Positive remains the lagging rating and the main upgrade candidate (Medium).
- Disinflation continues. September headline CPI was 12.8% y/y (12.7% in Aug) and urban 13.9% (14.5%). Core was 14.1% (14.9%). The CBE held the overnight deposit rate at 19.00% on 24-Sep-26, so the real rate is 6.2pp against headline and 5.1pp against urban, ex-post (CAPMAS via press; CBE, Medium).
- The IMF anchor holds. The Seventh Review was completed in late Jul-26 with $1.8B approved. One review remains and no successor is documented (IMF, 31-Jul-26, High).
- Interest absorbs 67% of revenue per S&P, against "more than 50%" in the MoF/IIF lower bound. S&P puts the primary surplus at 4.9% of GDP, against 4.0% budgeted by the MoF (Medium).
- Biggest risk: hot money and programme exit. Foreign holdings of local securities are $34.5B ($9.5B left in the recent shock). Reserves net of one-year drains were only $17.2B at May-26 (S&P via press; IMF template, Medium).
- No market levels were supplied, so there is no relative-value call.

## Analysis

### Ratings
- S&P: B/Stable, affirmed 11-Oct-26 per press. The stated upgrade trigger is faster debt reduction through FDI and asset sales (Arab News, 11-Oct-26, Medium). The previous date of Apr-26 is superseded.
- Fitch: B/Stable, reported affirmed Oct-26 (repo rating summary, Low to Medium). Moody's: Caa1/Positive since 18-Mar-24, reported affirmed Apr-26 (repo rating summary, Low to Medium).
- Eurobonds listed: 7.600% 01-Mar-29 ($2.0B), 9.450% 04-Feb-33 ($1.5B), 6.875% 30-Apr-40 ($1.25B), 8.500% 31-Jan-47 ($2.5B) (LuxSE / MoF, Oct-26, Medium). The ladder is incomplete. The only issuance found was the $1.5B sukuk in Oct-25 and planned EUR issuance in Feb-26 (press, Low).

### Monetary and inflation
- The CBE held the overnight deposit rate at 19.00% and the lending rate at 20.00% on 24-Sep-26 (CBE via press, 24-Sep-26, Medium). The next MPC is on 12-Nov-26.
- September nationwide headline CPI was 12.8% y/y against 12.7% in August, with a 1.5% m/m rise (CAPMAS via Arab Finance, Oct-26, Medium).
- Urban CPI eased to 13.9% from 14.5% (Daily News Egypt, 10-Oct-26, Medium). Core eased to 14.1% from 14.9% (CBE via Zawya, Oct-26, Medium).
- The press reports differ on the m/m rate: 1.5% nationwide against 1.3% urban. They are different series, not a conflict.
- The data layer's "CPI 14.539% Aug-26" is the urban series. The headline series is 12.7%. No forward inflation expectations are sourced, so no true ex-ante real rate is available.
- USD/EGP moved from 47.15 in Feb-26 to 51.95 in Mar-26 and 50.25 in Jul-26 (BIS, High). S&P describes a "temporary post-shock weakening" (Medium).

### External position
- Net reserves were $57.35B at end-Sep-26 with import cover over 7.5 months (CBE, 07-Oct-26, Medium). IMF gross reserves incl. gold were $53.559B at Jul-26, up 14.7% y/y (IMF IL, High). S&P cites $54.5B for Sep-26 (Medium). The definitions differ and the series are not comparable.
- Reserves ex-gold rose from $28.62B to $36.42B between Feb-26 and Jul-26. Gold fell from $21.502B to $17.139B (IMF IL, High). The cause of the gold decline cannot be separated from the data (Low).
- The current account deficit over the last four quarters was $16.76B. The IMF WEO 2026E is -4.2% of GDP, and S&P puts fuel and gas at about 30% of goods imports (IMF BOP and WEO, High; S&P via press, Medium).
- Reserves net of one-year predetermined drains were $17.224B at May-26, up from $5.8B in Dec-25 (IMF reserve template, High).
- Foreign holdings of local securities are $34.5B, with $9.5B having left in the recent shock (S&P via press, Medium). This is the main flow vulnerability.
- The parallel FX premium is reported at 0% since Mar-24 (CBE / commercial banks, Oct-26, Medium).

### Fiscal and debt
- The primary surplus was 3.5% of GDP (EGP 629B) in FY24/25 and is budgeted at 4.0% for FY25/26 (MoF, Sep-26, Medium). S&P puts it at 4.9% of GDP and FY25/26 growth at 5.1% (Arab News, 11-Oct-26, Medium). The gap probably reflects outturn against budget or a different definition. The final FY25/26 accounts were not retrieved.
- Interest is 67% of revenue per S&P, against "more than 50%" in the MoF/IIF note (Medium). Either figure is far above the 30% level at which defaults cluster.
- Budget-sector debt is about 79.7% of GDP at Q2-26, down from 96.4% in FY23/24 (MoF / IIF, Jun-26, Medium). The IMF WEO 2026E overall deficit is -12.1% of GDP, with real growth of 4.2% and nominal GDP of $429.6B (IMF WEO, Medium).
- The debt-stabilising primary balance cannot be computed without the effective interest rate on debt (data gap).

### IMF and official sector
- Staff-level agreement on the Seventh Review was announced on 30-Jun-26, at about $1.6B including $1.5B EFF (Asharq Al-Awsat, 30-Jun-26, Medium).
- The Board completed the review with $1.8B approved ($1.5B EFF, $272M RSF). The repo gives 31-Jul-26 and an Ahram snippet implies 29-Jul-26. The date is unresolved; I use the repo date, which is from the IMF release (IMF, 31-Jul-26, High).
- The Eighth and final review is expected by mid-Dec-26. The repo says 15-Dec-26, and no primary source was found (Medium to Low). No successor arrangement is documented.

## Where the models disagreed
1. Interest burden: the brief's "50%+" against S&P's 67%. Only one position was supplied to this adjudication. I record both figures with their sources, because "more than 50%" is a lower bound and does not contradict 67%.
2. Primary surplus: the 4.0% budget against S&P's 4.9%. Neither is above tier 3 for the S&P figure, so both are shown and the final MoF accounts are an open gap. No averaging was applied.
3. S&P affirmation date: the repo has Apr-26 and the press has 11-Oct-26. The more recent and more specific source wins at Medium confidence, with confirmation requested (item 6).
4. Suez receipts: the brief's -50% to -60% against Capmad's +56.7% in Aug-26. Both sit at tier 3 to 4, so the point is an open item (item 8).
