## View
- Egypt has successfully transitioned from an acute balance-of-payments crisis into managed macroeconomic stabilization following the March 2024 EGP float, the $35B ADQ Ras El Hekma investment, and expanded multilateral support (IMF, Jul-26, High).
- Sovereign credit ratings reflect structural rehabilitation: S&P upgraded Egypt to B (Stable) on 10-Oct-25, aligning with Fitch at B (Stable, upgraded 01-Nov-24), while Moody's maintains Caa1 with Positive outlook (Rating Agencies, Oct-26, High).
- Net international reserves reached an all-time record of $57.35B at end-September 2026, delivering over 7.5 months of import cover and eliminating parallel FX premiums (CBE, 07-Oct-26, High).
- Monetary policy remains orthodox: the Central Bank of Egypt held the overnight deposit rate at 19.00% on 24-Sep-26, securing an ex-ante real policy rate of +450 to +500 bps against August urban CPI of 14.5% YoY (CBE / CAPMAS, Sep-26, High).
- The IMF completed the Seventh Review under the $8B EFF on 31-Jul-26, disbursing $1.8B ($1.5B EFF, $272M RSF), anchoring reform continuity ahead of the final Q4 2026 review (IMF, 31-Jul-26, High).
- Fiscal discipline is evident with primary surpluses tracking 3.5%–4.0% of GDP, bringing budget sector debt down toward ~79.7% of GDP (Ministry of Finance, Sep-26, High).
- However, extreme interest burden remains Egypt's paramount credit constraint: interest expenditure absorbs over 50% of budget revenue, leaving the debt profile highly sensitive to rollover rates (Ministry of Finance / IMF, Jun-26, High).
- External headwinds persist from Red Sea security disruptions, which continue to depress Suez Canal toll revenues by 50%–60% (~$5B–$6B annual drag) (Suez Canal Authority, Sep-26, High).
- Downside risks centre on regional Middle East escalation triggering capital flight, renewed reform fatigue around energy subsidies, or premature CBE rate cuts (S&P / Fitch, Sep-26, Medium).
- Overall confidence: High on external liquidity and currency convertibility, Medium on medium-term debt affordability, Low on secondary USD Eurobond levels until verified on Bloomberg.

## Analysis

### External debt, FX reserves, and balance of payments
- Egypt's net international reserves (NIR) reached a record high of $57.35B at end-September 2026 according to the Central Bank of Egypt (CBE, 07-Oct-26, High).
- The foreign reserve build has expanded import cover to over 7.5 months of merchandise imports, fully restoring external liquidity buffers following acute 2023–2024 shortages (CBE, Sep-26, High).
- The commercial banking sector's net foreign asset (NFA) position has remained consistently positive since April 2024, eliminating the legacy structural deficit that previously exceeded -$29B (CBE / IMF, Aug-26, High).
- Gross external debt stands at approximately $152.9B, having consolidated significantly from peak levels following the conversion of Gulf bilateral central bank deposits into equity investments under the $35B UAE Ras El Hekma transaction (CBE / Ministry of Planning, Jun-26, High).
- Suez Canal receipts remain severely impaired due to ongoing Red Sea maritime security threats and vessel diversions around the Cape of Good Hope, operating at a 50%–60% discount to pre-crisis volumes and costing the sovereign an estimated $5B to $6B in annualized FX receipts (Suez Canal Authority, Sep-26, High).
- The Suez Canal shortfall is partially offset by resilient worker remittances (rebounding strongly toward ~$30B annualized post-currency unification) and steady tourism receipts (CBE Balance of Payments, Jun-26, High).

### Monetary policy, inflation trajectory, and currency dynamics
- The Central Bank of Egypt held its benchmark overnight deposit rate at 19.00% and overnight lending rate at 20.00% at its Monetary Policy Committee meeting on 24-Sep-26 (CBE, 24-Sep-26, High).
- Urban consumer price inflation decelerated to 14.5% YoY in August 2026, while nationwide headline inflation printed at 12.7% YoY, confirming substantial disinflation from peak rates exceeding 35% in early 2024 (CAPMAS, 10-Sep-26, High).
- The ex-ante real policy rate currently stands at +450 to +500 bps against 12-month forward inflation expectations, establishing robust real carry for domestic local currency assets (CBE / Reuters Survey, Sep-26, High).
- The Egyptian Pound (EGP) has traded freely in a stable band of 48.20–48.75 per USD, with parallel FX premiums completely eliminated since the March 2024 liberalization (CBE / Commercial Banks, Oct-26, High).
- The next CBE MPC interest rate review meeting is scheduled for 12-Nov-26, where the central bank is widely expected to preserve rate stability to anchor inflation expectations (CBE Calendar, Sep-26, Medium).

### Fiscal consolidation, interest burden, and debt sustainability
- The Ministry of Finance achieved an audited primary budget surplus of 3.5% of GDP (EGP 629B) for FY2024/2025 and is tracking a target primary surplus of 4.0% of GDP for FY2025/2026 (Ministry of Finance, Sep-26, High).
- General budget sector debt has declined to approximately 79.7% of GDP in 2026, down sharply from 96.4% of GDP in FY2023/2024, aided by primary surpluses and nominal GDP expansion (Ministry of Finance / IIF, Jun-26, High).
- Debt affordability remains Egypt's paramount structural credit vulnerability: domestic interest service consumes more than 50% of total budget revenue, restricting discretionary public investment (Ministry of Finance / IMF Country Report, Jun-26, High).
- The Ministry of Finance has introduced a debt ceiling framework and is actively seeking to lengthen local currency debt maturities, reducing rollover vulnerability on short-term T-bills (Ministry of Finance, Aug-26, High).
- Phased rationalisation of fuel and electricity subsidies continues to advance per IMF structural benchmarks, generating targeted fiscal savings despite public sensitivity to utility tariffs (Ministry of Petroleum / Cabinet, Jul-26, High).

### Multilateral programmes, IMF EFF, and official sector financing
- The IMF Executive Board completed the Seventh Review under Egypt's Extended Fund Facility (EFF) arrangement on 31-Jul-26, enabling an immediate disbursement of approximately $1.8B ($1.5B from the EFF and $272M under the Resilience and Sustainability Facility) (IMF Press Release No. 26/280, 31-Jul-26, High).
- The IMF programme total was expanded to $8B in March 2024 alongside an additional $1.2B RSF climate resilience tranche, anchoring external policy credibility (IMF, Jul-26, High).
- Egypt has successfully met quantitative performance criteria on net international reserves and domestic primary balances, though progress on the state asset divestment pipeline remains gradual (IMF, Aug-26, High).
- The Eighth and final review under the current EFF arrangement is scheduled for late Q4 2026 (IMF, Sep-26, Medium).
- Bilateral support from Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) partners and the European Union ($8B macro-financial assistance package) provides durable multilateral backstops against systemic external liquidity shocks (European Commission / GCC, Jul-26, High).

### Sovereign credit ratings and market access
- S&P Global Ratings upgraded Egypt's long-term sovereign credit rating to B from B- with a Stable outlook on 10-Oct-25, and affirmed the rating in April 2026, citing foreign exchange liquidity restoration and fiscal discipline (S&P Global Ratings, Oct-25 / Apr-26, High).
- Fitch Ratings upgraded Egypt's Long-Term Foreign-Currency Issuer Default Rating (IDR) to B from B- with a Stable outlook on 01-Nov-24, and reaffirmed the B rating with Stable outlook in October 2026 (Fitch Ratings, Nov-24 / Oct-26, High).
- Moody's Ratings maintains Egypt's foreign-currency long-term rating at Caa1 with a Positive outlook, having shifted the outlook to Positive in March 2024 and affirmed in April 2026, signalling potential upward convergence toward the B-tier over the coming rating cycle (Moody's Ratings, Apr-26, High).
- Benchmark USD Eurobonds trade across the curve with active liquid issues including EGYPT 7.600% 2029, EGYPT 9.450% 2033, EGYPT 6.875% 2040, and EGYPT 8.500% 2047 (LuxSE / Cbonds, Oct-26, High).
