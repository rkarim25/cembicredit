## View
Hold the BBB+/Baa2 credit. The conflict-driven Jebel Ali disruption has pushed leverage up in 2026, but liquidity and the 77% non-UAE footprint contain the damage.
- H1-26 Jebel Ali throughput fell 59.5% (Q2: 90.1%). Group adjusted EBITDA fell 5.6% to $2.86bn, and rose 9.7% excluding Jebel Ali.
- Net debt/EBITDA before IFRS 16 is 3.7x (3.4x a year ago) against the 4.0x policy ceiling, leaving about 0.3x of headroom.
- Liquidity is $8.2bn at H1-26. The EUR 750m 2026 notes were repaid on 25-Sep-26, and the September dual-tranche issue was priced 25bp inside initial price thoughts.
- Moody's affirmed Baa2/stable on 25-Jun-26 and forecasts 2026 EBITDA of about $5.9bn, down from $6.6bn in 2025. It expects recovery in 2027.
- The biggest risk is that Jebel Ali does not recover. Capex of about $3.0bn with falling operating cash flow would take leverage through 4.0x and risk a negative outlook.
- The repo financials are stale or templated and are not used for the 2025–26 view. No primary document was read in this run.
- Confidence: Medium. Figures come from press reports of issuer and agency releases (tier 3), and no Fitch or S&P 2026 action was found.

## Analysis
### Operating performance
- Jebel Ali H1-26 throughput fell 59.5% to 3.1m TEU, and Q2 fell 90.1% to 374k TEU (joshthompson.co.uk, H1-26 results coverage, undated, Medium).
- Adjusted EBITDA fell 5.6% from $3.03bn to $2.86bn. The margin fell to 22.5% from 27.0%, and profit fell 39.1% to $585m (joshthompson.co.uk; Khaleej Times H1-26 article, Medium).
- Excluding Jebel Ali, EBITDA grew 9.7% (Khaleej Times, Medium).
- Revenue was $12.7bn (+13.1%), but like-for-like growth was only 4.1% (Medium). The gap points to acquisitions or consolidation effects, which this run did not verify.

### Leverage and cash flow
- Net debt/EBITDA before IFRS 16 is 3.7x against 3.4x a year ago, under the 4.0x policy threshold. After IFRS 16 it is 4.3x against 4.0x (H1-26 coverage, Medium).
- H1 capex was $1.5bn (+36.4%), and the full-year guide is about $3.0bn. Operating cash generation was $2.0bn (-23.1%). Net finance costs were $760m, up from $631m (Medium).
- Moody's expects retained cash flow/debt of about 11% by 2027 and cash interest cover recovering to 3.5x (Khaleej Times, 25-Jun-26, Medium).

### Liquidity and funding
- H1-26 liquidity was $8.2bn: $5.5bn of cash and investments plus $2.7bn undrawn (Medium).
- Moody's cited $4.6bn of unrestricted cash at end-2025 and about $7.7bn of expected operating cash flow through 2027 (Khaleej Times, 25-Jun-26, Medium).
- The EUR 750m 2.375% notes were repaid on 25-Sep-26, with total payment of EUR 767.8m (RNS via Investegate, 25-Sep-26, High).
- On 11-Sep-26 the company priced a EUR 750m 6-year green note at 4.750% (mid-swaps +150bp) and a USD 750m 10-year note at 6.250% (T+150bp, 6.347% yield). Books were above EUR 1.5bn and USD 1.3bn, and pricing was 25bp inside initial price thoughts (Enterprise AM, 11-Sep-26, Medium).
- GlobalCapital reports a tender for two outstanding bonds. Details were not retrieved (Low).
- Near-term refinancing risk looks low. RCF expiry and covenant terms are unverified.

### Ratings
- Moody's affirmed Baa2/stable on 25-Jun-26 (Khaleej Times, Medium). The September issue was marketed at Baa2/BBB+, both stable (Enterprise AM, Medium).
- Fitch upgraded to BBB+ in 2023 on leverage below 4.0x (Medium). Whether Fitch reviewed the rating after the conflict began is unconfirmed. Hellenic Shipping News carries a Fitch piece on EMEA ports and Hormuz closure, but only the title was retrieved.
- No S&P rating for DPW was found.

### Ownership and sovereign links
- About 77% of the network is outside the UAE, generating over 60% of 2025 revenue and EBITDA (Moody's via Khaleej Times, 25-Jun-26, Medium).
- Dubai government ownership and support were not verified from a primary source in this run (Low). The conflict exposes the UAE-linked assets.

### Repo data
- The repo record shows 2024A EBITDA of $5,100m, no 2025 data, generic observation text and a flat 1.5% reconciliation variance in every year. This looks templated.
- Moody's 2025 EBITDA of $6.6bn does not tie to it. H1-26 revenue of $12.7bn also implies a much larger base than the stored 2024A revenue of $18.2bn.
- Bond data is stale: DPW 5.35% 2033 at 98 / 120bp as of 19-Sep-26.
- No Cognitive Credit model exists. A model must be built from the FY25 annual report and the H1-26 interim, neither of which was read.

### Data gaps
