## View
- Stable-to-improving credit, with low confidence on leverage and ratings because the H1-26 SENS tables could not be read.
- H1-26 operations are strong: service revenue R115.3bn (+17.5% constant currency), EBITDA before once-offs about R56bn (+24.4% constant currency), margin 47.1%.
- Nigeria is the engine and the main risk: 35.5% of Group EBITDA, margin 55.9%. IHS Towers would add further Nigeria exposure (about two-thirds of IHS revenue and EBITDA).
- Capital allocation is the change to watch: IHS Towers purchase (EV about $6.2bn, about $2.2bn cash consideration), a R6bn (about $375m) buyback, and a 40-60% equity free cash flow return framework. Capex excluding leases was R19.7bn in H1 (16.6% of revenue).
- Moody's called IHS modestly credit negative: pro forma adjusted leverage 2.9x against 2.4x on a 2024 basis. The "0.3x" company figure uses a different, unconfirmed perimeter and must not be used as a consolidated metric.
- The sovereign ceiling is improving: Moody's South Africa outlook positive (23-May-26) and S&P BB (30-May-26).
- Biggest risk: naira convertibility and devaluation combined with cash returns to shareholders and IHS funding.
- Confidence: Medium on direction, Low on leverage level, ratings and bond pricing.

## Analysis
### Operating performance (H1-26)
- Service revenue was R115.3bn, up 17.5% in constant currency. EBITDA before once-offs was about R56bn, up 24.4% in constant currency, at a 47.1% margin (MTN H1-26 release, 24-Aug-26, Medium: company release, figures taken via secondary summary).
- Nigeria EBITDA was R19,869m (35.5% of Group) at a 55.9% margin. Blended inflation was 9.3% against 14.0% (Nairametrics, 24-Aug-26, Medium).
- South Africa is soft: service revenue +1.5% in H1 and +2.3% in Q2 (H1 release, Medium). Most main-market currencies were stable against USD but weaker against the rand (H1 release, Medium).

### Leverage and liquidity
- The search summary of the H1-26 SENS cites net debt/EBITDA of 0.3x. The perimeter is unconfirmed and the PDF could not be read (Low). Moody's adjusted leverage was 2.4x before the IHS deal and 2.9x pro forma, on a 2024 basis (Moody's, Feb-26, via secondary summary, Medium).
- Net debt in rand and USD, leases, cash by country, the debt maturity ladder and upstreamed cash are not sourced. These are data gaps, not estimates.
- `database/issuers/mtn.json` is not used as evidence. Its multi-year figures show a uniform 1.5% EBITDA reconciliation variance and generic commentary, which indicates templating rather than filings. Its rating (Ba2 / BB+) and bond data (price 99.4, YTM 6.9%, 285bp, 19-Sep-26) are unverified.

### IHS Towers acquisition
- The announcement of 17-Feb-26 sets a price of $8.50 a share in cash, EV about $6.2bn, and about $2.2bn cash consideration for the 75.3% not already owned. Funding is the rolled 24% stake, about $1.1bn MTN cash and about $1.1bn IHS cash, with IHS debt rolling over (IHS/MTN announcement via Barchart, 17-Feb-26, Medium).
- Regulatory approvals are conditionally received. Close is expected in H2-26, with a 30% sell-down of IHS Nigeria to local investors (H1 release, Medium).
- The deal is financed largely from existing resources. IHS debt is not new Group debt at announcement but is consolidated at close.

### Shareholder returns
- R6bn buyback of about 31m shares, with 40-60% of equity free cash flow to be returned. No interim dividend was declared (H1 release and SENS search summary, Medium).
- MTN Nigeria declared an interim dividend of N26 a share, which supports upstreaming (Nairametrics, 24-Aug-26, Medium).

### Ratings and sovereign
- Moody's moved South Africa's outlook to positive from stable and affirmed Ba2 on 23-May-26 (SAnews, High). S&P affirmed South Africa at BB on 30-May-26, after the Nov-25 upgrade (IOL Business Report, Medium).
- The MTN corporate ratings could not be confirmed: Moody's Ba2 stable affirmed May-25 per search results, S&P BB- positive undated, and Fitch not found. Treat as unresolved (Low).

### Event risk
- A Syria settlement of $43.9m was authorised (Nairametrics, 24-Aug-26, Medium).
- Iran and Syria sanctions and licensing remain a tail risk.

- Not accessed: Moody's and S&P 2026 reports, and any Bloomberg or FT content.
