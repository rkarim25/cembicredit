## Analysis
### Instrument and record integrity
- The notes in issue are $750m 7.50% senior notes due 16-Jul-31 (ticker DAPERE). They were issued at par on 02-Jul-26 by JPM, BofA and StanChart, with a make-whole at T+50bp to 16-Jul-28 (The Lekki Playbook, Jul-26; ThisDay, 03-Jul-26; Medium).
- The repo's "12.50% 2029", price 95.0, spread 815bp and B / B2 are unsourced stored fields dated 19-Sep-26. No source supports them (Low).
- The repo financials show zero revenue for 2021A–2023A and "observations" text that does not describe a pre-commercial refinery. I treat them as templated and exclude them from the view.

### Leverage and liquidity (issuer-reported)
- Total debt was $5.67bn at 30-Jun-26, all secured, versus $6.24bn at 31-Dec-25 (IPO prospectus via ThisDay, 10-Sep-26; Medium).
- Dec-25 borrowings were $2.26bn secured bank debt and $3.99bn from DIL (same source; Medium).
- The $3.99bn DIL balance is the largest unknown. Its ranking, interest, maturity and subordination decide whether the stated leverage reflects true creditor exposure.
- Net debt/EBITDA was 0.27x at 30-Jun-26 (same source; Medium). The EBITDA definition is unknown and may be annualised from H1.
- Cash, capex and FY25 audited statements were not confirmed.

### Earnings quality
- H1-26 revenue was about $13.9–14.4bn and PAT $1.82bn. The two sources differ by about $0.5bn on revenue, probably from FX conversion (ThisDay and Dabafinance, 09-Oct-26; Medium).
- FY25 was a loss of $476m (Dabafinance; Medium).
- Industry refining margins benefited from Middle East disruption (Reuters via Zawya, 26-Aug-26; Medium).
- The swing from a $476m loss to $1.8bn profit in a half-year points to a margin-driven result, which may reverse. Throughput of 650–700kbpd is stated in the position without a primary source (Low).

### Feedstock
- Imports are about 30–40% of intake. Much Nigerian state crude is tied to oil-backed loans and pre-export deals, and domestic crude carries freight and logistics cost (Reuters via Zawya, 26-Aug-26; Medium).
- NNPC reportedly raised cargoes to 10 in March 2026 with 60% paid in naira and 40% in dollars (search snippet only; Low). The naira/dollar mismatch is a margin and FX exposure.

### Capital allocation and governance
- The IPO is 4.1bn shares at ₦525, about ₦2.15trn ($1.63bn). It is open 14-Sep to 13-Oct-26, with listing expected in Nov-26. Aliko Dangote's stake falls to 84.34% from 87.27% (Dabafinance; ThisDay, 10-Sep-26; Medium).
- A July placement raised $2.5bn at about a $40bn valuation (Hydrocarbon Processing/BNN, Aug-26; Medium).
- The 1.4mbpd target by 2029 (AllAfrica, 27-Oct-25; Medium) and a $15–16bn Lamu, Kenya refinery are in progress. Groundbreaking is due later in Oct-26 and a new legal challenge is reported (Bloomberg, 02-Oct-26; headline only, paywalled).
- Whether the notes' covenants restrict funding of Lamu or related-party flows is unknown.
- DIL's governance record is weak. Fitch downgraded DIL (national scale) to B+(nga) in Aug-24 and later withdrew (TheCable, Businessday; Medium).

### Sovereign context
- Nigeria is B (Fitch, Apr-25), B3 (Moody's, May-25) and B (S&P), per Ecofin and The Citizen (Medium, dated). S&P tied its upgrade partly to the refinery.
- No central bank, finance ministry or IMF release was checked this run. Transfer risk and the naira exposure of earnings versus dollar debt are therefore not quantified.

### Data gaps
- No Cognitive Credit model exists. A model must be built from filings: the IPO prospectus (https://ipo.dangote.com/prospectus.pdf), the FY25 audited accounts and the notes' offering memorandum. The prospectus PDF was not read in full.
- No market levels or agency rating for the notes.
