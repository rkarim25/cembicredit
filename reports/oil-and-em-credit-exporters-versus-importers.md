## View
- Brent is about USD 112 on 30-Oct-26 (Trading Economics, Medium), up about 13% in October on a Hormuz tanker attack. The October WEO assumes USD 95.74 for 2026 and USD 87.39 for 2027, with a gradual Hormuz reopening from Mar-27 and normalisation by Oct-27 (Medium). BMI sees Brent above USD 100 into 2027 (Enterprise, 27-Oct-26, Medium).
- The exporter versus importer split is only a first cut. Export route (Hormuz bypass or not), reserves and IMF anchoring separate credits better.
- Lean: long non-Gulf and Hormuz-bypass exporters (Nigeria, Angola, Gabon, Kazakhstan, Oman, Saudi Arabia, UAE). Underweight importers with thin buffers and large current account deficits (Pakistan, Turkey, Zambia, Senegal, Asian importers). Bahrain and Iraq are a volume and security trade, not an oil-price trade.
- Egypt is the exception among importers. Moody's upgraded it to B3 from Caa1 with a positive outlook on 16-Oct-26 (net reserves USD 57bn in Sep-26, debt 81.8% of GDP at Jun-26) (Medium).
- Ratings have lagged the oil move. Outlook risk sits in Gulf exporters more than in importers.
- Biggest risk: escalation to USD 110–125 hurts importers' external balances and Hormuz-dependent Gulf volumes together. A reopening favours importers, Bahrain and Iraq.
- Confidence: Medium on direction, Low on relative value, since no spread, CDS or post-July Gulf rating data was available.

## What changed
- Brent moved to about USD 112 (30-Oct-26) from USD 111 (22-Oct-26). The December contract is reported at USD 116.08.
- Moody's upgraded Egypt to B3 on 16-Oct-26 (positive outlook). This weakens the blanket "underweight importers" stance, and Egypt is now treated as an IMF-anchored exception.
- IMF October WEO path added: Hormuz reopening from Mar-27, normalisation by Oct-27, EM growth 3.7% and inflation 5.7% in 2026 (summaries).
- BMI (27-Oct-26) sees Brent above USD 100 into 2027.
- Pakistan current account added: FY26 deficit of about USD 139m against a USD 1.84bn surplus in FY25 (search summary of State Bank of Pakistan data, Low to Medium).
- Open items grew from seven to nine: reserves and rating-decision items added. The Hormuz item is updated with the MUFG reading.

## Analysis
### Oil regime
- Brent 112.44 on 30-Oct-26 (Trading Economics, Medium). https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil
- December contract USD 116.08 and January USD 112.19 on 30-Oct-26 (Financefeeds, Medium). https://financefeeds.com/brent-crude-oil-price-116-friday-hormuz-mine-strikes-december-expiry-fourth-monthly-gain-bull-120-bear-108/
- IMF October WEO: 2026 oil average USD 95.74 (USD 67.74 in 2025), 2027 USD 87.39. This is a simple average of Brent, Dubai and WTI, so it is not directly comparable with spot Brent (Bernama, Hans India, The Star summaries, 13-Oct-26, Medium). https://www.thehansindia.com/business/imf-sees-oil-at-9574-in-2026-warns-energy-importers-face-toughest-squeeze-1131985
- April WEO scenarios: reference USD 82, adverse USD 100 and USD 75, severe USD 110 and USD 125 (IMF, Apr-26, High).
- Fitch: 2026 base case USD 87 on an assumed reopening, falling to USD 60 by 2028. It sees USD 100 for a three-month closure and USD 120 for six months (Newsquawk, intellinews, 2026, dates unconfirmed, Low to Medium). A 10% oil rise worsens EM current accounts by 40–60bp (search summary, Low).
- Hormuz: Kpler flows at pre-war levels on 27–29 Sep (OilPrice, 05-Oct-26); record tanker attacks and a two-month low in crossings on 08-Oct-26 (CNN); about 80% of pre-conflict flows on 19-Oct-26 (MUFG). Sources conflict, so flows are volatile (Low to Medium).

### Gulf exporters
- Moody's moved the MENA outlook to negative on 31-Jul-26. Bahrain and Iraq have been negative since Apr-26; Qatar and Kuwait are stable (Enterprise, 31-Jul-26, Medium).
- Fitch has Qatar (AA) and Ras Al Khaimah (A+) on Rating Watch Negative (late Mar and early Apr-26). Oman is BBB-/Stable. Bahrain routes 87–95% of hydrocarbon exports via Hormuz. Kuwait and Qatar foreign assets are about 606% and 223% of GDP (Hellenic Shipping News summary of Fitch, 2026, Medium).
- Fitch's 2026 global sovereign outlook was revised to "deteriorating" (Enterprise, 11-Jun-26, Medium). Gulf ratings were broadly maintained as of 23-May-26 (The National, Medium). No agency actions after 31-Jul-26 were verified.
- UAE: reserves incl. gold USD 259.7bn (Jul-26) against USD 302.4bn (Feb-26), a fall that is unexplained. 2026E current account +11.4% of GDP (IMF, High).

### Non-Gulf exporters
- Nigeria: S&P upgraded to B from B- (stable) on 15-May-26 with a USD 100 Brent assumption (AllAfrica, 19-May-26, Medium).
- Angola: 2026 budget assumed USD 61 Brent (GlobalCapital, Medium). Spot is far above it. No newer rating action was found for either.
- Most of the windfall is probably priced. Upside is ratings momentum and issuance quality, untested without spreads (Low).

### Importers
- Moody's upgraded Egypt to B3 from Caa1, positive outlook, on 16-Oct-26: public debt 81.8% of GDP at Jun-26 (95.2% in 2023), net reserves USD 57bn in Sep-26 (about USD 52bn at end-2025) (Dabafinance, The National 17-Oct-26, Medium). https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/economy/2026/10/17/egypt-boosted-by-moodys-upgrade-as-inflation-eases-and-investor-confidence-grows/ IMF programme of about USD 8bn, final review targeted Dec-26 (MUFG, 19-Oct-26, Medium). Suez revenue USD 567.1m in Aug-26, +56.7% y/y (Capmad, Medium).
- S&P (28-Apr-26) named Egypt, Turkey and Asian net energy importers as most exposed. Fitch's net outlook balance moved from +3 to -1 (fi-desk, Medium). Ratings have not yet followed the macro exposure, as the Egypt upgrade shows.
- Turkey: annualised current account deficit USD 38.9bn at Jun-26 (ING, Medium). The USD 65bn energy import bill has no identified source (Low).
- Pakistan: FY26 current account deficit about USD 139m against a USD 1.84bn surplus in FY25; Jun-26 deficit about USD 649m (search summary of State Bank of Pakistan data, Low to Medium).
- The importer drag runs through the external balance, inflation and policy rates. Local-currency importers may carry more risk than hard-currency spreads show (inference, Low).
