## View
- No dated oil price, curve, spread or fiscal-breakeven data was available this run, and no web research was completed. This document is a framework and states no price levels.
- Oil reaches EM credit through four channels: fiscal revenue, the external balance, local-currency and inflation pass-through, and quasi-sovereign cash flow.
- The exporter/importer label is a weak guide. Buffers (reserves, sovereign wealth fund assets, fiscal breakeven against spot) explain more of the dispersion within each group.
- On a falling oil price, the weak-buffer exporters are the most exposed: Bahrain, Angola, Nigeria, Gabon, Iraq, with Oman between. Gulf states with large sovereign wealth funds are the least exposed. Confidence is Low to Medium.
- On a rising oil price, importers with subsidy burdens or thin reserves are the most exposed: Egypt, Pakistan, Kenya, Jordan, Turkey. IMF programme status matters as much as the oil price. Confidence is Low.
- Quasi-sovereign and corporate exporters likely move more than their sovereigns, and more so where hedging is absent. Confidence is Low.
- Biggest risk: treating exporters, or importers, as a single block.
- Overall confidence is Low until items 1 to 3 are answered. A relative-value call is not supportable without current spreads.

## Analysis
### Transmission channels
- Fiscal: an exporter's credit quality on a falling oil price depends on its fiscal breakeven relative to spot and on its buffers, more than on oil's share of exports (knowledge base framework, tier 2, Medium). No 2026 breakeven figure was retrieved, so none is stated.
- External: importers gain through the current account and inflation. The gain is largest where fuel prices pass through to consumers or subsidies have been reformed (Low, inferred).
- Quasi-sovereigns and corporates: cash flow is more oil-sensitive than the sovereign's. Hedge books and fixed-price offtake are the primary evidence and have not been checked (Low).

### Exporter dispersion
- Higher sensitivity: Bahrain, Angola, Nigeria, Gabon, Iraq.
- Lower sensitivity: Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait.
- Oman: between the two groups.
- Source: knowledge base universe pages (`knowledge/sovereigns/_universe.md`, tier 2). The ranking is unverified for 2026 (Low to Medium).

### Importer dispersion
- Highest sensitivity: Egypt, Pakistan, Kenya, Jordan, Turkey.
- Their external financing depends on IMF programme status and reserve cover as well as oil. The macro-monitor terms-of-trade and reserve fields (`knowledge/themes/macro-monitor.md`) were not read for current values (Low).

### Relative value
- Exporter spreads against importer spreads is attractive only if the market has priced oil beyond what breakevens and buffers justify. Current spread data is needed to test that (open items 2 and 3).

### Research coverage and gaps

- Missing: Brent and WTI levels and curve, OPEC+ decisions, IMF fiscal breakevens and WEO oil assumptions, rating actions in the last 90 days, country spreads, national oil company hedge books, and macro-monitor values.
