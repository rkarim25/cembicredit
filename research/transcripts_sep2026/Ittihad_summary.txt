Regional Disruption and Logistics

- The closure of the Strait of Hormuz starting in late February caused significant disruptions to oil and material exports.
- Ittihad established alternative routes through Fujairah, Jebel Ali, and Diba ports to ensure operational continuity.
- The company implemented a "green connection" between ports, allowing for clearance in Abu Dhabi while materials transit through Jeddah.
- While these measures secured local market share, they resulted in some lost export volume due to uncompetitive pricing for containerised goods.

Geographic Sales Redirection

- Consumer goods exports to North America decreased from 20% to 9% of total volumes.
- Sales were successfully redirected into the MENA region, increasing the UAE market share from 17% to 28%.
- The company maintains a 50% market share within the wider MENA region.
- Egypt remains a key African market due to efficient barge transport solutions across the Red Sea from Saudi Arabia.

Financial Performance and Margins

- Despite regional challenges, H1 revenue reached 1.96 billion with EBITDA growing by 37.4% to 113.9 million.
- Growth was driven by higher margins as competitors faced increased logistics and insurance costs for containerised products.
- Demand for building materials, tissue, and copper remained healthy despite the geopolitical situation.
- The company's strategy of geographical diversification has prevented dependence on any single market.

Raw Material Procurement Advantages

- Ittihad holds a competitive advantage by importing most raw materials in bulk rather than in containers.
- Bulk shipments for pulp, copper, and slag are less affected by the congestion currently hitting containerised hubs like Jebel Ali.
- Using bulk shipments avoids the high costs associated with empty container returns and transshipment delays.
- The company can negotiate more effectively for smaller barge shipments compared to large-scale containerised shipping lines.

Building Materials and Infrastructure Demand

- Building materials performance is bolstered by a backlog of projects scheduled for delivery through 2026–2028.
- Increased government investment in infrastructure, including water desalination, roads, and railways, is driving demand for cement and steel.

- High utilisation rates and improving margins in the building materials segment support overall group resilience.
